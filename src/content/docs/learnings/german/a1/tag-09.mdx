Yesterday the accusative changed the article in front of a noun. Today it changes the **pronoun that replaces the noun entirely** - and this is where German pays you back for yesterday's work, because the pronouns are short, they come up constantly, and three of the eight do not change at all.

The sentence you will get the most use out of today is `Ich verstehe Sie nicht`. You have been saying `Ich verstehe nicht` since Tag 02. Adding the one word `Sie` turns a vague statement into a specific one, and in a real conversation that difference decides whether the other person slows down or switches to English.

## 2. Aussprache - das verschluckte e

Nine days of learning how letters sound. Today: the letters Germans **do not** say.

Written German is full of unstressed `-e` and `-en` endings. Spoken German swallows most of them. This is not sloppiness or dialect - it is how the standard language is pronounced, including on the news, and it is the single biggest reason a sentence you can read perfectly sounds like noise when someone says it.

| Geschrieben | Gesprochen | Was passiert |
|---|---|---|
| hab**en** | hab'n | the e disappears, the n attaches to the b |
| mach**en** | mach'n | same |
| komm**en** | komm'n | same |
| geh**en** | geh'n | same |
| ein**en** | ein'n | the whole ending collapses |
| hab**e** ich | hab ich | the e vanishes completely |
| ein**e** Frag**e** | ein' Frag' | both reduced, but never deleted in careful speech |

The rule of thumb: **an unstressed e between consonants gets reduced to almost nothing, and -en becomes a syllabic n.** You should still write every letter. You should just stop expecting to hear them.

<Aside type="caution" title="Why this matters more than it looks">
This is a listening problem disguised as a pronunciation problem. When a German says `Hab'n Sie 'n Moment?` you are hearing four syllables where the page has seven, and if you are waiting for the seven you will miss the whole question. Learning to produce the reduced form is how you train your ear to accept it - which is why this is a speaking exercise even though the payoff is comprehension.

What you should **not** do is reduce in the other direction, dropping endings you have not learned yet. Say the full form when you are unsure. A slightly over-careful German is understood everywhere; a guessed ending is not.
</Aside>

Say each pair aloud, first the careful form, then the real one:

| Sorgfältig | Normal |
|---|---|
| Ich habe einen Moment. | Ich hab' ein'n Moment. |
| Was machen Sie? | Was mach'n Sie? |
| Woher kommen Sie? | Woher komm'n Sie? |
| Haben Sie Zeit? | Hab'n Sie Zeit? |
| Wir gehen jetzt. | Wir geh'n jetzt. |

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Die Pronomen im Akkusativ

A pronoun replaces a noun, so it has to carry the same case the noun would have. Here is the full set beside the nominative you already know from Tag 05:

| Nominativ | Akkusativ | English |
|---|---|---|
| ich | **mich** | me |
| du | **dich** | you (informal) |
| er | **ihn** | him / it (masculine) |
| sie | **sie** | her / it (feminine) |
| es | **es** | it (neuter) |
| wir | **uns** | us |
| ihr | **euch** | you (plural informal) |
| sie / Sie | **sie / Sie** | them / you (formal) |

Three of them do not move at all: `sie`, `es` and `Sie`. Two change in a way you can almost guess: `ich → mich`, `du → dich`. The one genuinely new form is **`er → ihn`**, and it is the one to drill hardest, because it carries the same `-n` logic as yesterday's `den`.

<Aside type="tip" title="The pattern that ties both days together">
Masculine is the gender that marks the accusative, in every word class:

| | Nominativ | Akkusativ |
|---|---|---|
| bestimmter Artikel | de**r** | de**n** |
| unbestimmter Artikel | ei**n** | eine**n** |
| Pronomen | e**r** | ih**n** |

Three rows, one story. If you can see that `der → den`, `ein → einen` and `er → ihn` are the same change, today is mostly done.
</Aside>

### Das Pronomen ersetzt das Nomen

Watch what happens when a noun is replaced:

| Mit Nomen | Mit Pronomen |
|---|---|
| Ich nehme <span class="de-der">den Zug</span>. | Ich nehme **ihn**. |
| Ich brauche <span class="de-die">die Adresse</span>. | Ich brauche **sie**. |
| Ich kaufe <span class="de-das">das Buch</span>. | Ich kaufe **es**. |
| Ich verstehe <span class="de-die">die Frau</span> nicht. | Ich verstehe **sie** nicht. |

Notice that `der Zug` becomes `ihn`, not `es`. German pronouns follow the **grammatical gender** of the noun, not whether the thing is alive. A train is a *he*. A street is a *she*. This is the thing that will feel most foreign today and it is non-negotiable - it is why the colour list in your notebook matters.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

Read each one aloud twice before the drill.

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Ich verstehe Sie nicht | I do not understand you | the most useful sentence today |
| Verstehen Sie mich? | do you understand me | the check you should run constantly |
| Können Sie mich verstehen? | can you understand me | softer version of the same |
| Ich kenne ihn | I know him | kennen, for people and places |
| Ich kenne sie nicht | I do not know her | same sie for her and them |
| Entschuldigen Sie mich | excuse me (leaving) | different from Entschuldigung |
| Wie finden Sie es? | how do you like it | literally: how do you find it |
| Ich finde es gut | I like it | the all-purpose opinion |
| Ich finde es zu teuer | I find it too expensive | builds on Tag 04 |
| Rufen Sie mich an | call me | anrufen, split in two |
| Ich hole Sie ab | I will pick you up | abholen, same split |
| Darf ich Sie etwas fragen? | may I ask you something | the polite opener |

<Aside type="caution" title="Two verbs that break in half">
`Rufen Sie mich **an**` and `Ich hole Sie **ab**` look wrong, and they are not. German has a large family of verbs whose first syllable detaches and goes to the end of the sentence: `anrufen` to call, `abholen` to pick up. Today just learn these two as fixed sentences. The rule behind them gets its own day on Tag 14, and once you have it, a whole category of German verbs opens up at once.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

Two people who have met before. Count the pronouns - there are nine, and every one is in the accusative.

> **Anna:** Guten Tag, Ravi! Kennen Sie Herrn Weber?
> **Ravi:** Nein, ich kenne ihn nicht. Darf ich Sie etwas fragen?
> **Anna:** Ja, genau.
> **Ravi:** Ich verstehe Sie manchmal nicht. Sprechen Sie bitte langsam.
> **Anna:** Natürlich. Verstehen Sie mich jetzt?
> **Ravi:** Ja, jetzt verstehe ich Sie gut. Danke!
> **Anna:** Wie finden Sie Hamburg?
> **Ravi:** Ich finde es sehr gut. Aber ich kenne die Stadt nicht.
> **Anna:** Ich hole Sie am Samstag ab. Rufen Sie mich an.
> **Ravi:** Gern. Ich rufe Sie heute Abend an.

Two small new words slipped in - `manchmal` sometimes, `natürlich` of course, `gern` gladly, `heute Abend` this evening. Take them as free vocabulary; nothing in today's drills depends on them.

Read it aloud twice, both parts, **with the swallowed endings from section 2**: `Kenn'n Sie`, `Versteh'n Sie mich jetzt`, `Ich ruf' Sie heut' Abend an`. Then cover Ravi's lines and produce them from Anna's cues.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Today the AI plays someone you have met once before, so pronouns are unavoidable. Stand up.

```text
Du bist Anna, eine Bekannte aus Hamburg. Wir haben uns einmal getroffen.
Ich bin Anfänger (Tag 9, A1). Heute übe ich die Akkusativpronomen:
mich, Sie, ihn, sie, es, uns.

Sprich langsam, maximal sieben Wörter pro Satz. Stelle mir oft Fragen wie
"Kennen Sie ...?", "Verstehen Sie mich?", "Wie finden Sie ...?", damit ich
mit einem Pronomen antworten muss. Sprich über: Hamburg, den Bahnhof,
den Kaffee, das Buch, die Stadt, Herrn Weber, Frau Schmidt.

Wenn ich das falsche Pronomen benutze - zum Beispiel "es" für "der Zug" -
wiederhole meinen Satz einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung, und mach weiter.
Korrigiere nichts anderes. Fang jetzt an und begrüße mich.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
Whether you say `ihn` at all. Under time pressure, learners reach for `es` for everything, because English *it* covers every object. Every time Anna mentions a masculine noun - der Zug, der Kaffee, der Bahnhof, der Mann - the answer needs `ihn`, and you will almost certainly say `es` the first three times. Notice it, say the sentence again, carry on.
</Aside>

Same strict shape as before:
