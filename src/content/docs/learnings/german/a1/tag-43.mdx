Week seven. The grammar is finished and this week is about getting things done in writing and on the phone, starting with the hardest administrative thing anyone does in Germany: **finding somewhere to live.**

The language problem here is specific. A German flat advert is written in abbreviations, and the words that decide whether you can afford the flat - `Kaltmiete`, `Nebenkosten`, `Kaution` - are not the words an English advert would use. People sign contracts they have misread. Today is the vocabulary that stops that.

Tag 22 gave you the rooms of a flat. Today is the flat as a transaction.

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Die Miete - drei Wörter, die man nicht verwechseln darf

This is the single most important distinction on the page:

| Deutsch | English | Was es bedeutet |
|---|---|---|
| die Kaltmiete | base rent | the rent alone, nothing included |
| die Nebenkosten | service charges | water, rubbish, stairwell, heating, caretaker |
| die Warmmiete | total rent | Kaltmiete plus Nebenkosten - what you actually pay |

An advert almost always shows the **Kaltmiete** in large type, because it is the smaller number. The figure that leaves your account each month is the **Warmmiete**, and the gap is routinely 150 to 300 euros.

The abbreviations you will see: **KM** for Kaltmiete, **NK** for Nebenkosten, **WM** for Warmmiete.

Electricity and internet are usually **not** in the Nebenkosten. You contract those yourself, separately.

### Die Abkürzungen

A German advert is half abbreviation. These eight cover most of them:

| Kurz | Lang | English |
|---|---|---|
| 2 ZKB | 2 Zimmer, Küche, Bad | 2 rooms, kitchen, bathroom |
| qm / m2 | Quadratmeter | square metres |
| KM / NK / WM | Kaltmiete / Nebenkosten / Warmmiete | base / service / total rent |
| EBK | Einbauküche | fitted kitchen |
| OG / EG / DG | Obergeschoss / Erdgeschoss / Dachgeschoss | upper floor / ground floor / attic |
| ZH | Zentralheizung | central heating |
| NR | Nichtraucher | non-smoker |
| provisionsfrei | ohne Maklerprovision | no agent's fee |

<Aside type="caution" title="Three things a German advert does not tell you">
**1. "2 Zimmer" does not include the kitchen or the bathroom.** A `2-Zimmer-Wohnung` has a living room and a bedroom, plus a kitchen and a bathroom that are not counted. So the German count is roughly the English count minus one - a `3 ZKB` is what an English advert would call a two-bedroom flat.

**2. The kitchen may not exist.** If the advert does not say `EBK`, there may be no kitchen at all - no units, no sink cupboard, sometimes no appliances. Germans move their kitchens with them. The same goes for light fittings: bare wires in the ceiling on handover day is normal, not a fault.

**3. The deposit is up to three months' Kaltmiete.** `Die Kaution` is legally capped at three months of the **base** rent, and you pay it before you move in. On a 900 euro Kaltmiete that is 2,700 euros in cash, on top of the first month's rent. It comes back when you leave, minus whatever the landlord deducts, and it can take months.

So the real question to ask at a viewing is not `Was kostet die Wohnung?` but **`Wie hoch sind die Nebenkosten und die Kaution?`**
</Aside>

### Was der Vermieter sehen will

German landlords ask for a document pack, and it is the same pack everywhere:

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| die Schufa-Auskunft | credit report |
| die Gehaltsabrechnungen | payslips, usually the last three |
| der Arbeitsvertrag | employment contract |
| die Mietschuldenfreiheitsbescheinigung | confirmation of no rent arrears |
| die Kopie vom Ausweis | copy of the ID |
| die Selbstauskunft | the tenant self-disclosure form |

`die Mietschuldenfreiheitsbescheinigung` is thirty-four letters long and means *confirmation-of-freedom-from-rent-debts*. Read it right to left, the way you learned on Tag 40, and it stops being frightening: a `Bescheinigung`, about `Freiheit`, from `Mietschulden`.

Have the whole pack as one PDF **before** the first viewing. The flat goes to whoever hands it over first, and that is not an exaggeration.

## 6. Text und Hören

Not a dialogue today. **An advert**, written the way they are actually written, and reading one correctly is the skill.

> **Schöne 2-Zimmer-Wohnung in Hamburg-Eimsbüttel, 58 qm, 2. OG**
>
> Helle, ruhige Altbauwohnung mit Balkon in beliebter Lage. Die Wohnung liegt im zweiten Obergeschoss ohne Aufzug. Zwei Zimmer, Küche, Bad mit Fenster und Wanne. Holzdielen, hohe Decken, Zentralheizung. Keine EBK vorhanden.
>
> KM 940 EUR · NK 180 EUR · WM 1.120 EUR · Kaution 2.820 EUR (3 Monatsmieten kalt)
>
> Bezugsfrei ab 01.01. Unbefristeter Mietvertrag, Kündigungsfrist 3 Monate. Provisionsfrei - direkt von der Hausverwaltung.
>
> Bitte bringen Sie zur Besichtigung mit: Schufa-Auskunft, die letzten drei Gehaltsabrechnungen, Kopie vom Ausweis und die ausgefüllte Selbstauskunft. Ohne vollständige Unterlagen ist leider keine Berücksichtigung möglich.
>
> Besichtigung: Samstag, 14.00 Uhr. Keine Haustiere. Nichtraucherwohnung.

Now answer these out loud, in German, before reading on: *Wie viele Schlafzimmer hat die Wohnung? Was kostet sie wirklich pro Monat? Wie viel Geld braucht man am Anfang? Was fehlt in der Küche? Was muss man mitbringen?*

The answers, because three of them are traps:

- **One bedroom.** Two Zimmer means a living room and a bedroom.
- **1,120 euros a month** - the Warmmiete, not the 940 in bold.
- **3,940 euros up front** - the 2,820 deposit plus the first month's 1,120. Plus a kitchen.
- **The kitchen does not exist.** `Keine EBK vorhanden.`
- **Four documents**, and `ohne vollständige Unterlagen ... keine Berücksichtigung` means they will not even look at you without them.

New words: `hell` bright, `ruhig` quiet, `beliebt` popular, `die Lage` the location, `der Aufzug` the lift, `die Wanne` the bathtub, `die Holzdielen` floorboards, `die Decke` the ceiling, `bezugsfrei` available to move into, `unbefristet` open-ended, `vollständig` complete, `die Berücksichtigung` consideration, `das Haustier` the pet.

Read the whole advert aloud twice. Reading German adverts out loud is unglamorous and it is the thing that makes the next one take two minutes instead of twenty.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

A viewing, with twelve other people in the room. Stand up.

```text
Du bist Mitarbeiter einer Hamburger Hausverwaltung. Ich bin Lernender
(Tag 43) und komme zur Wohnungsbesichtigung. Es sind noch zwölf andere
Leute da, also hast du wenig Zeit und bist freundlich aber knapp.

Beschreibe mir die Wohnung in drei Sätzen. Dann lass MICH fragen -
antworte kurz und warte auf die nächste Frage.

Ich muss nach Nebenkosten, Kaution, Küche, Datum und Kündigungsfrist
fragen. Wenn ich eine davon vergesse, frag am Ende: "Haben Sie keine
Fragen zu den Kosten?" - und lass mich nachholen.

Am Ende sagst du "Wir melden uns." Dann muss ich noch zwei Dinge sagen:
dass ich alle Unterlagen dabei habe, und wann ich Bescheid bekomme.

Maximal zehn Wörter pro Satz. Fang an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="The two sentences that actually get you the flat">
At a mass viewing you have about forty seconds of the agent's attention, and almost everybody uses them to say something about themselves. Use them for these two instead:

**`Ich habe alle Unterlagen dabei.`** - said while holding the folder, not while describing it.

**`Ich würde den Vertrag gern unterschreiben.`** - which answers the only question the landlord actually has.

Everything else - that you are quiet, employed, non-smoking, in Hamburg for an MBA - belongs in the Selbstauskunft form, where it will be read. At the viewing, what distinguishes you is that your paperwork is complete and in your hand.

Note that `Ich würde ... gern` is a `würde` form, which is week eight's grammar on Tag 52. For now, learn it as a block. It is the politest way German has of saying what you want, and you will need it before Tag 52 arrives.
</Aside>
