The most consequential page in the course.

A German job application is not a translated English one. The structure is different, the conventions are stricter, the tone is flatter, and three of the things a German employer expects - a tabular CV, a reference from every previous employer, and a salary figure stated as **gross per year** - have no direct English equivalent. Applications get discarded over the format alone.

So today is partly vocabulary and partly convention. The vocabulary is in section 3; the conventions are in the boxes, and they are the part that will change what you actually send.

<Aside type="note" title="How to run this page">
Two hours. The paper block is again the real work and needs thirty minutes. Section 7 reverses the eight telephone sentences from Tag 44, and section 9 has the second distillation of Goldlist 6.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Die Bewerbungsunterlagen

A German application is a **pack**, and it has a fixed order:

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| die Bewerbung | the application |
| das Anschreiben | the cover letter |
| der Lebenslauf | the CV |
| die Zeugnisse | certificates and references |
| das Arbeitszeugnis | the employer's reference |
| das Abschlusszeugnis | the degree certificate |
| die Anlagen | the enclosures |
| das Bewerbungsfoto | the application photo |

Send the whole thing as **one PDF**, in that order, named with your own name. Four separate attachments is a small, avoidable irritation.

### Die Wörter der Stellenanzeige

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| die Stellenanzeige | the job advert |
| die Stelle | the position |
| sich bewerben um | to apply for |
| der Arbeitgeber | the employer |
| der Arbeitnehmer | the employee |
| die Berufserfahrung | professional experience |
| die Kenntnisse | knowledge, skills |
| der Abschluss | the degree |
| die Vollzeit / die Teilzeit | full time / part time |
| unbefristet / befristet | permanent / fixed-term |
| das Gehalt | the salary |
| die Gehaltsvorstellung | salary expectation |
| der Eintrittstermin | start date |

`sich bewerben` is reflexive, as on Tag 34, and it takes `um`: **`Ich bewerbe mich um die Stelle als Projektmanager.`** Not `für`.

<Aside type="caution" title="The Lebenslauf is a table, not a story">
A German CV is **tabellarisch** - a table. No paragraphs, no summary, no personal statement, no achievements written as prose. Two pages at the absolute most, and one is better.

The structure, in this order:

1. **Persönliche Daten** - name, address, email. Date of birth is traditional and optional.
2. **Berufserfahrung** - most recent first, with month and year on the left: `09/2024 - 08/2026`.
3. **Ausbildung** - education, same format.
4. **Kenntnisse** - languages with levels, software, certificates.
5. **Ort, Datum und Unterschrift** - place, date and a signature at the bottom.

Three things that trip people up:

- **Reverse chronological is now standard.** Older German CVs ran oldest-first; current practice is newest-first, as in English.
- **Gaps get named, not hidden.** A German reader will notice an unexplained six months and read it as evasion. One line - `Sprachkurs`, `Elternzeit`, `berufliche Neuorientierung` - closes it.
- **Signing and dating the CV is still common** in Germany and costs nothing. `Hamburg, 8. Oktober 2026` and your signature at the foot.

Your email address and the LinkedIn profile belong in the personal data block. A phone number is conventional on a German CV, and whether you put one there is entirely your decision - it is a document you hand to a specific employer, not something published.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip" title="Brutto im Jahr - and the photo question">
**The salary figure is annual and gross.** When a German advert asks for your `Gehaltsvorstellung`, it wants one number: `55.000 Euro brutto im Jahr`. Not a monthly figure, not a range, not net. Germans discuss salaries in annual gross and will read a monthly figure as a misunderstanding.

Two words you need permanently: **`brutto`** before deductions, **`netto`** after. Between them sits roughly a third to a half of the number, which is worth knowing before you negotiate anything.

**The photo.** A photo is **not legally required** - the General Equal Treatment Act of 2006 means an employer cannot demand one, and a growing number of large companies explicitly ask you not to include one. But a photo remains customary in much of the German market, and many recruiters still expect to see one.

So it is a judgement call, not a rule, and the honest answer is: include a professional photo unless the advert says otherwise, and leave it out for international employers and anyone who says `ohne Foto`. If you include one, it is a proper headshot taken by a photographer, top right of the CV. A holiday snap is worse than nothing.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Zeugnissprache - the coded reference">
This one has no English equivalent at all, and it matters.

In Germany you are **entitled to a written reference** from every employer when you leave - `das Arbeitszeugnis` - and you attach them to applications. Because the law requires the reference to be benevolent in tone, employers grade you through a **fixed code** instead. The key phrase is how your performance satisfied them:

| Formulierung | Was es wirklich heißt |
|---|---|
| stets zu unserer vollsten Zufriedenheit | very good |
| stets zu unserer vollen Zufriedenheit | good |
| zu unserer vollen Zufriedenheit | satisfactory |
| zu unserer Zufriedenheit | mediocre |
| insgesamt zu unserer Zufriedenheit | poor |

The whole grading sits in `stets`, `vollsten` and `vollen`. Every German HR reader decodes that sentence automatically.

What to do with this: **read your own references** before you attach them, and if yours says `zu unserer Zufriedenheit` you are attaching a quiet complaint. You can ask for a correction, and plenty of people do.

If your previous jobs were outside Germany and you have no `Arbeitszeugnis`, say so in one line in the Anschreiben rather than leaving the gap - `Arbeitszeugnisse nach indischem Standard liegen nicht vor; Referenzen nenne ich gern auf Anfrage.`
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

A complete Anschreiben. Four paragraphs, one page, and every sentence in it is from section 3.

> **Betreff:** Bewerbung als Projektmanager - Kennziffer 2026-114
>
> Sehr geehrte Frau Schneider,
>
> mit großem Interesse habe ich Ihre Stellenanzeige für die Position als Projektmanager gelesen. Ich bewerbe mich um diese Stelle, weil Ihr Unternehmen international arbeitet und Projekte im Bereich Energie umsetzt - genau das Feld, in dem ich seit vier Jahren tätig bin.
>
> Zurzeit mache ich einen MBA am Northern Institute of Technology Management in Hamburg. Davor habe ich vier Jahre in der Schifffahrt gearbeitet. Dort habe ich Projekte geplant, Budgets verantwortet und in internationalen Teams gearbeitet.
>
> Meine Stärken sind technisches Verständnis, Organisation und Kommunikation. Ich bringe fließende Englischkenntnisse mit und lerne Deutsch - zurzeit auf A2-Niveau, mit dem Ziel B1 im nächsten Jahr. Ich kann ab dem ersten Januar anfangen. Meine Gehaltsvorstellung liegt bei [Betrag] Euro brutto im Jahr.
>
> Über ein persönliches Gespräch würde ich mich sehr freuen.
>
> Mit freundlichen Grüßen
> Ravi Chaudhary
>
> **Anlagen:** Lebenslauf, Abschlusszeugnisse, Arbeitszeugnisse

`die Kennziffer` is the reference number from the advert - quote it if there is one. `umsetzen` is to implement, `tätig sein` is to work in a field, `verantworten` is to be responsible for, `das Ziel` is the goal. `[Betrag]` stays a placeholder here on purpose - the figure is yours and belongs in the letter you actually send, not in a study page.

Read it aloud twice. Then look at what the third paragraph does with the German problem: **it names the level and the plan.** `Deutsch auf A2-Niveau, mit dem Ziel B1 im nächsten Jahr` is far stronger than either claiming more than you have or saying nothing. German employers hire people whose German is improving; they do not hire people whose German is unclear.

<Aside type="tip" title="The sentence an English application would not contain">
Look at paragraph one: `Ich bewerbe mich um diese Stelle, weil Ihr Unternehmen international arbeitet und Projekte im Bereich Energie umsetzt.`

That is a sentence about **them**, placed before anything about you, and it names something specific and checkable. German recruiters read the first paragraph to find out whether you have read the advert, and a generic opening is read as a mass mailing.

Equally, notice what is *not* in the letter: no enthusiasm about the opportunity, no description of yourself as passionate or dynamic, no thanks for considering the application. German application prose is flat on purpose. Facts, dates, numbers, one reason, and a request for a meeting.
</Aside>

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Say the letter rather than write it. Stand up.

```text
Du bist Personalerin bei einem Hamburger Unternehmen. Ich bin Lernender
(Tag 46).

ERSTER TEIL - fünf Minuten. Ich erzähle dir mein Anschreiben MÜNDLICH,
in vier Teilen: warum diese Firma, wer ich bin, was ich mitbringe, wann
ich anfangen kann und was ich verdienen möchte. Unterbrich mich nicht.

Dann sag mir nur eins: Welcher der vier Teile war der schwächste, und
warum?

ZWEITER TEIL - fünf Minuten. Jetzt stell mir die drei Fragen, die eine
deutsche Personalerin nach so einem Anschreiben stellen würde. Eine davon
muss unangenehm sein - zum Beispiel zur Lücke im Lebenslauf oder zum
Deutschniveau.

Maximal zehn Wörter pro Satz. Korrigiere nur Fehler, die die Bewerbung
schwächer machen.
```
