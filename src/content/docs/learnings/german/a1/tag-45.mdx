Three quarters of the way. Yesterday was the telephone; today is the thing you can do instead of telephoning, and do better.

Written German is the learner's advantage. You get to be slow, you get to check, and the sentences that matter are **formulaic** - a German business email is built from about fifteen fixed phrases, and once you own them you can write one in four minutes that reads as entirely competent. This is the only page in the course where you can perform well above your actual level, and it is worth exploiting.

### Die Teile einer E-Mail

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| der Betreff | the subject line |
| die Anrede | the salutation |
| die Grußformel | the closing formula |
| der Anhang | the attachment |
| anbei | enclosed, attached |
| die Unterschrift | the signature |
| der Absender | the sender |
| der Empfänger | the recipient |

### Die drei Register

German has three clearly separated levels, and using the wrong one is the most visible mistake in a written German message. Pick by **how you would address the person out loud**.

| Register | Anrede | Grußformel | Wann |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Formal** | Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren, | Mit freundlichen Grüßen | you do not know the name |
| **Formal** | Sehr geehrter Herr Müller, / Sehr geehrte Frau Schneider, | Mit freundlichen Grüßen | landlord, office, employer, professor |
| **Halbformell** | Guten Tag Frau Schneider, | Freundliche Grüße / Viele Grüße | ongoing business contact |
| **Informell** | Hallo Anna, / Liebe Anna, / Lieber Tom, | Viele Grüße / Liebe Grüße / Bis bald | friends, classmates |

If you are addressing the person as `Sie` in person, use `Sehr geehrte...` and `Mit freundlichen Grüßen` in writing. If you say `du` to them, use `Hallo` and `Viele Grüße`. That single test gets it right nearly every time.

<Aside type="caution" title="Three rules Germans notice instantly">
**1. A comma after the salutation, and the next line starts lower case.**

> Sehr geehrte Frau Schneider,
>
> **v**ielen Dank für Ihre E-Mail.

Not `Vielen`. The comma means the sentence has not begun yet, so the first word after it is lower case unless it is a noun or `Ich`. You met this on Tag 41 in the letter from the Behörde. It is the single most reliable marker of someone who has learned German writing properly.

**2. No comma after the closing.** `Mit freundlichen Grüßen` and then the name on the next line. Nothing after `Grüßen`.

**3. `Sie`, `Ihnen`, `Ihr` are always capitalised.** Always, everywhere, mid-sentence: `Vielen Dank für Ihre E-Mail`, `Ich schreibe Ihnen wegen ...`. And `sie` meaning *she* or *they* is lower case, so the capital letter is carrying real information. Getting this wrong makes a sentence briefly unreadable.

By contrast, `du`, `dir`, `dein` are lower case in modern spelling. Capitalising them in a personal letter is still permitted and slightly old-fashioned, so leave them small.
</Aside>

### Die Bausteine, aus denen jede E-Mail gebaut ist

These are the fifteen phrases. The six in **bold** will appear in almost everything you write.

| Deutsch | English | Wo |
|---|---|---|
| **Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,** | Dear Sir or Madam, | Anrede |
| **Sehr geehrter Herr Müller,** | Dear Mr Müller, | Anrede |
| **vielen Dank für Ihre E-Mail** | thank you for your email | opening |
| ich habe Ihre Anzeige gesehen | I have seen your advert | opening |
| **ich schreibe Ihnen wegen ...** | I am writing to you about ... | opening |
| ich interessiere mich für ... | I am interested in ... | opening |
| **im Anhang finden Sie ...** | please find attached ... | middle |
| anbei sende ich Ihnen ... | I am sending you herewith ... | middle |
| können Sie mir bitte sagen, wann ... | can you please tell me when ... | middle |
| ich hätte eine Frage | I would have a question | middle |
| **ich würde mich über eine Antwort freuen** | I would be glad of a reply | closing |
| vielen Dank im Voraus | thank you in advance | closing |
| für Rückfragen stehe ich Ihnen gern zur Verfügung | I am glad to answer any questions | closing |
| **Mit freundlichen Grüßen** | Yours sincerely | Grußformel |
| Viele Grüße | best wishes | Grußformel, lighter |

`ich hätte` and `ich würde` are forms you meet properly on Tag 52. For now they are blocks - and they are the two most useful blocks in written German, because both of them turn a demand into a request.

### Der Betreff

The subject line is not decorative in German correspondence. It is expected to name the matter, and often a reference and a date:

| Betreff | Wofür |
|---|---|
| Besichtigungstermin 2-Zimmer-Wohnung Eimsbüttel | the flat |
| Bewerbung als Projektmanager - Ravi Chaudhary | a job application |
| Terminanfrage - Anmeldung | an appointment at the Amt |
| Nachfrage zu meinem Antrag vom 3. Oktober | chasing something up |
| Kündigung meines Mietvertrags zum 31.12. | giving notice |

Note the pattern: **noun phrase, no verb, no greeting, no "Hello".** `Frage` or `Hallo` as a subject line reads as spam.

## 6. Text und Hören

Two texts today. The first is the email that follows Tag 43's advert - and it is a complete, sendable German email in eight lines.

> **Betreff:** Besichtigungstermin 2-Zimmer-Wohnung Eimsbüttel
>
> Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,
>
> ich habe Ihre Anzeige für die 2-Zimmer-Wohnung in Eimsbüttel gesehen und interessiere mich sehr für die Wohnung.
>
> Ich mache einen MBA an der TUHH und suche eine Wohnung ab Januar. Ich bin Nichtraucher und habe keine Haustiere.
>
> Können Sie mir bitte sagen, ob die Wohnung noch frei ist und wann die Besichtigung stattfindet?
>
> Im Anhang finden Sie meine vollständigen Unterlagen: Schufa-Auskunft, die letzten drei Gehaltsabrechnungen, eine Kopie meines Ausweises und die ausgefüllte Selbstauskunft.
>
> Über eine Antwort würde ich mich sehr freuen. Vielen Dank im Voraus.
>
> Mit freundlichen Grüßen
> Ravi Chaudhary

`stattfinden` is to take place, separable - `die Besichtigung findet am Samstag statt`.

Look at what that email does in four short paragraphs: names the matter, says who you are in two facts a landlord cares about, asks exactly two questions, and lists the attachments by name so nobody has to open them to check. Nothing is explained, nothing is apologised for, no feelings are mentioned. That is the German register.

And the second text - the layout of a **paper letter**, which you still need in Germany for notice periods, insurance and anything with a signature:

> Ravi Chaudhary
> Beispielstraße 12
> 20249 Hamburg
>
> Hausverwaltung Nordstadt
> Musterweg 3
> 20095 Hamburg
>
> Hamburg, den 8. Oktober 2026
>
> **Kündigung meines Mietvertrags zum 31.12.2026**
>
> Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,
>
> hiermit kündige ich meinen Mietvertrag für die Wohnung Beispielstraße 12 fristgerecht zum 31. Dezember 2026.
>
> Bitte bestätigen Sie mir den Erhalt dieser Kündigung schriftlich.
>
> Mit freundlichen Grüßen
>
>
> Ravi Chaudhary

Six things to notice: **your address top left**, then the recipient's, then **place and date on the right**, the **Betreff in bold with no label**, `hiermit` (*hereby*) which is the standard opening of any formal letter that does something, and a **blank line for the handwritten signature** above the typed name.

`fristgerecht` means within the notice period, `der Erhalt` is receipt, `schriftlich` is in writing. A German notice letter that does not ask for written confirmation is an unfinished letter.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

A dictation-and-correction session rather than a conversation, because today's skill is written.

```text
Du bist mein Deutschlehrer. Ich bin Lernender (Tag 45) und habe heute
E-Mails und Briefe gelernt.

ERSTER TEIL - fünf Minuten. Gib mir fünf Situationen, eine nach der
anderen. Ich sage dir laut die Anrede, EINEN Satz zur Sache, und die
Grußformel. Korrigiere sofort, besonders das Register.

Situationen zum Beispiel: eine Wohnung, eine Rechnung, ein Termin beim
Amt, eine Nachricht an einen Kommilitonen, eine Krankmeldung an den
Arbeitgeber.

ZWEITER TEIL - fünf Minuten. Jetzt diktiere ich dir eine ganze E-Mail,
laut, Satz für Satz: Betreff, Anrede, drei Sätze, Grußformel. Schreib
genau das, was ich sage - nicht, was ich meine.

Dann zeig mir die Fehler, aber nur die, die ein deutscher Leser merken
würde. Ignoriere alles andere.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="Dictating is harder than writing, which is the point">
Reading out an email forces you to produce the punctuation and the capitals from memory - the comma after the Anrede, the lower-case line after it, the capital `Ihnen`, no comma after `Grüßen`. Typing hides all of that behind your fingers.

And it has a practical use beyond the drill: when you write a German email that matters, **read it aloud before you send it.** The mistakes you cannot see on screen are audible, especially a sentence whose verb has drifted out of second position.
</Aside>

## 9. Auf Papier - Goldlist

<div class="de-paper">

Today the paper block is the lesson. Allow it thirty minutes rather than the usual fifteen.

**Diktat.** Ten-second loop on the shadowing audio. Six German words by hand, then correct in a second colour.

**Die E-Mail, von Hand.** Copy the email from section 6 out by hand, completely. Every comma, the lower-case `ich` after the Anrede, no comma after `Grüßen`. Handwriting it is the only way the punctuation sticks.

**Deine eigene E-Mail.** Now write **your own**, by hand, no looking at section 6, to a real advert you found yesterday. Betreff, Anrede, three sentences, closing, Grußformel, name. Seven lines. Then check it against the model and mark your own errors in a second colour.

This is the most directly useful thing you will produce in the whole sixty days. Keep it. The next time you need it, you will be editing rather than composing.

**Die Grußformel-Tabelle.** Write the three registers from section 3 by hand - Anrede and Grußformel side by side, formal, semi-formal, informal. Six short lines, and they close off a whole category of mistake.

**Fehlerliste.** Week-seven page. Every wrong item from today, including every punctuation error from the handwritten email.

**Genus-Farben.** Eight new nouns. Blue: `der Betreff`, `der Anhang`, `der Absender`, `der Empfänger`, `der Erhalt`. Red: `die Anrede`, `die Grußformel`, `die Unterschrift`, `die Kündigung`.

**Goldlist 15.**

Fifteenth page - today is divisible by three.

<Steps>

1. **Fresh double page**, today's date, plus *Goldlist* and the number 15.

2. **Twenty-five items by hand**, German left, English right, numbered. Take them from Tag 43 and Tag 44 - the rent words, the advert abbreviations, the telephone sentences. Write the rescue sentences **whole**, as sentences, because that is how they have to come out.

3. **Say each item aloud once as you write it.** Once.

4. **Read the whole list aloud once** when finished, slowly, without stopping.

5. **Close the notebook and walk away.**

6. **Write in your calendar, 14 days from today:** *Goldlist 15 distillieren* - that is Tag 59.
