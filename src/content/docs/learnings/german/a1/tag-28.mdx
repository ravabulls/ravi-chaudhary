Four weeks. The last day of this block, and it is deliberately the most practical: **the weather**, which is what Germans actually talk about in the first thirty seconds of any encounter, and **`es gibt`**, which is one of the half-dozen most useful constructions in the language.

Nothing structurally new, except one small thing worth its own section: `es gibt` takes the **accusative**, which trips people up because the English *there is* feels like a description rather than an action. `Es gibt **einen** Supermarkt` - there is a supermarket.

And then the four-week assessment, which is the honest part of today.

Section 10 closes the block with a self-assessment - do it properly rather than skipping to the end, because the answer to *what can I actually do in German* is the thing that decides what week five should contain.

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### es gibt

**`es gibt`** means *there is* and *there are* - one form for both, and it never changes:

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| **Es gibt** einen Supermarkt in der Straße. | There is a supermarket in the street. |
| **Es gibt** viele Cafés in Hamburg. | There are a lot of cafés in Hamburg. |
| **Gibt es** hier ein Café? | Is there a café here? |
| **Es gibt** kein Problem. | There is no problem. |

<Aside type="tip" title="es gibt takes the accusative">
`Es gibt **einen** Supermarkt`, not `ein Supermarkt`. Literally the phrase means *it gives*, and whatever it gives is the direct object - so the thing that exists is in the accusative.

The practical consequence is small but constant: **masculine nouns after `es gibt` take `-en`.** `Es gibt einen Bahnhof.` `Es gibt einen Bus.` `Es gibt keinen Supermarkt.` Feminine and neuter are unaffected, as always.

The question form just inverts: `**Gibt es** hier einen Supermarkt?` - the single most useful question for finding anything in a strange town.
</Aside>

### Das Wetter

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| Wie ist das Wetter? | what is the weather like |
| Es ist warm / kalt | it is warm / cold |
| Es ist schön / schlecht | it is nice / bad |
| Es regnet | it is raining |
| Es schneit | it is snowing |
| Die Sonne scheint | the sun is shining |
| Es ist windig | it is windy |
| Es sind zwanzig Grad | it is twenty degrees |

Almost all of these use **`es`** as an empty subject, exactly like English *it*. That makes the weather the easiest topic in German - no gender, no case, no plural.

### Die Jahreszeiten und Monate

| Jahreszeit | im ... |
|---|---|
| der Frühling | im Frühling |
| der Sommer | im Sommer |
| der Herbst | im Herbst |
| der Winter | im Winter |

All four masculine, all taking `im` - which is `in dem`, the dative contraction from Tag 22.

The months are all masculine too: `Januar, Februar, März, April, Mai, Juni, Juli, August, September, Oktober, November, Dezember`, and all take `im`: `im Januar`, `im Juli`.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Wie ist das Wetter? | what is the weather like | the opener |
| Es ist kalt und es regnet | it is cold and it is raining | |
| Die Sonne scheint | the sun is shining | |
| Es sind zwanzig Grad | it is twenty degrees | note: sind, not ist |
| Im Sommer ist es warm | in summer it is warm | im plus verb second |
| Im Winter schneit es oft | in winter it often snows | |
| Gibt es hier einen Supermarkt? | is there a supermarket here | accusative |
| Es gibt viele Cafés in Hamburg | there are a lot of cafés in Hamburg | |
| Das Wetter ist heute besser | the weather is better today | comparative |
| Es ist schönes Wetter, nicht wahr? | nice weather, isn't it | nicht wahr, the tag question |
| Ich mag den Sommer lieber | I prefer the summer | accusative after mögen |
| Hoffentlich regnet es nicht | hopefully it will not rain | hoffentlich plus verb second |

<Aside type="caution" title="Two small things in that list">
**`Es sind zwanzig Grad`** - plural verb, because twenty degrees is plural. `Es ist ein Grad` for one. Germans do say `Es hat zwanzig Grad` as well, especially in the south.

**`nicht wahr?`** is the German tag question - *isn't it, don't you think* - and unlike English it has exactly one form for every sentence. English has to pick between *isn't it, doesn't he, haven't you, wasn't she*; German says `nicht wahr?` to all of them. In speech you will also hear plain `oder?` and, in the north, `ne?`, which do the same job.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

The first minute of a conversation between neighbours, which is almost pure weather and `es gibt`.

> **Nachbarin:** Guten Morgen! Schönes Wetter heute, nicht wahr?
> **Ravi:** Guten Morgen! Ja, die Sonne scheint. Es sind ungefähr zwanzig Grad.
> **Nachbarin:** Endlich! Letzte Woche hat es jeden Tag geregnet.
> **Ravi:** Das stimmt. Ich bin fast jeden Tag zu Hause geblieben.
> **Nachbarin:** Mögen Sie den Sommer lieber als den Winter?
> **Ravi:** Ja, viel lieber. Im Winter ist es hier zu kalt für mich.
> **Nachbarin:** In Indien ist es wärmer, nicht wahr?
> **Ravi:** Viel wärmer. Im Sommer sind es manchmal vierzig Grad.
> **Nachbarin:** Vierzig! Das ist zu warm für mich.
> **Ravi:** Entschuldigung, darf ich etwas fragen? Gibt es hier einen Supermarkt?
> **Nachbarin:** Ja, es gibt einen in der Straße, gegenüber der Post.
> **Ravi:** Und ist er am Sonntag offen?
> **Nachbarin:** Nein, am Sonntag sind die Geschäfte zu. Das ist in Deutschland so.
> **Ravi:** Gut zu wissen. Danke!
> **Nachbarin:** Gern. Hoffentlich bleibt das Wetter schön. Schönen Tag noch!

`endlich` is finally, `fast` is almost, `jeden Tag` is every day, `offen` and `zu` are open and closed, `die Geschäfte` are the shops, `Gut zu wissen` is *good to know*. All free.

The piece of real information in there is worth more than the vocabulary: **shops in Germany are closed on Sundays.** That is the kind of thing a language course rarely tells you and which reshapes a week.

Read it aloud twice, both parts. Then cover Ravi's lines and have the conversation about **your** actual weather, your actual preference, and something you actually need to find.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

The first minute of a conversation, run five times. Stand up.

```text
Du bist eine Nachbarin in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger (Tag 28, A1).
Heute übe ich Smalltalk, das Wetter und es gibt.

Wir treffen uns FÜNF MAL kurz - jedes Mal ein neuer Tag mit anderem
Wetter. Fang jedes Mal mit dem Wetter an und benutze "nicht wahr?".
Dann frag mich eine normale Smalltalk-Frage: Wie geht es Ihnen? Was
machen Sie heute? Mögen Sie den Sommer? Waren Sie am Wochenende weg?

Ich frage dich jedes Mal, ob es hier etwas gibt - einen Supermarkt,
eine Apotheke, ein Café, einen Bahnhof, eine Post. Antworte mit "Es gibt"
und einer kurzen Wegbeschreibung.

Sprich langsam, maximal neun Wörter pro Satz. Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
Whether the first thirty seconds come out without thought. Smalltalk is the only part of a language where being automatic matters more than being correct, because its entire function is to fill the gap while two people decide what they actually want to say.

By the fifth repetition the weather exchange should need no planning at all. If it does, that is the one thing to drill tomorrow - it is the gateway to every other conversation you will have.
</Aside>

### Was du jetzt sicher kannst

<Steps>

1. **Sich vorstellen und ein Gespräch beginnen** - greet correctly for the time of day, say who you are, where you are from, what you do, how long you have been here, and ask the same back.

2. **Etwas bestellen und bezahlen** - a full café or restaurant visit from sitting down to leaving, including `zusammen oder getrennt` and the change.

3. **Nach dem Weg fragen und ihn verstehen** - ask, and follow a four-step answer, and repeat each step back to confirm it.

4. **Einen Termin machen** - name a day, name a time, understand a time said back at you in both the `halb` and the 24-hour form, and confirm it.

5. **Über die Vergangenheit sprechen** - what you did yesterday and at the weekend, with both helper verbs.

6. **Über deine Familie und deine Wohnung sprechen** - who they are, where they are, how many rooms, what is where.

7. **Sagen, was du willst, musst, darfst und kannst** - all six modal verbs, and the difference between *not having to* and *not being allowed to*.

8. **Eine Meinung sagen und begründen** - what you think, what you prefer, and why, with `weil` and `dass`.

9. **Smalltalk machen** - the weather, the seasons, and whether there is a supermarket nearby.

</Steps>

### Der ehrliche Test

Four questions. Answer them honestly, because they decide what the next block should contain.

| Frage | |
|---|---|
| **1.** Can you run a ten-minute conversation about yourself without reaching for English? | ja / nein |
| **2.** Can you ask fifteen *different* questions in ten minutes? | ja / nein |
| **3.** Do the dative articles come out right without thinking? | ja / nein |
| **4.** Can you tell a story about yesterday with the right helper verb each time? | ja / nein |

<Aside type="caution" title="What the answers mean">
**Four yeses** - A1 is done as a speaking level, and the next block should push into new ground: the Präteritum, adjective endings, and the vocabulary of work and bureaucracy.

**Yes to 1 and 2, no to 3 or 4** - the most common result, and not a problem. Your conversation works and your accuracy lags. The next block should keep the drills and add volume rather than new grammar.

**No to 1 or 2** - the grammar has gone in and the speaking has not. The fix is not more pages; it is more of section 8. Twenty minutes of talking to a voice AI every day for a week, using only the material you already have, will move this faster than any new material.

A1 is roughly 300 to 400 words and the structures on this page. You have 288 drilled chunks and five reliable gender rules, which means the grammar of A1 is essentially complete and the remaining gap is vocabulary and speed - both of which are volume problems rather than understanding problems. That is a good place to be at day twenty-eight.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip" title="One line about the next four weeks">
The structures from here are fewer and the words are many more. Week five onwards is where German stops being a puzzle and starts being a vocabulary problem - which is slower and much less frustrating.

Specifically, what is still missing: the **Präteritum** (`war`, `hatte`, `konnte` properly), **adjective endings** (`ein **gutes** Buch`), the **Genitiv**, **reflexive verbs** (`sich freuen`), the **du-form** used consistently, and the vocabulary of actually living somewhere - doctor, bank, authorities, a flat contract, a job interview. That is the A2 shape, and it is what the next block is for.
</Aside>
