Yesterday you could say who you are. Today you get the verb that carries half of all beginner German - **sein** - in all six of its forms, and the three question words that open almost every real conversation: **wie**, **wo**, **was**.

The sounds today are the three umlauts. Two of them, ö and ü, do not exist in English at all. That is not a problem to postpone; it is the single thing that makes a beginner sound foreign for years if it is skipped in week one.

## 2. Aussprache - die Umlaute ä, ö, ü

Three letters, three sounds. One is easy, two are not.

**ä** is the easy one: it is simply an **e**. Long ä sounds like the *ay* in *say* held flat; short ä sounds like the *e* in *bed*. For a beginner, treating ä and e as the same sound is accurate enough. `Käse` and `Bäcker` give you both lengths.

**ö** and **ü** are built the same way, and there is a mechanical trick for each. Say a vowel, freeze your tongue, then move only your lips:

| Umlaut | Start from | Then | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|---|
| ö | say **e** as in *say* | keep the tongue exactly there, round the lips as if for *o* | schön, hören, Köln |
| ü | say **ee** as in *see* | keep the tongue exactly there, round the lips as if for *oo* | müde, über, Tschüss |

That is the whole secret: **tongue forward, lips rounded**. English never combines those two things, which is why there is no English equivalent to point at. If you round the lips and let the tongue slide back as well, ö collapses into *oh* and ü collapses into *oo* - that is the mistake to listen for.

The contrast that matters most in week one is **ä against e**, because they are close, and **ü against u**, because they are not:

| Paar | Unterschied |
|---|---|
| Bäcker / Becker | almost none - both are the e sound |
| müde / Mude | ü is lips rounded, tongue forward |
| über / Uber | same |
| schön / schon | ö versus plain o, and the meaning changes |
| Köln / Kohlen | ö versus long o |

<Aside type="caution" title="The two-letter escape hatch, and when to stop using it">
On a keyboard without umlauts you may write `ae`, `oe`, `ue` and `ss` - `Tschuess`, `muede`. The drills on this site accept that and then tell you to write it properly. It is a typing convenience only: it must never change how you **say** the word. Fix the keyboard this week.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Das Verb sein

German has one verb you cannot avoid, and it is irregular in every person. Learn it as a block, out loud, six forms in one breath, the way you would learn a phone number:

| Person | Form | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| ich | **bin** | Ich bin müde |
| du | **bist** | Du bist Student |
| er / sie / es | **ist** | Das ist ein Café |
| wir | **sind** | Wir sind aus Indien |
| ihr | **seid** | Ihr seid in Hamburg |
| sie / Sie | **sind** | Sind Sie aus Deutschland |

Two things to notice now and stop being surprised by later. **Sie** with a capital is the polite *you*, and it takes the same form as *they*: `sind`. And German does not use an article before a job: `Ich bin Student`, never *ich bin ein Student*.

### Die Fragewörter wie, wo, was

| Wort | English | Frage |
|---|---|---|
| **wie** | how / what (in a name) | Wie geht es Ihnen? |
| **wo** | where | Wo ist der Bahnhof? |
| **was** | what | Was ist das? |

The pattern is fixed and worth saying aloud five times: **question word, then the verb, then the rest.** `Wo ist der Bahnhof?` Never *wo der Bahnhof ist*.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

Read each aloud twice before the drill.

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Wie geht es Ihnen? | how are you | formal, the standard opener |
| Danke, gut | fine, thanks | the standard answer |
| Und Ihnen? | and you | hand the question back |
| Ich bin müde | I am tired | sein, ich form |
| Ich bin Student | I am a student | no article before a job |
| Sind Sie aus Deutschland? | are you from Germany | verb first in a yes-no question |
| Wo ist der Bahnhof? | where is the station | the most useful sentence in Germany |
| Was ist das? | what is that | point at the thing |
| Das ist ein Café | that is a cafe | the answer to the one above |
| Entschuldigung | excuse me | to open, or to apologise |
| Sprechen Sie Englisch? | do you speak English | use it late, not first |
| Ich verstehe nicht | I do not understand | say this instead of going silent |

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Paste this into a voice AI and actually speak. Ten minutes, out loud, standing up.

```text
Du bist eine freundliche Person an einem Bahnhof in Hamburg. Ich bin
Anfänger (Tag 2, A1). Sprich sehr langsam und nur mit diesen Wörtern:
Guten Tag, Hallo, Tschüss, Auf Wiedersehen, Entschuldigung, Wie geht es
Ihnen, Danke gut, Und Ihnen, Ich bin müde, Ich bin Student, Sind Sie aus
Deutschland, Wo ist der Bahnhof, Was ist das, Das ist ein Café, Sprechen
Sie Englisch, Ich verstehe nicht, Ich heiße, Ich wohne in, Ich bin aus,
Ja genau, Nein danke.
Stelle mir drei Fragen: wie es mir geht, woher ich komme, und ob ich
Student bin. Benutze nur die Formen von sein: bin, bist, ist, sind, seid.
Maximal sechs Wörter pro Satz. Wenn ich einen Fehler mache, wiederhole den
Satz einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung. Fang jetzt an und begrüße mich.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="Use Ich verstehe nicht on purpose">
Today you have the sentence that keeps a conversation alive: `Ich verstehe nicht`. Use it at least three times in the ten minutes, and do **not** follow it with English. Say it, wait, and let them say the sentence again more slowly. Learning to stay inside German while lost is a separate skill from vocabulary, and this is the day it starts.
</Aside>
