Three days of sounds and nouns are now behind you, so today is the first day that builds a sentence you could actually use at a counter: **haben** in all six forms, the indefinite articles **ein** and **eine**, the negation **kein**, and enough numbers to understand a price.

The sounds block does not add anything new. Instead it mixes everything from Tag 01 to Tag 03 into one pile of minimal pairs, which is harder than it sounds - knowing a rule and hearing the difference at speed are different skills.

One thing to notice as you go: **kein is just ein with a k in front of it**. German negates a noun by changing its article, not by adding a separate word. That single fact removes a whole category of beginner mistakes later.

## 2. Aussprache - alles aus Tag 01 bis Tag 03

No new sounds today. Everything you have met so far, in one table, then mixed into pairs that force you to choose fast.

| Zeichen | Klang | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| Vokal + h, oder doppelt | lang | Bahnhof, Tee |
| Vokal + zwei Konsonanten | kurz | Mann, kostet |
| ä | like the e in bed | Käse, Männer |
| ö | lips rounded, tongue on e | schön, Köln |
| ü | lips rounded, tongue on i | müde, Tschüss |
| ei | rhymes with English eye | eine, Zeit, kein |
| ie | long ee | viel, Wien, sieben |
| eu | oy, as in boy | teuer, Euro, neun |
| au | ow, as in how | Haus, Frau, kaufen |
| w | English v | Wasser, wohnen, wie viel |
| v | English f | viel, Vater |
| z | ts | zwanzig, Zeit, zehn |
| ch after i, e, ä, ö, ü | soft, at the front | ich, Milch |
| ch after a, o, u, au | hard, at the back | acht, Buch, auch |

Now the pairs. Say each pair three times, then say only the right-hand word and check that it still sounds different from the left:

| Erstes Wort | Zweites Wort | Was sich ändert |
|---|---|---|
| Wein | Wien | ei against ie |
| Haus | Häuser | au against the oy sound |
| Mutter | Mütter | u against ü |
| schon | schön | o against ö |
| viel | wie | f-sound against v-sound |
| Zeit | seit | ts against a soft s |
| ich | ach | soft ch against hard ch |
| Stadt | Straße | short a against long a |

<Aside type="caution" title="The three that still slip">
Under time pressure, the same three go wrong. **w** is a v: `wie viel` starts *vee feel*. **v** is an f: `viel` is *feel*, not *veel*. **z** is ts: `zwanzig` is *tsvanzich*, and the ending `-ig` is itself pronounced like the soft ich. Say `zwanzig` ten times now, correctly, and you have saved yourself a month.
</Aside>

| kein Problem | no problem | das Problem, so kein |
| Ich habe Zeit | I have time | die Zeit, no article needed here |
| Ich habe keine Zeit | I have no time | the polite way to decline |
| Haben Sie Zeit? | do you have time | formal |
| Was kostet das? | what does that cost | the short version |
| Das kostet drei Euro | that costs three euros | der Euro, but prices stay plain |
| Das ist zu teuer | that is too expensive | zu means too, not to |
| zwanzig Euro | twenty euros | Euro does not add an s |
| Wie viel kostet der Kaffee? | how much is the coffee | two words: wie viel |
| Ich habe eine Frage | I have a question | say this in every class |

### Die Grammatik dahinter

**haben - to have.** The second verb you own after `sein`. It is irregular in exactly one place, the `du` and `er` forms, which drop the b:

| Person | Form | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| ich | habe | Ich habe Zeit |
| du | hast | Du hast Zeit |
| er / sie / es | hat | Er hat Zeit |
| wir | haben | Wir haben Zeit |
| ihr | habt | Ihr habt Zeit |
| sie / Sie | haben | Haben Sie Zeit? |

**ein und eine - a and an.** On Tag 03 every noun came with `der`, `die` or `das`. The indefinite article follows straight from that, and there are only two forms to learn:

| Tag 03 sagt | Unbestimmt | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| der | ein | der Kaffee, ein Kaffee |
| das | ein | das Haus, ein Haus |
| die | eine | die Frage, eine Frage |

So `der` and `das` share one form, and only `die` gets the extra e. This is why the gender colours in your notebook matter: get the gender wrong and every article in the sentence goes wrong with it.

There is one shape you already have as a chunk: `Einen Kaffee, bitte`. When a `der` word is the **object** of the sentence rather than the subject, `ein` becomes `einen`. That is next week's grammar. Today, just notice that it happens and keep the phrase whole.

**kein und keine - not a, no.** German does not say *not a coffee*. It puts a k on the front of the article and the whole thing becomes one negative word:

| Positiv | Negativ | English |
|---|---|---|
| ein Problem | kein Problem | no problem |
| eine Frage | keine Frage | no question |
| Ich habe Zeit | Ich habe keine Zeit | I have no time |
| Wasser | kein Wasser | no water |

The endings are identical to `ein` and `eine`: `die` words take `keine`, `der` and `das` words take `kein`. As with `einen`, a `der` word as an object gives `keinen` - `Ich habe keinen Kaffee`. Notice it, do not drill it yet.

**Die Zahlen bis hundert.** First the twelve you cannot derive:

| 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| null | eins | zwei | drei | vier | fünf | sechs |

| 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sieben | acht | neun | zehn | elf | zwölf |

From 13 to 19 the unit takes `-zehn` on the end: dreizehn, vierzehn, fünfzehn, **sechzehn** (sechs loses its s), **siebzehn** (sieben loses its en), achtzehn, neunzehn.

The tens: zwanzig, **dreißig** (the only one with ß and not -zig), vierzig, fünfzig, **sechzig**, **siebzig**, achtzig, neunzig, hundert.

Then the part that feels backwards. Everything between the tens is said **unit first, then und, then the ten**, and the whole thing is written as **one word with no spaces**:

| Zahl | Deutsch | Wörtlich |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | einundzwanzig | one-and-twenty |
| 34 | vierunddreißig | four-and-thirty |
| 47 | siebenundvierzig | seven-and-forty |
| 68 | achtundsechzig | eight-and-sixty |
| 99 | neunundneunzig | nine-and-ninety |

Two traps. `eins` loses its s inside a compound: 21 is `einundzwanzig`, never *einsundzwanzig*. And a spoken price arrives in that order too, so when a cashier says a number you must hold the first half until the second half lands.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Paste this into a voice AI and actually speak. Ten minutes, out loud, standing up. Today you are at a kiosk and the only thing that matters is surviving a price.

```text
Du arbeitest an einem Kiosk in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger (Tag 4, A1).
Sprich sehr langsam und nur mit diesen Wörtern: Guten Tag, Hallo, bitte,
danke, Entschuldigung, Kaffee, Wasser, Milch, Zeit, Frage, Problem, Euro,
kosten, haben, ein, eine, kein, keine, zu teuer, ja, nein, und die Zahlen
von eins bis hundert.
Frag mich zuerst: Guten Tag, was möchten Sie? Sag mir dann einen Preis mit
einer Zahl zwischen zwanzig und hundert, zum Beispiel vierunddreißig Euro,
damit ich die Zahl rückwärts hören muss. Wenn ich sage: Das ist zu teuer,
mach den Preis billiger.
Maximal sechs Wörter pro Satz. Wenn ich einen Fehler mache, wiederhole den
Satz einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung. Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="Make it say the numbers slowly">
The one thing to get out of these ten minutes is the reversed number. If the price goes past you, say `Noch einmal, bitte` - you meet it properly on Tag 07, but it works now. Do not ask for the digits in English, and do not switch languages to fix a misunderstanding.
</Aside>
