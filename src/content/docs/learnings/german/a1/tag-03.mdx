Today is the day German stops being a list of phrases and starts being a system. Every German noun has one of three genders - **der**, **die** or **das** - and every noun is written with a capital letter. Neither rule has exceptions worth worrying about at A1.

The sounds today are the ones that decide whether a written word comes out right: the four diphthongs, the two different ch sounds, and what happens to **st** and **sp** at the start of a word. The pair **ei** and **ie** causes more beginner errors than any other spelling in the language, so it gets drilled twice.

This is also the first Goldlist day, and the first page where your notebook needs three colours.

## 2. Aussprache - Diphthonge, ch, sch, st und sp

### Die vier Diphthonge

A diphthong is two written vowels making one sound. German has four, and each one is completely regular - once you know the rule, you can say any new word on sight.

| Schreibweise | Klingt wie | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| **ei** | English *eye* | ein, Wein, heißen, Zeit |
| **ie** | English *ee* | die, wie, viel, Bier |
| **eu** and **äu** | English *oy* in *boy* | neun, heute, Häuser |
| **au** | English *ow* in *now* | Frau, Haus, auch, aus |

### Die ei / ie Falle

This is the one to overlearn, because English spelling hands you exactly the wrong instinct. There is no clever rule to derive. There is one blunt fact, repeated until it is boring:

- **ei** sounds like English **eye**. `ein`, `Wein`, `nein`, `heißen`, `drei`.
- **ie** sounds like English **ee**. `die`, `wie`, `Bier`, `vier`, `Sie`.

If you need a hook: read the pair as its **second** letter. In **ei** the second letter is i, and you say *eye*. In **ie** the second letter is e, and you say *ee*. But the two lines above are what has to end up in your mouth, not the hook.

Now say these two columns across, not down, ten times:

| ei = eye | ie = ee |
|---|---|
| nein | nie |
| Wein | wie |
| drei | die |
| mein | Miete |
| Bein | Biene |

If you catch yourself reading `die` as *dye*, stop and do the column again. Every beginner does it. The ones who fix it in week one stop doing it forever.

### ch - zwei Laute, nicht einer

The letters **ch** cover two different sounds, and which one you get depends only on the vowel in front:

| Nach | Laut | Wie | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|---|
| i, e, ä, ö, ü, and consonants | the **ich-Laut** | soft, breathy, like a whispered English *h* in *huge* | ich, nicht, Milch, möchte |
| a, o, u, au | the **ach-Laut** | rough, from the back of the throat | auch, Buch, acht, Nacht |

There is no English sound for either. The safest beginner error is to make the ich-Laut too soft rather than turning it into a *k* or a *sh* - `ich` must never come out as *ick* or *ish*.

### sch, st und sp

- **sch** is a single sound: English *sh*, never an *s* followed by a *ch*. `Deutsch`, `schön`, `Entschuldigung`.
- At the **start of a word**, **st** and **sp** are pronounced *scht* and *schp*. `Straße` is *Schtraße*. `Student` is *Schtudent*. `sprechen` is *schprechen*. Inside or at the end of a word they are normal: `ist`, `Post`.

<Aside type="caution" title="You already mispronounce three words you know">
`Straße`, `Student` and `Sprechen Sie Englisch` all start with the *sch* sound, and you have been saying them since Tag 02. Say all three aloud now, correctly, five times. Fixing a word you already use is much harder than learning a new one, which is exactly why this comes on day three and not day thirty.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Substantive

### Zwei Regeln, keine Ausnahmen

**Erstens: jedes Substantiv bekommt einen Großbuchstaben.** Every noun is capitalised, everywhere in the sentence, always. `der Mann`, `die Frau`, `das Kind`. This is not decoration - it is how a German reader finds the nouns in a long sentence, and the drill engine on this page marks wrong capitals as a near miss and tells you off.

**Zweitens: jedes Substantiv hat ein Geschlecht.** Every noun is *der*, *die* or *das*. The gender is mostly not guessable and has almost nothing to do with meaning: `das Mädchen`, the girl, is neuter. So do not try to work it out.

| Artikel | Geschlecht | Farbe im Heft |
|---|---|---|
| **der** | maskulin | blau |
| **die** | feminin | rot |
| **das** | neutral | grün |

<Aside type="caution" title="Learn the article WITH the noun, never after">
This is the single highest-value habit of your first month. Never write or say `Bahnhof`. Say **der Bahnhof**. Never think of the word for street as `Straße`; think of it as **die Straße**, one word, three syllables, one unit. A noun learned without its article has to be relearned later with it, and relearning costs roughly four times what learning it right the first time costs. From today, a bare noun in your notebook counts as a mistake and goes on the Fehlerliste.
</Aside>

### Die zwölf Substantive

Read each aloud twice, **always with the article**, and note the sound from section 2 in the right-hand column.

| Deutsch | English | Laut von heute |
|---|---|---|
| **der** Mann | the man | short a |
| **die** Frau | the woman | au as in *now* |
| **das** Kind | the child | short i, and the final d sounds like a t |
| **der** Bahnhof | the station | long a from the h |
| **die** Straße | the street | starts *scht*, and ß is a sharp s |
| **das** Haus | the house | au as in *now* |
| **der** Kaffee | the coffee | stress on the first syllable |
| **die** Milch | the milk | ich-Laut at the end |
| **das** Wasser | the water | W is an English v, short a |
| **der** Zug | the train | Z is *ts*, long u |
| **die** Stadt | the city | starts *scht*, short a |
| **das** Büro | the office | ü, and the stress is on the second syllable |

## 6. Text und Hören

Every line uses only Tag 01, Tag 02 and Tag 03.

> **A:** Guten Tag! Entschuldigung, wo ist der Bahnhof?
> **B:** Was ist das? Ich verstehe nicht.
> **A:** Der Bahnhof. Der Zug.
> **B:** Ah, ja. Der Bahnhof ist in der Stadt.
> **A:** Danke. Und das Büro?
> **B:** Das Büro ist in der Straße.
> **A:** Ich verstehe. Ich bin müde. Auf Wiedersehen!
> **B:** Tschüss!

One thing will look wrong, and it is not a typo: **die Stadt** becomes `in der Stadt`, and **die Straße** becomes `in der Straße`. After `in`, the articles change shape. That change is a whole day of its own later in the course. For now, take `in der Stadt` and `in der Straße` as two fixed phrases and do not try to reason about them.

Read it aloud twice, both parts. Then cover the text and say A's lines from memory.

## 7. Bidirektional

These are **the same eight sentences you translated on Tag 01**, exactly as promised on that page - but the direction is reversed. Two days ago you built them from English into German. Today you read the German and give the English back.

That is the whole point of the bidirectional method: a sentence you can only produce in one direction is half learned. Read each German line aloud first, then write the English, then compare. Where your English differs from the model, the interesting question is never which is better - it is which German word you skipped over without really reading.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Paste this into a voice AI and actually speak. Ten minutes, out loud, standing up.

```text
Du bist eine freundliche Person in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger (Tag 3, A1).
Sprich sehr langsam und nur mit diesen Wörtern:
der Mann, die Frau, das Kind, der Bahnhof, die Straße, das Haus,
der Kaffee, die Milch, das Wasser, der Zug, die Stadt, das Büro,
Guten Tag, Hallo, Tschüss, Auf Wiedersehen, Entschuldigung, Danke,
Wie geht es Ihnen, Danke gut, Und Ihnen, Ich bin müde, Ich bin Student,
Wo ist, Was ist das, Das ist, Sprechen Sie Englisch, Ich verstehe nicht,
Ich heiße, Ich wohne in, Ich bin aus, Ja genau, Nein danke.
Zeige auf Dinge und frage mich immer wieder: Was ist das? Ich antworte mit
Das ist der, die oder das. Wenn ich den falschen Artikel sage, wiederhole
den Satz einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung. Frage mich auch zweimal: Wo ist
der Bahnhof? Maximal sechs Wörter pro Satz. Fang jetzt an und begrüße mich.
```

Ten minutes of `Was ist das? Das ist der Zug.` sounds absurdly simple and is exactly right for day three. You are not practising conversation today, you are burning twelve article-noun pairs into your mouth under mild time pressure. If the AI starts explaining grammar or adding new vocabulary, stop it and paste the prompt again.

**Genus-Farben.** This is the day the colour system starts. **Goldlist.** Today is the first Goldlist page, and it has a strict shape:

<Steps>

1. Open a fresh double page and write today's date at the top, plus the word *Goldlist* and the number 1.

2. By hand, in your best handwriting, write **25 items**: German on the left, English on the right. Use today's twelve nouns with their articles, and fill the remaining thirteen from Tag 01 and Tag 02.

3. Say each item aloud once as you write it. Do not rush, do not abbreviate, and do not use a screen.

4. Read the whole list aloud once, slowly, top to bottom.

5. Close the notebook and do nothing else with it. Do not test yourself, do not reread it tonight, do not copy it to an app.

6. Write in your calendar, 14 days from today: *Goldlist 1 distillieren*. On that day you will reread the list and find that roughly a third of it has stuck without any revision at all. You cross those out and rewrite only the rest, which becomes list 2. That waiting period is the whole mechanism, and checking early destroys it.
