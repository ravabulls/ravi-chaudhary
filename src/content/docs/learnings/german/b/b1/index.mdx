**The level that counts.**

B1 is where German stops being something you are learning and becomes something you have. It is the level of the **Integrationskurs**, the usual language requirement for **naturalisation**, and the point at which an employer treats your German as a working language rather than a project.

It is also the first level where the grammar is no longer the hard part. A2 finished the structures a speaker needs; B1 adds the structures a *reader and writer* needs - the passive, the past subjunctive, nominalisation - and then spends most of its time on something else entirely: **having opinions about things, in German, at length.**

<Aside type="caution" title="Where this module stands">
**The syllabus below is final and published. The pages are not written yet.**

That is deliberate rather than lazy. Sixty B1 pages written before the first sixty have been worked through once would be sixty pages built on a guess. The A1 and A2 modules exist because they were built in order, each week answering what the previous week actually turned out to leave missing.

What is on this page is the plan, in enough detail that you can see exactly what is coming and start on the parts that do not need a page - the listening and the talking, which is most of B1 anyway.
</Aside>

## The honest arithmetic

Worth stating before the table, because B1 is where self-study courses quietly overpromise.

<Steps>

1. **A1 plus A2 is roughly 180 to 200 hours** of study from zero, for most learners, in most settings.

2. **B1 is roughly 350 to 400 hours in total** - so another 150 to 200 hours on top.

3. **This course is 120 days at two hours**, which is 240 hours, of which 120 are these sixty B1 pages.

4. **That is short of the range**, and the gap is closed by exactly two things that are not pages: **hours of German audio**, and **hours of talking to people**. Every week below has both built into it, and they are not optional extras - they are where the missing hours live.

</Steps>

## The nine weeks

Each week keeps the same nine-block page as A1 and A2, and the three timed schedules run on unbroken - review at N-1, N-2, N-5, N-12 and N-30, the bidirectional chain at N to N+2, and the Goldlist cycle keyed to the day number.

  <Card title="Woche 10 - Tag 61 bis 67" icon="setting">
    **Das Passiv.** Präsens, Präteritum and Perfekt passive, the passive with modal verbs, `werden` against `sein` (Vorgangs- and Zustandspassiv), and the `man` construction that German often prefers to either.

    *Thema:* Arbeit und Beruf - the workplace, meetings, processes, instructions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Woche 11 - Tag 68 bis 74" icon="seti:clock">
    **Das Plusquamperfekt**, and the conjunctions that need it: `nachdem`, `bevor`, `sobald`, `seitdem`, `während`, `bis`. Sequencing two past events correctly, which A2 could only do with `dann`.

    *Thema:* Ausbildung und Studium - qualifications, recognition of foreign degrees, applications.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Woche 12 - Tag 75 bis 81" icon="approve-check">
    **Purpose and concession.** `damit` and `um ... zu` and the rule for choosing between them, `so dass`, `falls`, `sofern`, `obwohl` against `trotzdem` against `trotz`.

    *Thema:* Gesundheit - the body in detail, prevention, insurance, a specialist appointment.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Woche 13 - Tag 82 bis 88" icon="random">
    **Konjunktiv II in the past.** `hätte gemacht`, `wäre gegangen`, `hätte machen können` - unreal conditions about things that are already over, regrets, reproaches, and the politest refusals German has.

    *Thema:* Umwelt und Klima - the single most common B1 exam topic, and unavoidable in Germany.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Woche 14 - Tag 89 bis 95" icon="document">
    **Nominalisierung and Verbalisierung** - turning `weil es regnet` into `wegen des Regens` and back, which is the one skill that separates B1 writing from A2 writing. Plus the genitive prepositions (`aufgrund`, `innerhalb`, `außerhalb`, `während`, `trotz`) and the **n-Deklination**.

    *Thema:* Medien und Internet - news, platforms, privacy, screen time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Woche 15 - Tag 96 bis 102" icon="list-format">
    **Two-part connectors** - `je ... desto`, `entweder ... oder`, `weder ... noch`, `sowohl ... als auch`, `nicht nur ... sondern auch` - and **Partizip I and II used as adjectives** (`die steigenden Preise`, `die renovierte Wohnung`).

    *Thema:* Wohnen und Zusammenleben - neighbours, the Hausordnung, shared flats, conflict.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Woche 16 - Tag 103 bis 109" icon="comment">
    **Reported speech.** `indirekte Rede` with and without `dass`, **Konjunktiv I** to recognise rather than produce, the **subjective modal verbs** (`Er soll krank sein`, `Das muss teuer gewesen sein`), and the **Nomen-Verb-Verbindungen** that German news runs on - `eine Entscheidung treffen`, `in Frage kommen`, `zur Verfügung stehen`.

    *Thema:* Politik, Gesellschaft und Ehrenamt - how Germany is governed, volunteering, and having an opinion about both.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Woche 17 - Tag 110 bis 116" icon="open-book">
    **Wortbildung** - the prefixes and suffixes that let you guess a word you have never seen (`-ung`, `-keit`, `-heit`, `-bar`, `-los`, `un-`, `ver-`, `be-`, `ent-`) - plus a second pass at verbs with fixed prepositions, and a **Redemittel bank** for each of the four exam parts.

    *Thema:* Geld, Konsum und Reisen - contracts, cancellations, complaints, bookings.

## What B1 is tested on

Three exams matter in Germany and they test the same level in slightly different shapes. The **formats** are published by the examining bodies; what follows is a description of them, not material from them.

| Prüfung | Wofür | Besonderheit |
|---|---|---|
| **Goethe-Zertifikat B1** | the general-purpose proof, recognised everywhere | the four parts can be taken and passed **separately**, which is unusually forgiving |
| **telc Deutsch B1** | equally recognised, common at adult education centres | one sitting, slightly different task types |
| **DTZ - Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer** | the test at the end of the Integrationskurs | scored across B1 **and** A2, so it reports which level you actually reached |

All three are built from four parts - **Lesen**, **Hören**, **Schreiben**, **Sprechen** - and the last two are where self-taught candidates lose marks, because both are judged on things a drill page cannot rehearse: whether your letter has the right register, and whether you can plan something out loud with a stranger.

Weeks 17 and 18 are built around exactly those two.

<Aside type="tip" title="What to do about B1 before these pages exist">
Three things, and none needs a page.

**Audio, twenty minutes a day.** The same material more than once: once cold, once with subtitles, once cold again. News in slow German, then real news. This is the gap, and it closes faster than any other.

**One hour a week with people.** A Sprachtreff, a Tandempartner, a sports club, a volunteer shift, the canteen at work. One hour of being talked at by people who are not waiting for you is worth five hours of pages at this level.

**Keep the notebook going.** The Goldlist cycle, the Problemliste and the weekly Fehlerliste from A1 and A2 do not stop at Tag 60 - pages 13 to 18 of the Goldlist all come due after the course ends, which is the point.
</Aside>
