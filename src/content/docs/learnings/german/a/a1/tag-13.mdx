*Tag 13 von 60 - mir, dir, ihm, ihr, Ihnen, und die Verben mit Dativ. Zwei Stunden.*

Yesterday's table had a dative pronoun column you were told to ignore. Today it is the lesson, and it closes the case system for A1 - after this page there is no new case form to learn, only practice.

The reason this day matters more than it looks: a small group of very common German verbs take their object in the **dative** rather than the accusative, and there is no way to predict which from the meaning. `Ich sehe **ihn**` but `Ich helfe **ihm**`. Both are *I [verb] him* in English. Germans do not hear these as the same shape at all, and getting them wrong is the mistake that most reliably marks a sentence as foreign even when every word is right.

And one sentence you have used thirty times finally comes apart: **`Wie geht es Ihnen?`** It is not *how are you*. Literally it is *how goes it to you* - `es` is the subject, `Ihnen` is dative, and you have been producing a dative pronoun since Tag 02.

Two hours, top to bottom, every answer **out loud** before you type it. Nothing is saved. Section 7 is the Tag 11 sentences coming back into English. The hardest thing today is section 3's second half - `gefallen` and `schmecken` put the English subject in the German dative, and that takes more than one pass.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Die Dativpronomen

The third and last pronoun column:

| Nominativ | Akkusativ | **Dativ** | English dative |
|---|---|---|---|
| ich | mich | **mir** | to me |
| du | dich | **dir** | to you |
| er | ihn | **ihm** | to him |
| sie | sie | **ihr** | to her |
| es | es | **ihm** | to it |
| wir | uns | **uns** | to us |
| ihr | euch | **euch** | to you |
| sie / Sie | sie / Sie | **ihnen / Ihnen** | to them / to you |

Three of them are new and worth drilling on their own: **`mir`**, **`ihm`**, **`ihr`**. Three you already have for free, because they are identical to the accusative: `uns`, `euch`, and - the useful one - **`Ihnen`**.

<Aside type="tip" title="ihm and ihr are the pair to drill">
`ihm` is masculine and neuter. `ihr` is feminine. They are one letter apart and they mean opposite genders, which is exactly the shape of error that survives for months if you do not attack it early. Say them as a pair, ten times, out loud, now: *ihm, ihr, ihm, ihr*. Then attach them to nouns from your colour list - blue nouns take `ihm`, red nouns take `ihr`, green nouns take `ihm`.
</Aside>

### Die Verben mit Dativ

A short list, and it covers almost everything you need at A1. These verbs take their object in the dative, always:

| Verb | English | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| helfen | to help | Können Sie **mir** helfen? |
| danken | to thank | Ich danke **Ihnen**. |
| antworten | to answer | Ich antworte **ihm**. |
| gehören | to belong to | Das gehört **mir**. |
| passen | to suit / fit | Das passt **mir** gut. |
| gefallen | to please | Hamburg gefällt **mir**. |
| schmecken | to taste | Der Kaffee schmeckt **mir**. |

`Können Sie mir helfen?` is the most useful sentence on this page and probably the most useful sentence of week two. Learn it before anything else.

### gefallen und schmecken drehen den Satz um

These two work backwards compared to English, and this is the part that needs real attention.

In English *I like Hamburg* makes **I** the subject. In German the thing being liked is the subject and the person doing the liking goes in the dative:

| English | Deutsch | Wörtlich |
|---|---|---|
| I like Hamburg | Hamburg gefällt **mir** | Hamburg pleases to-me |
| I like the coffee | Der Kaffee schmeckt **mir** | the coffee tastes to-me |
| Do you like it? | Gefällt es **Ihnen**? | does it please to-you |
| He likes the city | Die Stadt gefällt **ihm** | the city pleases to-him |

Two consequences you have to watch:

- **The verb agrees with the thing, not the person.** `Hamburg gefällt mir` but `Die Städte gefallen mir`.
- **`schmecken` is only for food and drink.** `gefallen` is for everything else.

You already have a way around all of this from Tag 09 - `Ich finde es gut` - and it is completely correct German. Keep using it. But `Gefällt es Ihnen?` is what a German will actually ask you, so you need to recognise it even on the days you cannot produce it.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Können Sie mir helfen? | can you help me | the sentence of the day |
| Ich helfe Ihnen gern | I am glad to help you | the answer to it |
| Ich danke Ihnen | thank you (formal, full) | more formal than danke |
| Das gehört mir | that belongs to me | |
| Das passt mir gut | that suits me fine | for arranging a time |
| Wie gefällt es Ihnen? | how do you like it | the question you will be asked |
| Hamburg gefällt mir | I like Hamburg | the thing is the subject |
| Es gefällt mir sehr | I like it very much | |
| Der Kaffee schmeckt mir | I like the coffee | food and drink only |
| Können Sie mir das zeigen? | can you show me that | dative person, accusative thing |
| Geben Sie mir bitte die Rechnung | please give me the bill | same shape |
| Es tut mir leid | I am sorry | fixed phrase, dative inside |

<Aside type="caution" title="Two objects in one sentence">
`Können Sie mir das zeigen?` has both cases at once - `mir` is the person (dative) and `das` is the thing (accusative). German puts them in that order: **person before thing**, dative before accusative. Same in `Geben Sie mir die Rechnung`. You do not need the rule today, only the two sentences - but notice the shape, because it is how every giving-and-showing sentence in German is built.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

Lost at the station, which is where `Können Sie mir helfen?` earns its place.

> **Ravi:** Entschuldigung, können Sie mir helfen?
> **Mann:** Ja, natürlich. Was suchen Sie?
> **Ravi:** Ich suche den Zug nach Berlin. Können Sie mir das zeigen?
> **Mann:** Gern. Gleis vier. Der Zug fährt um zehn Uhr.
> **Ravi:** Wie bitte? Noch einmal, bitte.
> **Mann:** Gleis vier. Um zehn Uhr.
> **Ravi:** Danke, ich danke Ihnen! Gefällt es Ihnen hier in Hamburg?
> **Mann:** Ja, sehr. Und Ihnen?
> **Ravi:** Es gefällt mir auch sehr. Ich bin seit einem Monat hier.
> **Mann:** Und schmeckt Ihnen der Kaffee am Bahnhof?
> **Ravi:** Nein! Der Kaffee schmeckt mir nicht.
> **Mann:** Mir auch nicht. Gute Reise!

`das Gleis` is the platform, `um zehn Uhr` is at ten o'clock - a first glance at times, which get their own day on Tag 17 - and `Gute Reise` is *have a good journey*. `Mir auch nicht` is worth stealing: *me neither*, built from nothing but a dative pronoun.

Read it aloud twice, both parts, with the reduced endings and the vowel-r. Then cover Ravi's lines and produce them from the man's cues.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Asking for help, over and over, until it stops needing thought. Stand up.

```text
Du bist eine hilfsbereite Person am Bahnhof in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger
(Tag 13, A1). Heute übe ich die Dativpronomen mir, Ihnen, ihm, ihr und
die Verben helfen, danken, gefallen, schmecken, gehören.

Sprich langsam, maximal sieben Wörter pro Satz. Frag mich mehrmals:
Wie gefällt es Ihnen in Hamburg? Schmeckt Ihnen der Kaffee? Gehört das
Ihnen? Kann ich Ihnen helfen? Passt Ihnen zehn Uhr?

Wenn ich "mich" statt "mir" sage, oder "ihn" statt "ihm", wiederhole
meinen Satz einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung, und mach weiter. Korrigiere
nichts anderes. Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
Whether you answer `Wie gefällt es Ihnen?` with a dative at all. The reflex is `Ich finde es gut`, which is correct and which you should keep - but try at least three times in the ten minutes to answer with `Es gefällt mir`, because that is the construction a German expects back and producing it once properly is worth more than understanding it ten times.
</Aside>
