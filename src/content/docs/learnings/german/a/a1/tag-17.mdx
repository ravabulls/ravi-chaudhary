Two weeks of saying `um acht` and `um neun` without ever learning the clock properly. Today it arrives, and it contains the single most expensive misunderstanding available to an English speaker learning German.

**`halb drei` does not mean half past three. It means half past two.** German counts *halfway to* the coming hour, not *half past* the one that has gone. Get this wrong and you arrive an hour late to things, which is a different category of mistake from a wrong article.

Everything else today is straightforward, and by the end of it you can make an appointment - which, with the modal verbs from the last two days, is the first genuinely transactional conversation you can hold end to end.

<Aside type="note" title="How to run this page">

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Wie spät ist es?

Two questions, both completely normal:

- **Wie spät ist es?** - literally *how late is it*
- **Wie viel Uhr ist es?** - *what time is it*

And the answer always starts `Es ist ...`.

### Die einfache Uhrzeit

| Uhrzeit | Deutsch |
|---|---|
| 1:00 | Es ist **ein** Uhr. |
| 2:00 | Es ist zwei Uhr. |
| 3:15 | Es ist **Viertel nach** drei. |
| 3:30 | Es ist **halb vier**. |
| 3:45 | Es ist **Viertel vor** vier. |
| 3:05 | Es ist fünf **nach** drei. |
| 3:55 | Es ist fünf **vor** vier. |

Note `ein Uhr`, not `eins Uhr` - the number loses its `-s` in front of `Uhr`.

### Das halb-Problem

This is the part to burn in.

| Deutsch | Wörtlich | Englisch |
|---|---|---|
| halb **zwei** | halfway to two | half past **one**, 1:30 |
| halb **drei** | halfway to three | half past **two**, 2:30 |
| halb **vier** | halfway to four | half past **three**, 3:30 |
| halb **acht** | halfway to eight | half past **seven**, 7:30 |

<Aside type="caution" title="The rule in one line">
**German names the hour it is heading towards. English names the hour it has left.** So the German number is always one higher than the English one.

`halb acht` is 7:30. If somebody says `Wir treffen uns um halb acht` and you turn up at 8:30, you have missed it by an hour. When you are unsure, say the digits instead - `um sieben Uhr dreißig` is completely acceptable German and nobody will misunderstand it. Use the digits until `halb` is genuinely automatic.
</Aside>

### Die offizielle Zeit

Timetables, appointments and anything official use the 24-hour clock, read straight off:

| Geschrieben | Gesprochen |
|---|---|
| 14:30 | vierzehn Uhr dreißig |
| 09:15 | neun Uhr fünfzehn |
| 20:45 | zwanzig Uhr fünfundvierzig |
| 18:00 | achtzehn Uhr |

This is the easier system and the one you should use for anything that matters. `Der Zug fährt um vierzehn Uhr dreißig` cannot be misread.

### Die Wochentage

All masculine, all `am` when you say *on*:

| Tag | am ... |
|---|---|
| Montag | am Montag |
| Dienstag | am Dienstag |
| Mittwoch | am Mittwoch |
| Donnerstag | am Donnerstag |
| Freitag | am Freitag |
| Samstag | am Samstag |
| Sonntag | am Sonntag |

`Mittwoch` is literally *mid-week* and does not end in `-tag`, which is the only irregularity.

### um, am, von ... bis

| Präposition | Wofür | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| **um** | a clock time | **um** acht Uhr |
| **am** | a day | **am** Montag |
| **von ... bis** | from ... to | **von** neun **bis** fünf |
| **ab** | from (onwards) | **ab** Montag |

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|

| Es ist halb acht | it is half past seven | the trap |
| Es ist Viertel nach drei | it is quarter past three | |

| Der Zug fährt um vierzehn Uhr | the train leaves at two pm | official time |
| Wann haben Sie Zeit? | when do you have time | |

## 6. Text und Hören

Booking an appointment by phone, which is the hardest real-world German task you can now attempt - no face, no gestures, and numbers flying past.

> **Praxis:** Praxis Doktor Weber, guten Tag.
> **Ravi:** Guten Tag. Ich möchte einen Termin, bitte.
> **Praxis:** Gern. Wann haben Sie Zeit?
> **Ravi:** Ich arbeite von neun bis fünf. Haben Sie am Freitag Zeit?
> **Praxis:** Am Freitag um halb sechs?
> **Ravi:** Wie bitte? Noch einmal, bitte.
> **Praxis:** Am Freitag um siebzehn Uhr dreißig.
> **Ravi:** Ah, siebzehn Uhr dreißig. Das passt mir gut.
> **Praxis:** Gut. Und wie heißen Sie?
> **Ravi:** Ravi Chaudhary. Soll ich etwas mitbringen?
> **Praxis:** Nur Ihre Karte. Bis Freitag!
> **Ravi:** Danke, bis Freitag!

The move in the middle is the one to copy: `halb sechs` was not understood, Ravi asked for a repeat, and the receptionist switched to the official 24-hour form - which was unambiguous. If you ever cannot parse a `halb`, **ask for it again and you will usually get the digits.**

`die Praxis` is a doctor's practice, `mitbringen` is to bring along, `nur` is only, `die Karte` is the card. All free.

Read it aloud twice, both parts. Then cover Ravi's lines and produce them from the receptionist's cues.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Booking three appointments, which means hearing three times spoken at speed. Stand up.

```text
Du arbeitest in einer Arztpraxis in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger (Tag 17, A1).
Heute übe ich die Uhrzeit und die Wochentage.

Ich rufe an und möchte einen Termin. Biete mir Termine an und nenne die
Zeit dabei **immer zuerst mit halb oder Viertel** - zum Beispiel "am
Dienstag um halb vier" - und erst wenn ich nachfrage, sage sie offiziell
("fünfzehn Uhr dreißig").

Mach das drei Mal mit drei verschiedenen Tagen und Zeiten. Frag mich auch:
Wann haben Sie Zeit? Von wann bis wann arbeiten Sie? Passt Ihnen das?

Sprich langsam, maximal acht Wörter pro Satz. Wenn ich eine halbe Stunde
falsch verstehe - "halb vier" als 4:30 - sage einfach die offizielle Zeit
und mach weiter. Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
Whether you can repeat a time back correctly. The habit worth building today is confirming: whenever someone gives you a time, **say it back to them before agreeing to it.** `Am Freitag um halb sechs? Also siebzehn Uhr dreißig?` It takes four seconds, it catches the `halb` error before it costs you an appointment, and Germans do it to each other constantly.
</Aside>

## 9. Auf Papier - erste Destillation

<div class="de-paper">

**Diktat.** Ten-second loop on the shadowing audio. Write six German words by hand without pausing, then correct in a second colour.

**Satz des Tages.** Write out by hand, from memory, your week: what time you get up, what time the course starts, what hours you work, which day you have time, and one appointment on a named day at a named time. Five lines, with at least two `halb` times in them. Check against section 3.

**Die Uhr zeichnen.** Draw four clock faces by hand and label them in German: 1:30, 3:15, 7:30, 8:45. Under each one write what you would say. The drawing matters because `halb` is a spatial idea - halfway to the next hour - and seeing the hand pointing down at the two while you write `halb drei` fixes it faster than any rule.

**Fehlerliste.** Week-three page. Every item the drills marked wrong today, in German only. Every `halb` error gets written twice.

**Genus-Farben.** Four new nouns: blue `der Dienstag`, `der Termin`; red `die Uhr`, `die Praxis`. The days continue to confirm the masculine rule.

**Goldlist 1 - Destillation.**

Fourteen days ago, on Tag 03, you wrote twenty-five items on a double page and closed the notebook. Today you open it for the first time.

<Steps>

1. **Read the page once, aloud, slowly.** Top to bottom. Do not test yourself, do not cover the English, do not score anything. Just read it.

2. **Cross out everything you know.** An item counts as known if the German came back without effort while you read it. Be honest but not harsh - hesitation is fine, struggle is not.

3. **Count what is left.** Write the number at the bottom of the page.

4. **Copy the survivors onto a fresh page**, renumbered, headed *Goldlist 1 - Destillation 1* with today's date. German left, English right, by hand, saying each one aloud once as you write it.

5. **Close the notebook and walk away** again. Same rule as before.

6. **Write in your calendar, 14 days from today:** *Goldlist 1 - zweite Destillation* - that is Tag 31.

</Steps>

The claim behind the method is that around a third of the list will have stayed without a single review, which means twenty-five should come down to roughly seventeen. **Whatever number you get, write it down** - because the number itself is the most useful piece of feedback in this whole course. If twenty survived, the items were too hard or the list was too long. If five survived, you have been reviewing it on the quiet and the experiment is spoiled. Around seventeen means the method is working exactly as advertised.

</div>
