**From nothing to your first real conversations.**

Four weeks, twenty-eight sessions of two hours, and at the end of it you can introduce yourself, ask for what you need, order a meal, buy a ticket, make an appointment, say what you did yesterday and give a reason for it. That is A1, and it is more than it sounds.

The method is on the [How this works](/learnings/german/) page - the nine blocks of a session, the three timed schedules, and why nothing is ever saved. This page is just the map of the four weeks.

<Aside type="note" title="Where to start">
**Tag 01.** Not Tag 03 because you already know some greetings, and not a placement test. The first week teaches the sounds of German before anything else, and every later page assumes you have the Tag 01 to Tag 07 chunks in your mouth rather than on a list.

If you genuinely have German already, the fastest honest check is the self-assessment on [Tag 28](/learnings/german/a/a1/tag-28/). If you can do what it asks, start module A2 instead.
</Aside>

## What A1 actually gets you

The Council of Europe's description is modest and worth reading plainly: at A1 you can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases, introduce yourself and others, ask and answer questions about personal details, and interact in a simple way provided the other person talks slowly and clearly and is prepared to help.

That last clause is the honest part. **A1 works when the other person is cooperating.** Twenty-eight days gets you there, and it is the point at which German stops being a wall and starts being a slow conversation.

What A1 does **not** get you: a conversation between two Germans talking to each other, a phone call, a newspaper, or anything abstract. Those arrive in [A2](/learnings/german/a/a2/) and B1.

<Aside type="tip" title="The one habit that decides whether this works">
**Say every answer out loud before you type it**, including the ones the page asks you to type, and write the paper block from memory rather than copying it off the screen.

Those two habits are the whole difference between twenty-eight days that produce German and twenty-eight days that produce the feeling of having studied German. Everything else on these pages is scaffolding around them.
</Aside>
