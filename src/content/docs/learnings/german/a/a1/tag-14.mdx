*Tag 14 von 60 - trennbare Verben, die Satzklammer, der Tagesablauf. Zwei Stunden.*

---

On Tag 09 you learned `Rufen Sie mich **an**` and `Ich hole Sie **ab**` as unanalysable blocks, with a promise that the rule behind them would arrive today. Here it is, and it is one of the best-value rules in German: learn it once and a few hundred verbs become available, because German builds new verbs by sticking a preposition on the front of an old one.

`stehen` is to stand. `**auf**stehen` is to get up. `kaufen` is to buy. `**ein**kaufen` is to go shopping. `kommen` is to come. `**mit**kommen` is to come along. You are not learning new verbs so much as learning to recognise that you already half-know them.

The catch is where the prefix goes when you actually use it: **it detaches and goes to the end of the sentence.** That produces the shape that defines German word order, and the shape you will meet again on Tag 15, Tag 18 and Tag 25 - the **Satzklammer**, the sentence bracket.

Two hours, top to bottom, every answer **out loud** before you type it. Nothing is saved. Today's drills are longer than usual because the only way to get the bracket into your mouth is volume. Section 10 closes week two with what you can now actually do - read it, because the honest comparison with Tag 07 is the point of a fortnight.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Verben, die auseinanderbrechen

A separable verb has two parts: a **prefix** that carries most of the meaning, and a **stem** that is an ordinary verb you already know.

| Verb | Teile | Bedeutung |
|---|---|---|
| **auf**stehen | auf + stehen | to get up |
| **ein**kaufen | ein + kaufen | to go shopping |
| **an**rufen | an + rufen | to call (by phone) |
| **ab**holen | ab + holen | to pick up |
| **mit**kommen | mit + kommen | to come along |
| **an**fangen | an + fangen | to begin |
| **auf**hören | auf + hören | to stop |
| **zu**rückkommen | zurück + kommen | to come back |

In the dictionary they are written as one word. In a sentence they are not.

### Die Satzklammer

Here is the whole rule:

**The conjugated verb goes in position two, as always. The prefix goes to the very end of the sentence. Everything else sits between them.**

| Position 2 | ...alles andere... | Ende |
|---|---|---|
| Ich **stehe** | um sieben Uhr | **auf**. |
| Ich **kaufe** | heute | **ein**. |
| Ich **rufe** | Sie heute Abend | **an**. |
| Ich **hole** | Sie am Bahnhof | **ab**. |
| **Kommen** Sie | morgen | **mit**? |
| Der Kurs **fängt** | um neun Uhr | **an**. |

The two halves of the verb are the two arms of a bracket, and the content of the sentence sits inside it. That is why German speakers can hold a long sentence open for a remarkably long time before the final word lands - and why you cannot know what a German sentence means until it finishes.

<Aside type="tip" title="The practical consequence for listening">
This is the single most important listening fact in German. `Ich rufe Sie...` could still turn into `an` (I will call you), `ab` (I will cancel), or nothing at all. **Wait for the end of the sentence.** English speakers habitually decide what a sentence means halfway through and stop listening, which works in English and fails in German. Train yourself to hold on until the last word.
</Aside>

### Der Tagesablauf

The vocabulary this grammar is best practised on, because a daily routine is nothing but separable verbs and times:

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf | I get up at seven |
| Ich frühstücke | I have breakfast |
| Ich fahre zur Uni | I go to the university |
| Der Kurs fängt um neun an | the course starts at nine |
| Mittags esse ich etwas | at midday I eat something |
| Nachmittags lerne ich | in the afternoon I study |
| Abends koche ich | in the evening I cook |
| Ich gehe um elf ins Bett | I go to bed at eleven |

`um ... Uhr` is enough time vocabulary for today; the full clock arrives on Tag 17.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf | I get up at seven | prefix at the end |
| Wann stehen Sie auf? | when do you get up | question word, verb second, prefix last |
| Ich kaufe heute ein | I am going shopping today | |
| Ich rufe Sie morgen an | I will call you tomorrow | morgen is tomorrow |
| Ich hole Sie um acht ab | I will pick you up at eight | |
| Kommen Sie mit? | are you coming along | the invitation |
| Ja, ich komme mit | yes, I am coming along | the answer |
| Der Kurs fängt um neun an | the course starts at nine | |
| Wann fängt der Kurs an? | when does the course start | |
| Ich höre um fünf auf | I stop at five | aufhören, to stop |
| Ich komme am Montag zurück | I am coming back on Monday | am + day |
| Was machen Sie heute Abend? | what are you doing this evening | the small-talk opener |

<Aside type="caution" title="morgen and Morgen">
`morgen` with a small m is **tomorrow**. `der Morgen` with a capital M is **the morning**. And `morgen Morgen` is not a thing - tomorrow morning is `morgen früh`. German is unhelpful here and the only defence is to notice the capital letter, which is one more reason the Tag 03 rule about capitalising nouns is worth keeping.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

Arranging to meet, which is what separable verbs are for.

> **Anna:** Hallo Ravi! Was machen Sie heute Abend?
> **Ravi:** Heute Abend? Ich lerne Deutsch. Und Sie?
> **Anna:** Ich kaufe heute ein. Kommen Sie mit?
> **Ravi:** Ja, gern. Wann?
> **Anna:** Um sechs. Ich hole Sie am Bahnhof ab.
> **Ravi:** Das passt mir gut. Ich komme mit dem Bus.
> **Anna:** Gut. Und wann stehen Sie morgen auf?
> **Ravi:** Um sieben. Der Kurs fängt um neun an.
> **Anna:** Und wann hören Sie auf?
> **Ravi:** Um fünf. Dann fahre ich nach Hause.
> **Anna:** Ich rufe Sie morgen an, ja?
> **Ravi:** Gern. Ich komme am Montag zurück.
> **Anna:** Alles klar. Bis heute Abend!

`Alles klar` is *all clear, fine*, and `Bis heute Abend` is *see you this evening* - both free and both very common.

Read it aloud twice, both parts. Then do the thing today's grammar demands: cover Ravi's lines and produce them, **and do not start speaking until Anna's sentence has finished.** If you answer before the prefix arrives you will answer the wrong question.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Making a plan, which forces the bracket in every second sentence. Stand up.

```text
Du bist Anna, eine Bekannte aus Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger (Tag 14, A1).
Heute übe ich trennbare Verben: aufstehen, einkaufen, anrufen, abholen,
mitkommen, anfangen, aufhören, zurückkommen.

Wir verabreden uns für heute Abend. Frag mich: Was machen Sie heute Abend?
Kommen Sie mit? Wann stehen Sie auf? Wann fängt der Kurs an? Wann hören
Sie auf? Soll ich Sie abholen?

Sprich langsam, maximal acht Wörter pro Satz. Wenn ich das Präfix
vergesse - "Ich stehe um sieben Uhr" ohne "auf" - wiederhole meinen Satz
einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung, und mach weiter. Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
The missing prefix. It is the error of the day, every day, for about a week: you will build a perfectly good sentence and simply stop before the last word, because in English the preposition came early and you have already said it in your head. The fix is physical rather than intellectual - **do not take a breath until the prefix is out.** Treat `Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf` as one word with a long middle.
</Aside>

What you can actually do now that you could not on Tag 07: order a full meal and pay for it, ask a stranger for help and understand the directions, say how you travel and how long you have been here, arrange to meet someone at a specific time, say what you like and what you do not, apologise, and - the quiet one - **wait for the end of a German sentence before deciding what it means.** That last habit is worth more than any twelve chunks on the list.

<Aside type="caution" title="One honest line about week three">
Week two was hard because of the cases. Week three is hard for the opposite reason: almost nothing in it is difficult to understand, and all of it has to become fast. Modal verbs, the clock, and the past tense are each a single clear rule - but they are the rules you use in every second sentence, so a half-second hesitation on any of them makes your German sound like work rather than speech.

The drill scores should go **up** this week. If they do not, the problem is week two's cases leaking rather than week three's material, and the honest fix is to go back to the Tag 12 table rather than pushing forward.
</Aside>
