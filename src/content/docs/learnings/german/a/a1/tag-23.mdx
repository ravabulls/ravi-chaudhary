*Tag 23 von 60 - der Imperativ, links und rechts, den Weg erklären. Zwei Stunden.*

You have been asking `Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?` since Tag 11 and understanding roughly half of the answer. Today you learn the other half - both how to give directions yourself, and more importantly how to **parse them when they come back at you fast**.

The grammar is the easiest of the whole course. To tell someone politely to do something, take the `Sie` form of the verb and put it first: `Sie gehen` becomes `**Gehen Sie!**`. That is the entire rule, and you have been using it unknowingly since Tag 07 - `Sprechen Sie bitte langsam`, `Nehmen Sie die Straße`, `Rufen Sie mich an`. All imperatives.

<Aside type="note" title="How to run this page">
Two hours, top to bottom, every answer **out loud** before you type it. Nothing is saved. Today's real difficulty is listening rather than producing: section 6 contains a set of directions at natural length and the exercise is to follow them, not to translate them. Section 9 has the **third Goldlist distillation** - the page from Tag 09.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Der Imperativ mit Sie

**Take the `Sie` form, move the verb to the front, keep the `Sie`.**

| Aussage | Imperativ |
|---|---|
| Sie gehen | **Gehen Sie!** |
| Sie nehmen | **Nehmen Sie!** |
| Sie warten | **Warten Sie!** |
| Sie kommen | **Kommen Sie!** |
| Sie fahren | **Fahren Sie!** |
| Sie sprechen | **Sprechen Sie!** |

No exceptions, no vowel changes, nothing to learn. And the yes-no question has the same shape - `Gehen Sie?` versus `Gehen Sie!` - so **the melody is doing the work**: a question rises, an imperative falls. That is the Tag 07 melody lesson turning out to be grammar.

<Aside type="tip" title="Always add bitte">
A bare `Gehen Sie!` is an order. `Gehen Sie bitte...` is a request, and in German `bitte` is doing most of the politeness work that English does with *could you* and *would you mind*.

Put it after the `Sie`: `Nehmen Sie bitte die erste Straße.` `Warten Sie bitte hier.` `Sprechen Sie bitte langsam` - which you have been saying correctly since Tag 07.
</Aside>

### Die du-Form, kurz

You will hear this constantly even if you do not use it yet. The informal imperative is the **bare stem with no ending and no pronoun**:

| Sie-Form | du-Form |
|---|---|
| Gehen Sie! | **Geh!** |
| Kommen Sie! | **Komm!** |
| Warten Sie! | **Warte!** |
| Nehmen Sie! | **Nimm!** |

Recognise them; keep using `Sie` yourself with anyone you have not been invited to address informally.

## 6. Text und Hören

A full set of directions at realistic length. **Read the woman's long answer once and try to follow it without translating** - then check yourself against the summary underneath.

> **Ravi:** Entschuldigung, können Sie mir helfen? Ich suche die Post.
> **Frau:** Die Post? Ja, gern. Gehen Sie hier geradeaus, bis zur Ampel.
> **Ravi:** Bis zur Ampel, ja.
> **Frau:** An der Ampel gehen Sie nach rechts. Dann nehmen Sie die zweite Straße links.
> **Ravi:** Die zweite Straße links. Und dann?
> **Frau:** Dann sehen Sie einen Supermarkt. Die Post ist gegenüber dem Supermarkt.
> **Ravi:** Gegenüber dem Supermarkt. Ist es weit?
> **Frau:** Nein, es sind ungefähr sieben Minuten.
> **Ravi:** Und wann schließt die Post?
> **Frau:** Um sechs, glaube ich. Gehen Sie schnell!
> **Ravi:** Vielen Dank! Das war sehr nett.
> **Frau:** Gern. Alles Gute!

The route, in four steps: **straight to the traffic light, right, second street on the left, opposite the supermarket.** If you got those four from the German without going through English, your listening has moved a long way in three weeks.

The thing Ravi does right, and which you should copy whenever anyone gives you directions: **he repeats each step back.** `Bis zur Ampel, ja.` `Die zweite Straße links.` It buys thinking time, it confirms you heard correctly, and it makes the other person slow down naturally without you having to ask.

`der Supermarkt`, `schließen` to close, `glaube ich` I think, `schnell` fast, `Alles Gute` all the best. All free.

Read it aloud twice, both parts. Then cover the woman's lines and give the directions yourself from the four-step summary.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Both directions, literally. Stand up.

```text
Du bist eine Person auf der Straße in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger
(Tag 23, A1). Heute übe ich den Imperativ und die Wegbeschreibung.

Erster Teil: Ich frage dich dreimal nach dem Weg - zur Post, zum Bahnhof,
zum Supermarkt. Gib mir jedes Mal eine Wegbeschreibung mit MINDESTENS
drei Schritten und benutze dabei: geradeaus, nach links, nach rechts,
bis zur Ampel, an der Kreuzung, die erste oder zweite Straße, gegenüber.
Sprich langsam, aber mach die Beschreibung wirklich dreischrittig.

Zweiter Teil: Dann fragst DU mich nach dem Weg, dreimal, und ich erkläre
es dir. Frag nach: dem Bahnhof, der Uni, einem Café.

Wenn ich einen Imperativ falsch bilde - "Sie gehen geradeaus" statt
"Gehen Sie geradeaus" - wiederhole meinen Satz einfach richtig, ohne
Erklärung. Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
Whether you repeat each step back. It is the single most useful conversational habit in this entire course and today is the day to install it, because directions are the one situation where getting it wrong has an immediate physical cost.

Say the step back in German, short: `Bis zur Ampel.` `Zweite Straße links.` Then wait. You will find the other person automatically gives you the next step more slowly - and you will have understood a four-step route in a language you have been learning for three weeks.
</Aside>

## 9. Auf Papier - dritte Destillation

<div class="de-paper">

**Diktat.** Ten-second loop on the shadowing audio. Write six German words by hand without pausing, then correct in a second colour.

**Satz des Tages.** Write out by hand, from memory, the route from your front door to the nearest station or bus stop, in German, in five imperative steps as if you were telling a stranger. Five lines, every one starting with a verb. Then walk it in your head and check the steps are in the right order - this is the one `Satz des Tages` with a factual right answer.

**Die Richtungs-Karte.** Draw a simple street map by hand: a straight road, a traffic light, two side streets, a supermarket and a post office. Then write the four-step route from section 6 alongside it in German. Keep it - on Tag 27 you will use the same map to give directions under time pressure.

**Fehlerliste.** Week-four page. Every item the drills marked wrong today, in German only. Every imperative with the verb in the wrong place gets written twice.

**Genus-Farben.** Five new nouns: red `die Ampel`, `die Kreuzung`, `die Post`, `die Minute`; blue `der Supermarkt`. Note `die Post` is feminine and so is `die Ampel` - both are things you will ask about, so they are worth being sure of.

**Goldlist 3 - Destillation.**

Fourteen days ago, on Tag 09, you wrote twenty-five items and closed the notebook. Today you open it.

<Steps>

1. **Read the page once, aloud, slowly.** No testing, no covering, no scoring.

2. **Cross out everything you know** - known meaning the German came back without effort as you read it.

3. **Count what is left** and write the number at the bottom.

4. **Copy the survivors onto a fresh page**, renumbered, headed *Goldlist 3 - Destillation 1* with today's date. By hand, each one said aloud once.

5. **Close the notebook and walk away.**

6. **Write in your calendar, 14 days from today:** *Goldlist 3 - zweite Destillation* - that is Tag 37.

</Steps>

Third distillation, third number. Write all three side by side somewhere: Tag 17, Tag 20, Tag 23. If the survival rate is steady at around two thirds crossed out, the method is stable and you can stop checking. If it is climbing, your lists are getting easier - make the next one harder.
