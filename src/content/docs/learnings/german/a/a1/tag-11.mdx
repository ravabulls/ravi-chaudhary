*Tag 11 von 60 - der Dativ, mit bei nach von zu aus seit, zum und zur. Zwei Stunden.*

---

Two structural things change today. The first is that **the Aussprache block is gone** - from now on section 2 is shadowing, which means ten minutes of copying a real voice instead of studying rules about sounds. You have met every German sound; what is left is a motor skill, and motor skills are trained by imitation.

The second is the **dative**, the third of German's four cases and the last one you need at A1. And once again you have been using it for a week without being told. `Wie geht es **Ihnen**?` from Tag 02 - dative. `Ich studiere an **der** TUHH` from Tag 05 - dative. `Ich lerne seit zwei **Monaten**` from Tag 07 - dative, including the strange `-n` on the end of the plural. Three sentences you can already say, all of them built on grammar you meet properly today.

<Aside type="note" title="How to run this page">
Two hours, top to bottom, every answer **out loud** before you type it. Nothing is saved. The dative is the point where learners start writing the article tables out on paper, and they are right to - section 9 has you do it. Expect today to be the slowest page of week two.
</Aside>

From today this is where the listening work lives. The method does not change for the rest of the course, so it is worth reading once properly.

**Und dann Hören, fünf Minuten.** Same clip, different job, and this is the half that self-study usually skips.

Play the whole forty seconds **three times**, without stopping and without reading anything on this page.

1. **First pass - words.** Write down every German word you catch. Three is a result. Nine is a good day.
2. **Second pass - one sentence.** Write down one complete sentence, even if half of it is a guess.
3. **Third pass - check yourself.** Listen again and correct what you wrote.

**There is no answer key, deliberately.** No transcript is printed here and you are not meant to go hunting for one. A transcript would spare you the exact thing being trained: holding unfamiliar German in your head long enough to make sense of it. The clip never changes, so the only thing that can improve between today and the last day is you.

<Aside type="tip" title="Why ten passes over one stretch">
The instinct is to shadow a long passage once, because it feels like more work. It is less. The first three passes are spent working out where the words are; the gains start around pass five, when your mouth stops lagging and begins anticipating. One ten-second stretch done ten times produces a measurable change in your own rhythm. Ten different stretches done once each produce nothing except the feeling of having practised.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Der dritte Fall

You now know two cases. The dative is the third, and the easiest way to hold all three apart is by the question each one answers:

| Fall | Frage | Rolle |
|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | **Wer?** | who is doing it |
| Akkusativ | **Wen? Was?** | what it is being done to |
| Dativ | **Wem?** | who it is being done **for** or **to** |

`Ich gebe **dem Mann** das Buch` - I give the book to the man. The book is accusative (the thing given), the man is dative (the person it goes to).

But at A1 you will meet the dative far more often for a different reason: **certain prepositions simply demand it**, regardless of meaning. That is today's real work, and it is memorisation rather than logic.

### Die Dativ-Tabelle

| Genus | Nominativ | Akkusativ | **Dativ** |
|---|---|---|---|
| maskulin | <span class="de-der">der</span> | <span class="de-der">den</span> | <span class="de-der">**dem**</span> |
| feminin | <span class="de-die">die</span> | <span class="de-die">die</span> | <span class="de-die">**der**</span> |
| neutral | <span class="de-das">das</span> | <span class="de-das">das</span> | <span class="de-das">**dem**</span> |
| Plural | die | die | **den** + Nomen**n** |

And the indefinite article:

| Genus | Nominativ | Akkusativ | **Dativ** |
|---|---|---|---|
| maskulin | ein | einen | **einem** |
| feminin | eine | eine | **einer** |
| neutral | ein | ein | **einem** |

<Aside type="caution" title="The trap in this table">
**`der` means two completely different things.** It is the masculine nominative - `der Mann` - and it is the feminine dative - `mit der Frau`. There is no way around this; you simply have to know which slot you are in. The clue is almost always a preposition sitting in front of it: after `mit`, `bei`, `nach`, `von`, `zu`, `aus` or `seit`, a `der` is feminine dative, never masculine nominative.

This is why `Ich studiere an **der** TUHH` works: `die TUHH` is feminine, `an` is taking the dative, so `die` becomes `der`.
</Aside>

### Die sieben Präpositionen

These seven **always** take the dative. No exceptions, no thinking required. Learn them as a chant - most German courses teach them in a fixed order for exactly that reason:

**mit · bei · nach · von · zu · aus · seit**

| Präposition | Bedeutung | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| mit | with / by (transport) | Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus. |
| bei | at (a person or place of work) | Ich arbeite **bei** Siemens. |
| nach | to (a city or country) / after | Ich fahre **nach** Hamburg. |
| von | from / of | Ich komme **von der** Arbeit. |
| zu | to (a person or a place) | Ich gehe **zum** Bahnhof. |
| aus | out of / from (origin) | Ich komme **aus** Indien. |
| seit | since / for (time) | Ich lerne **seit einem** Monat. |

### Die Verschmelzungen

German contracts the commonest of these, and the contracted form is the normal one - using the long form sounds stilted:

| Lang | Kurz |
|---|---|
| zu dem | **zum** |
| zu der | **zur** |
| bei dem | **beim** |
| von dem | **vom** |

`Ich gehe zu dem Bahnhof` is understandable and nobody says it. `Ich gehe **zum** Bahnhof` is what you want.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Ich fahre mit dem Bus | I go by bus | mit + Dativ, every time |
| Ich fahre mit der Bahn | I go by train | die Bahn, so der |
| Ich gehe zum Bahnhof | I am going to the station | zu dem, contracted |
| Ich gehe zur Arbeit | I am going to work | zu der, contracted |
| Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof? | how do I get to the station | the question of the day |
| Ich wohne bei einer Familie | I live with a family | bei + Dativ |
| Ich komme von der Arbeit | I am coming from work | |
| Ich fahre nach Berlin | I am going to Berlin | nach + city, no article |
| Ich bin seit einem Monat hier | I have been here for a month | seit + Dativ, present tense |
| Nach der Arbeit habe ich Zeit | after work I have time | nach also means after |
| Ich gehe nach Hause | I am going home | fixed phrase, no article |
| Ich bin zu Hause | I am at home | fixed phrase, note zu not nach |

<Aside type="caution" title="nach Hause and zu Hause">
Two fixed expressions that break every rule and cannot be reasoned out. **`nach Hause`** is *homewards*, used with movement. **`zu Hause`** is *at home*, used when you are already there. Both keep the old `-e` ending nobody else uses any more. Learn them as two unanalysable blocks and do not try to make them fit the table.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

Asking for directions, which is the single most likely reason you will need the dative in your first month.

> **Ravi:** Entschuldigung, darf ich Sie etwas fragen?
> **Frau:** Ja, natürlich.
> **Ravi:** Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?
> **Frau:** Zum Bahnhof? Nehmen Sie den Bus. Der Bus fährt zum Bahnhof.
> **Ravi:** Wie bitte? Sprechen Sie bitte langsam.
> **Frau:** Sie fahren mit dem Bus. Nummer vier.
> **Ravi:** Mit dem Bus, Nummer vier. Und wie lange?
> **Frau:** Zehn Minuten. Kommen Sie von der Arbeit?
> **Ravi:** Nein, ich komme von der Uni. Ich studiere an der TUHH.
> **Frau:** Ah, gut. Sind Sie lange in Hamburg?
> **Ravi:** Ich bin seit einem Monat hier. Jetzt fahre ich nach Hause.
> **Frau:** Schönen Tag noch!

`Wie lange?` is *how long*, `die Uni` is the everyday short form of `die Universität`, and `die Nummer` is free. Everything else is from today or earlier.

Read it aloud twice, both parts, reducing the endings as in Tag 09: `Nehm'n Sie 'n Bus`, `Komm'n Sie von der Arbeit`. Then cover Ravi's lines and produce them from the woman's cues.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Directions, repeatedly, until the dative articles stop needing thought. Stand up.

```text
Du bist eine Person auf der Straße in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger
(Tag 11, A1). Heute übe ich den Dativ und die Präpositionen
mit, bei, nach, von, zu, aus, seit.

Ich frage dich nach dem Weg. Antworte kurz und langsam, maximal sieben
Wörter pro Satz, und benutze dabei immer eine Präposition mit Dativ:
"Nehmen Sie den Bus", "Fahren Sie mit der Bahn", "Gehen Sie zum Bahnhof",
"Das ist bei der Post".

Frag mich auch: Wie kommen Sie zur Arbeit? Seit wann sind Sie in Hamburg?
Wo wohnen Sie? Wenn ich einen falschen Dativartikel benutze - "mit der Bus"
statt "mit dem Bus" - wiederhole meinen Satz einfach richtig, ohne
Erklärung, und mach weiter. Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
`dem` versus `der`. These two are the whole dative at A1 and they are one syllable apart, which means under pressure you will produce whichever one came out last time. The useful trick is to say the gender to yourself before the noun - *der Bus, so dem Bus* - and to accept that for the first week this is slow. It gets automatic through the article, not through the rule.
</Aside>
