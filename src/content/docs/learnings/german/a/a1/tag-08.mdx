*Tag 08 von 60 - das deutsche R, der Akkusativ, den und einen, die Verben mit Objekt. Zwei Stunden.*

Week one worked because every sentence had a fixed shape you could swallow whole. Today that stops, and it stops with the single most important piece of grammar in beginner German: **the case system**. You have already been using it without knowing - on Tag 04 you learned `Einen Kaffee, bitte` and nobody explained why it was not `Ein Kaffee`. Today you find out.

The idea is simple even though the endings are not. German marks **what a noun is doing in the sentence** by changing the word in front of it. The thing doing the action keeps its dictionary article - that is the **Nominativ**, everything you have had so far. The thing having the action done to it switches to the **Akkusativ**.

And here is the piece of luck that makes today survivable: **only the masculine changes.** Feminine, neuter and plural look exactly the same as they did yesterday. One gender out of three, one ending to learn. That is the whole of the accusative.

<Aside type="note" title="How to run this page">
Two hours, top to bottom, every answer **out loud** before you type it. Nothing is saved. Today will feel slower than any day last week and your drill scores will drop - that is the material, not you. If you get `den` and `einen` right on masculine nouns and leave everything else alone, the day has done its job.
</Aside>

## 2. Aussprache - das deutsche R

The last genuinely new sound, and the one that most obviously marks a foreigner. English r is made with the tongue curled back in the middle of the mouth. German r is made **at the back of the throat**, in the same place as the `ach-Laut` from Tag 03 - and in many positions it is barely a consonant at all.

Three positions, three behaviours:

| Position | Was passiert | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| am Wortanfang | back-of-throat r, clearly audible | **r**ot, **R**eise, **r**ichtig |
| nach einem Konsonanten | same back r, lighter | d**r**ei, b**r**auchen, g**r**oß |
| am Wortende oder vor einem Konsonanten | **becomes a vowel**, like a short *uh* | de**r**, Bie**r**, Uh**r**, a**r**beiten |

That third row is the one that transforms your accent. `der` does not end in an r sound at all - a German says something close to *dea*. `Bier` is *Bia*. `Uhr` is *Ooa*. If you pronounce the r in `der Zug` the way you would in English, it is the single loudest thing in the sentence.

Say each pair aloud. Left column has a real r, right column has the vowel-r:

| Echtes R | Vokal-R |
|---|---|
| rot | der |
| richtig | Bier |
| Reise | Uhr |
| drei | Tür |
| braucht | Wasser |
| grün | Vater |

<Aside type="caution" title="Do not over-correct">
Plenty of learners hear *back of the throat* and produce a heavy rolled or gargled r in every position. That is worse than an English r, because now it is loud **and** wrong. The useful order is: first get the vowel-r right at the ends of words, which is most of the r sounds you will ever say, and let the initial r stay slightly English for now. Nobody will notice the second one; everybody notices the first.
</Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Nominativ und Akkusativ

Every German sentence with an object has two nouns doing two different jobs:

| Rolle | Frage | Fall |
|---|---|---|
| wer oder was **tut** es | **Wer?** | Nominativ |
| wen oder was **trifft** es | **Wen?** oder **Was?** | Akkusativ |

Take `Ich trinke den Kaffee`. Who drinks? *Ich* - nominative. What is drunk? *den Kaffee* - accusative. The coffee is not doing anything; it is being done to.

### Die Tabelle, die du heute lernst

This is the whole of today's grammar in six cells:

| Genus | Nominativ | Akkusativ |
|---|---|---|
| maskulin | <span class="de-der">der</span> Kaffee | <span class="de-der">**den**</span> Kaffee |
| feminin | <span class="de-die">die</span> Milch | <span class="de-die">die</span> Milch |
| neutral | <span class="de-das">das</span> Wasser | <span class="de-das">das</span> Wasser |

And the same for the indefinite article:

| Genus | Nominativ | Akkusativ |
|---|---|---|
| maskulin | <span class="de-der">ein</span> Kaffee | <span class="de-der">**einen**</span> Kaffee |
| feminin | <span class="de-die">eine</span> Frage | <span class="de-die">eine</span> Frage |
| neutral | <span class="de-das">ein</span> Problem | <span class="de-das">ein</span> Problem |

<Aside type="tip" title="The only thing to memorise today">
**Masculine takes -n.** `der` becomes `den`, `ein` becomes `einen`, `kein` becomes `keinen`. Feminine and neuter do not move at all. If you remember nothing else from this page, remember that one line - it is around ninety percent of the accusative at A1.
</Aside>

Two things you already know suddenly make sense:

- `Einen Kaffee, bitte` from Tag 04. You were ordering a coffee - the coffee is the thing being ordered, so it is accusative, so masculine `ein` grew an `-en`. You have been using the accusative correctly since day four.
- `Ich mache einen MBA` from Tag 05. Same reason. `der MBA` is masculine, and you are *doing* it, so `einen`.

### Verben, die ein Objekt brauchen

Not every verb takes an object. `Ich wohne` is complete; `Ich trinke` is not - you have to say what. Today's verbs all need one:

| Verb | Beispiel |
|---|---|
| trinken | Ich trinke <span class="de-der">einen</span> Kaffee. |
| essen | Ich esse <span class="de-das">ein</span> Brot. |
| nehmen | Ich nehme <span class="de-der">den</span> Zug. |
| brauchen | Ich brauche <span class="de-die">die</span> Adresse. |
| kaufen | Ich kaufe <span class="de-das">das</span> Buch. |
| suchen | Ich suche <span class="de-der">den</span> Bahnhof. |

Note `essen` and `nehmen` change their vowel in the `du` and `er/sie/es` forms - `du isst`, `er isst`, `du nimmst`, `er nimmt`. You only need the `ich` and `Sie` forms today.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

Read each one aloud twice before the drill.

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Ich trinke einen Kaffee | I am drinking a coffee | masculine, so einen |
| Ich nehme den Zug | I am taking the train | der Zug becomes den Zug |
| Ich suche den Bahnhof | I am looking for the station | the question you will actually ask |
| Ich brauche die Adresse | I need the address | feminine, nothing changes |
| Ich kaufe das Buch | I am buying the book | neuter, nothing changes |
| Ich esse ein Brot | I am eating a bread roll | neuter, so plain ein |
| Haben Sie einen Moment? | do you have a moment | der Moment, so einen |
| Ich habe keinen Hunger | I am not hungry | literally: I have no hunger |
| Ich möchte einen Tee | I would like a tea | möchte is the polite want |
| Nehmen Sie die Straße | take the street | directions, feminine |
| Ich verstehe das Wort nicht | I do not understand the word | neuter object, nicht at the end |
| Können Sie das wiederholen? | can you repeat that | the other rescue sentence |

<Aside type="caution" title="keinen, not kein">
`kein` is just `ein` with a k, so it inherits the same ending. `Ich habe **keinen** Hunger` - `der Hunger` is masculine and it is the object, so `keinen`. Compare `Ich habe **keine** Zeit` from Tag 04: `die Zeit` is feminine, so nothing changes. Those two sentences are the pair to drill until they are automatic.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

A café, and this time Ravi is ordering rather than introducing himself. Every object in the dialogue is in the accusative - find all seven before you read it aloud.

> **Kellner:** Guten Tag! Was möchten Sie?
> **Ravi:** Guten Tag. Ich möchte einen Kaffee, bitte.
> **Kellner:** Einen Kaffee. Möchten Sie auch ein Brot?
> **Ravi:** Nein danke, ich habe keinen Hunger. Was kostet der Kaffee?
> **Kellner:** Drei Euro.
> **Ravi:** Gut. Entschuldigung, ich suche den Bahnhof. Haben Sie einen Moment?
> **Kellner:** Ja, genau. Nehmen Sie die Straße und dann den Bus.
> **Ravi:** Wie bitte? Ich verstehe das Wort nicht. Können Sie das wiederholen?
> **Kellner:** Nehmen Sie die Straße, dann den Bus.
> **Ravi:** Danke! Ich nehme den Bus.

The seven objects: `einen Kaffee`, `einen Kaffee`, `ein Brot`, `keinen Hunger`, `den Bahnhof`, `einen Moment`, `die Straße`, `den Bus`, `das Wort`, `den Bus`. More than seven, in fact - which is the point. Almost every sentence that does anything has one.

Read it aloud twice, both parts, with the melody from Tag 07: rising on `Möchten Sie auch ein Brot?`, falling everywhere else. Then cover Ravi's lines and produce them from the waiter's cues.

**Und dann Hören, fünf Minuten.** Same clip, different job, and this is the half that self-study usually skips.

Play the whole forty seconds **three times**, without stopping and without reading anything on this page.

1. **First pass - words.** Write down every German word you catch. Three is a result. Nine is a good day.
2. **Second pass - one sentence.** Write down one complete sentence, even if half of it is a guess.
3. **Third pass - check yourself.** Listen again and correct what you wrote.

**There is no answer key, deliberately.** No transcript is printed here and you are not meant to go hunting for one. A transcript would spare you the exact thing being trained: holding unfamiliar German in your head long enough to make sense of it. The clip never changes, so the only thing that can improve between today and the last day is you.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

A café roleplay, which is the situation today's grammar was built for. Stand up.

```text
Du bist Kellner oder Kellnerin in einem Café in Hamburg. Ich bin Anfänger
(Tag 8, A1). Heute übe ich den Akkusativ.

Sprich langsam, maximal sechs Wörter pro Satz, und nur mit diesen Wörtern:
Guten Tag, Was möchten Sie, Möchten Sie auch, Ja genau, Nein danke,
Das kostet, Euro, Entschuldigung, Nehmen Sie, die Straße, der Bus,
Kaffee, Tee, Wasser, Milch, Brot, Zeit, Hunger, Moment, Bahnhof.

Frag mich mindestens fünfmal, was ich möchte oder was ich nehme, damit ich
den Akkusativ üben muss. Wenn ich "ein Kaffee" statt "einen Kaffee" sage,
wiederhole meinen Satz einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung, und mach weiter.
Korrigiere nichts anderes.
Fang jetzt an und begrüße mich.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
One thing only: the `-n` on masculine objects. You will say `Ich möchte ein Kaffee` at least twice in ten minutes, because your mouth learned `ein Kaffee` as a unit on Tag 03 before it learned the rule. Do not stop to apologise when it happens - say the sentence again correctly and carry on. The repair is the practice.
</Aside>

**Diktat.** Set a ten-second loop on the shadowing audio and let it run. Write down any six German words by hand without pausing, then check and correct them in a second colour. Today, mark every `r` you wrote and decide whether it is a real r or a vowel-r.

**Satz des Tages.** Write out by hand, from memory, a six-line café order: greet, order a coffee, decline the bread because you are not hungry, ask what it costs, ask for the station, and thank them. Then check it against section 6 and mark every difference.

**Die Akkusativ-Tabelle.** Copy the six-cell table from section 3 by hand, twice, with the masculine row in a different colour from the other two. You are drawing attention to the only part that moves. Keep this page - it gets a dative column added to it on Tag 11.

**Fehlerliste.** The week-two page you started on Tag 06. Every item the drills marked wrong today, written correctly, in German only. Any wrong article gets written twice, not once.

**Genus-Farben.** Six new nouns today: `der Bus`, `der Moment`, `der Hunger`, `der Tee` all blue; `die Adresse` red; `das Brot`, `das Buch`, `das Wort` green. Four masculines in one day is not an accident - they are there because masculines are the only ones the accusative touches.

**Goldlist.** Not today. Goldlist 2 from Tag 06 is sitting in the notebook with a date on it - leave it completely alone until Tag 20. The next Goldlist page is Tag 09, tomorrow.
