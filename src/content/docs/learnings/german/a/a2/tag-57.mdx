Week nine, and the last four days are the handover rather than new grammar.

Today is the **exam**, and the reason it gets a page is narrow and practical: an A2 exam does not test whether you can speak German. It tests whether you can do **four specific things in four specific shapes under a clock**, and people who speak better German than the format expects still lose marks by not knowing the shapes.

So this page is about the shapes. The German on it you already have.

<Aside type="note" title="How to run this page">
Two hours. Section 3 describes the formats, section 4 drills the Redemittel the speaking exam needs, and section 8 is a full mock oral - which is the only part of today that really matters. Section 9 has Goldlist 19.
</Aside>

**Und dann Hören, fünf Minuten.** Same clip, different job, and this is the half that self-study usually skips.

Play the whole forty seconds **three times**, without stopping and without reading anything on this page.

1. **First pass - words.** Write down every German word you catch. Three is a result. Nine is a good day.
2. **Second pass - one sentence.** Write down one complete sentence, even if half of it is a guess.
3. **Third pass - check yourself.** Listen again and correct what you wrote.

**There is no answer key, deliberately.** No transcript is printed here and you are not meant to go hunting for one. A transcript would spare you the exact thing being trained: holding unfamiliar German in your head long enough to make sense of it. The clip never changes, so the only thing that can improve between today and the last day is you.

## 3. Neue Bausteine - die Prüfung

### Welche Prüfung

Three bodies examine A2 and all three are recognised. They test the same level in slightly different shapes.

| Prüfung | Wer | Besonderheit |
|---|---|---|
| **Goethe-Zertifikat A2** | Goethe-Institut | the most widely recognised; four parts, paired speaking |
| **telc Deutsch A2** | telc | has a **Sprachbausteine** section - a grammar gap-fill that the Goethe exam does not have |
| **ÖSD Zertifikat A2** | Austrian body | same level, accepted in Germany too |

<Aside type="caution" title="Check the current format before you book">
Examining bodies revise their formats, and a page is a worse source than the body itself. Everything below describes the shape of these exams as published, and it is a **guide to what to practise, not a specification**.

Before you book anything, download the free **Modellsatz** - the official sample paper with audio - from the examining body's own site, and do it under the clock. That is the single most useful hour of exam preparation available, it is free, and it replaces any description including this one.

What follows is here so that today's practice has the right shape. The authority is theirs.
</Aside>

### Die vier Teile

| Teil | Etwa | Was gefragt wird |
|---|---|---|
| **Lesen** | 30 min | short everyday texts - notices, adverts, emails, a forum post - with matching and true/false questions |
| **Hören** | 30 min | short announcements, phone messages and conversations, usually heard twice |
| **Schreiben** | 30 min | two short texts: one informal message, one semi-formal or formal one |
| **Sprechen** | ~15 min | **in a pair**, with another candidate, in three parts |

Roughly ninety minutes of written exam and a quarter of an hour of speaking. The pass mark is **sixty per cent**.

### Der mündliche Teil - drei Teile

This is the part worth preparing, because it is the only one whose shape is genuinely unfamiliar.

<Steps>

1. **Teil 1 - Fragen zur Person.** You get keyword cards and **you ask your partner the questions**, and answer theirs. Not the examiner - your partner. Cards say things like *Wohnort*, *Arbeit*, *Hobbys*, *Familie*.

2. **Teil 2 - über den Alltag sprechen.** A card gives you a topic and you talk about your own life for a minute or two. Then your partner asks you a question about it.

3. **Teil 3 - etwas gemeinsam planen.** The two of you organise something together - a birthday, a trip, a visit, a shared shopping list. You have to propose, disagree, counter-propose and agree.

</Steps>

**Teil 3 is where marks are won and lost**, and it has nothing to do with vocabulary. It is a negotiation, so a candidate who agrees with everything scores badly and a candidate who proposes, declines once, offers an alternative and settles scores well. That is exactly twelve sentences, and they are today's Bausteine.

| Deutsch | English | Wo |

### Was im Schreiben verlangt wird

Two short texts, and you have both already. **Tag 45** taught the formal one - Betreff, `Sehr geehrte`, the fixed phrases, `Mit freundlichen Grüßen`. The informal one is lighter and the register rule from Tag 45 decides which is which.

| Register | Anrede | Schluss | Wofür |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informell | Hallo Anna, / Liebe Anna, | Viele Grüße / Liebe Grüße | a friend, a classmate, a neighbour you know |
| Formell | Sehr geehrte Frau Schneider, | Mit freundlichen Grüßen | a school, a landlord, an office, a company |

Two things the markers look for and candidates forget: **answer every bullet point in the task** - a text that misses one loses marks however good it is - and **keep the comma after the Anrede with a lower-case line after it.**

## 6. Text und Hören

A Teil 3 done well - two candidates planning a birthday. Fourteen lines, and notice that they disagree twice.

> **Prüferin:** Sie planen zusammen eine Geburtstagsfeier für eine Kollegin. Bitte.
> **Ravi:** Wollen wir am Samstag feiern?
> **Marta:** Samstag ist schwierig. Ich arbeite bis sechs. Wie wäre es mit Sonntag?
> **Ravi:** Sonntag passt mir leider nicht - da habe ich Deutschkurs. Vielleicht Samstagabend, um acht?
> **Marta:** Das ist eine gute Idee. Und wo?
> **Ravi:** Ich bin mir nicht sicher - was meinst du? Bei mir oder im Restaurant?
> **Marta:** Bei dir ist billiger. Hast du genug Platz?
> **Ravi:** Für acht Leute, ja. Dann bei mir um acht.
> **Marta:** Einverstanden. Was bringen wir mit?
> **Ravi:** Ich mache das Essen. Bringst du die Getränke mit?
> **Marta:** Gern. Und der Kuchen?
> **Ravi:** Den kaufe ich beim Bäcker. Soll ich sie einladen oder machst du das?
> **Marta:** Mach du das - du arbeitest mit ihr. Können wir das noch einmal besprechen? Also: Samstag, acht Uhr, bei dir.
> **Ravi:** Genau. Essen von mir, Getränke von dir, Kuchen vom Bäcker. Und ich lade sie ein.
> **Marta:** Perfekt. Dann bis Samstag.

Count what Ravi and Marta actually do: **four proposals, two refusals with a reason, two counter-proposals, one handed-over decision, a division of tasks, and a summary at the end.** That is a full-marks Teil 3, and there is not one word of difficult German in it.

Then look at Marta's second-to-last line: `Können wir das noch einmal besprechen? Also: Samstag, acht Uhr, bei dir.` She **summarises the plan**. That costs fifteen seconds and it demonstrates, out loud, that she understood everything her partner said. If you remember one tactic from this page, remember that one.

<Aside type="tip" title="The partner is not your opponent">
A2 speaking is paired, often with a stranger whose German may be much worse or much better than yours, and that frightens people more than the exam does.

Three things are worth knowing. **You are marked separately** - their performance does not lower yours. **A weak partner is an opportunity**, because asking them a clear question and waiting is itself good communicative behaviour, and the examiner sees you doing it. And **a strong partner who talks over you** is handled with one sentence: `Darf ich auch etwas sagen?` - may I say something too.

The behaviour that scores is interaction: asking, answering, reacting, agreeing, disagreeing. A monologue scores badly even in perfect German.
</Aside>

## 8. Sprechen - fünfzehn Minuten, ein kompletter Durchlauf

The whole oral exam, in order, with the model playing both the examiner and your partner. Stand up, and time it.

```text
Du bist Prüferin für das Goethe-Zertifikat A2 UND gleichzeitig meine
Partnerin in der Prüfung. Ich bin Lernender (Tag 57).

Mach den kompletten mündlichen Teil, realistisch und unter Zeit:

TEIL 1 - Fragen zur Person, etwa 4 Minuten.
Gib mir vier Stichwörter, eins nach dem anderen (zum Beispiel Wohnort,
Arbeit, Hobbys, Sprachen). Ich stelle die Frage, du antwortest kurz, und
dann fragst DU mich dasselbe. Wechsel sauber ab.

TEIL 2 - über den Alltag sprechen, etwa 4 Minuten.
Gib mir EIN Thema. Ich spreche eine bis zwei Minuten. Dann stell mir
EINE Nachfrage. Danach machst du dasselbe mit einem anderen Thema und
ich stelle dir die Nachfrage.
```

<Aside type="caution" title="What a mock with a model cannot tell you">
Be clear-eyed about what you just did. A language model is patient, articulates clearly, never interrupts awkwardly, and has no accent from Saxony. A real paired exam has none of those properties, and neither does a real conversation.

So treat the verdict in point 1 as a **floor, not a ceiling** - if the model says you would fail, you would; if it says you would pass, that is weaker evidence than it sounds.

The thing that actually closes the gap is on [Tag 59](/learnings/german/a/a2/tag-59/) and it is the same thing as always: an hour a week with people who are not waiting for you.
</Aside>

## 9. Auf Papier - Goldlist

<div class="de-paper">

**Diktat.** Ten-second loop on the shadowing audio. Six German words by hand, then correct in a second colour.

**Die Redemittel, von Hand.** Write the twelve Teil 3 sentences out by hand, grouped by job: **proposing** (three), **declining with a reason** (two), **counter-proposing** (two), **agreeing** (two), **handing over** (one), **rescuing** (two). Grouping them by what they *do* is what makes them come out at the right moment.

**Zwei Schreiben-Aufgaben, unter der Uhr.** Thirty minutes total, by hand, no model and no dictionary.

1. **Informell, 40 words.** Write to a friend: you cannot come on Saturday, say why, suggest another day, and ask them to reply.
2. **Formell, 40 words.** Write to a language school: you want information about an evening B1 course, ask when it starts and what it costs, and ask for a reply.

Then mark your own. Did you answer **every** point? Comma after the Anrede, lower-case next line, no comma after the Grußformel?

**Fehlerliste.** A fresh week-nine page. Everything from today, plus the three errors from the mock.

**Genus-Farben.** No new nouns worth adding. Instead write out the four exam parts in German - `Lesen, Hören, Schreiben, Sprechen` - with the rough minutes next to each, so the shape of the paper is in your hand and not just on this page.

**Goldlist 19.**

Nineteenth page - today is divisible by three.

<Steps>

1. **Fresh double page**, today's date, plus *Goldlist* and the number 19.

2. **Twenty-five items by hand**, German left, English right, numbered. Take them from Tag 55 and Tag 57 - the connectors that hold a story together, and the twelve Redemittel from today. Write the Redemittel **as whole sentences**, because that is the only form in which they are any use in an exam room.

3. **Say each item aloud once as you write it.** Once.

4. **Read the whole list aloud once** when finished, slowly, without stopping.

5. **Close the notebook and walk away.**

6. **Write in your calendar, 14 days from today:** *Goldlist 19 distillieren* - day 71, after the course ends. Diarise it anyway.

</Steps>

</div>
