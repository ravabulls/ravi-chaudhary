Week six taught you the doctor, the bank and the Amt. Those are the appointments you prepare for. Today is everything in between - the German of a Tuesday, which no course teaches because none of it is grammar and all of it is local.

And this is the page where Germany is most unlike wherever you came from. Not the language: the **rules**. A bottle has a price on top of its price. Shops shut on Sunday. Drilling a hole at nine in the evening is a complaint about you. Rubbish goes into four different containers and the glass goes somewhere else entirely, by colour.

None of that is hard. It is just entirely unobvious, and getting it wrong is how a new neighbour becomes *der Neue, der nichts versteht*.

**Und dann Hören, fünf Minuten.** Same clip, different job, and this is the half that self-study usually skips.

Play the whole forty seconds **three times**, without stopping and without reading anything on this page.

1. **First pass - words.** Write down every German word you catch. Three is a result. Nine is a good day.
2. **Second pass - one sentence.** Write down one complete sentence, even if half of it is a guess.
3. **Third pass - check yourself.** Listen again and correct what you wrote.

**There is no answer key, deliberately.** No transcript is printed here and you are not meant to go hunting for one. A transcript would spare you the exact thing being trained: holding unfamiliar German in your head long enough to make sense of it. The clip never changes, so the only thing that can improve between today and the last day is you.

### Einkaufen

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| die Tüte | the bag |
| der Einkaufswagen | the trolley |
| der Pfand | the deposit |
| die Pfandflasche | the deposit bottle |
| der Leergutautomat | the bottle-return machine |
| passend | exact (of change) |
| das Kleingeld | small change |
| der Kassenbon | the receipt |
| geöffnet / geschlossen | open / closed |
| der Spätkauf / der Kiosk | the late-night shop |

<Aside type="caution" title="Pfand - the price that is not the price">
A bottle of water marked 0.49 costs you 0.74 at the till, and you get the difference back when you bring the bottle in. This is **das Pfand**, it is on nearly every drink container in Germany, and newcomers lose real money by throwing bottles away.

- **Einweg** - single-use cans and most plastic bottles: **25 cents** each.
- **Mehrweg** - reusable glass and sturdier plastic: usually **8 or 15 cents**.
- **No deposit** on wine, spirits, milk and most juice cartons.

You get it back at the **Leergutautomat** in any shop that sells that kind of container: feed the bottles in, take the slip, hand the slip in at the till or let it come off your shopping.

The slip is called **der Pfandbon**, and the sentence you need in front of the machine is `Wo kann ich das Pfand abgeben?` or, when the machine refuses a bottle, `Der Automat nimmt die Flasche nicht.`

One cultural note, offered without comment: bottles left next to a public bin rather than in it have been put there deliberately, for someone who collects them.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Sunday, and the Saturday evening that goes with it">
**Shops are shut on Sundays.** Not most shops - effectively all of them. Supermarkets, chemists, hardware shops, clothes shops: closed. This is law rather than custom, and it does not bend for the fact that you forgot the milk.

What is open: **railway stations and airports**, **petrol stations**, bakeries for a few morning hours, restaurants, and the odd **Spätkauf** or Kiosk. A handful of **verkaufsoffene Sonntage** are declared each year by each city, and they are an event rather than a convenience.

The consequence is a habit, not a sentence: **Saturday is the shopping day**, and a German supermarket at five on a Saturday afternoon is a different experience from the same shop on a Tuesday morning. Learn `Haben Sie am Sonntag geöffnet?` and expect the answer `Nein, sonntags ist zu.`

And one more till habit worth knowing: German cashiers often ask **`Haben Sie es passend?`** - do you have the exact change - and they are not being difficult. Small coins are genuinely useful at bakeries and markets, and `Leider nicht` is a perfectly fine answer.
</Aside>

### Die Nachbarn

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| die Hausordnung | the house rules |
| die Ruhezeit | the quiet period |
| die Mittagsruhe | the midday quiet period |
| der Lärm | the noise |

| der Keller | the cellar |
| die Waschküche | the laundry room |
| der Hausmeister | the caretaker |
| sich beschweren über | to complain about |

<Aside type="tip" title="Die Ruhezeiten, and why the Hausordnung is the authority">
Germany has legally protected quiet hours, and they are taken seriously in a way that surprises almost everyone.

Typically: **from 22:00 to 06:00 every night**, and **all day on Sundays and public holidays**. Many a Hausordnung adds a **Mittagsruhe**, often around 13:00 to 15:00. The exact times come from your building's Hausordnung and your state's noise rules, so **the Hausordnung pinned up in your stairwell is the authority, not this page** - read it once, properly, in the first week.

Inside those hours: no drilling, no hammering, no vacuuming, no washing machine in many buildings, no loud music, and no glass into the bottle bank.

Two sentences to have ready, and the polite one first:

- **`Könnten Sie bitte etwas leiser sein?`** - could you be a bit quieter, please. The Konjunktiv from Tag 52, doing exactly the job it is for.
- **`Wir haben eine Party - ist das in Ordnung?`** - said to the neighbours *beforehand*, which in Germany buys more goodwill than an apology afterwards. A note in the stairwell two days ahead is entirely normal and very German.
</Aside>

### Die Mülltrennung

Four bins and a trip to the glass bank. Colours vary slightly by city, so the labels matter more than the lids.

| Tonne | Was hinein kommt |
|---|---|
| **Papier** (blau) | paper, cardboard, newspaper |
| **Bio** (braun oder grün) | food waste, garden waste, coffee grounds |
| **Verpackung** - Gelbe Tonne oder Gelber Sack | plastic, tins, cartons, anything packaging |
| **Restmüll** (schwarz oder grau) | everything that fits nowhere else |

And separately: **Altglas** goes to a container in the street, sorted **by colour** - `weiß`, `grün`, `braun` - and blue or other glass goes in with the green. **Pfandflaschen do not go here at all**; they go back to the shop. Batteries, bulbs and electronics go to a shop or a **Recyclinghof**.

The sentence that gets you through your first week: **`Wo kommt das hin?`** - where does this go? Short, correct, and it is what Germans themselves ask in somebody else's kitchen.

### Der Handwerker und die Beschwerde

| Deutsch | English |
|---|---|
| der Handwerker | the tradesman |
| der Klempner / der Installateur | the plumber |
| der Elektriker | the electrician |
| der Kostenvoranschlag | the written estimate |
| die Reparatur | the repair |
| tropfen | to drip |
| funktionieren | to work, to function |
| defekt / kaputt | faulty / broken |
| umtauschen | to exchange |
| die Garantie / die Gewährleistung | guarantee / statutory warranty |
| die Rückerstattung | the refund |

<Aside type="tip" title="Umtauschen - what you are actually entitled to">
Worth separating two things that English runs together, because German law does.

**If the thing is faulty**, you have a statutory claim - **die Gewährleistung** - against the seller for **two years** from purchase. That is a legal right and the shop cannot waive it in its terms. What you say is `Das Gerät ist defekt` and `Ich möchte es reklamieren`.

**If the thing is fine and you simply changed your mind**, you have **no general right to an exchange** in a shop. Most shops do it anyway as **Kulanz** - goodwill - within a week or two with the receipt, and that is a favour rather than a right. What you say is `Ich möchte das umtauschen - hier ist der Kassenbon.`

**Online is different again**: distance selling carries a **14-day right of withdrawal** - `das Widerrufsrecht` - with no reason required.

Keep the **Kassenbon**. Germany is a receipt country, the paper fades, and a photograph of it is worth taking.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

A Tuesday, in four short scenes. Nothing in it is difficult and all of it is unobvious.

> **1. An der Kasse**
>
> **Kassiererin:** Zusammen oder getrennt?
> **Ravi:** Zusammen, bitte.
> **Kassiererin:** Brauchen Sie eine Tüte?
> **Ravi:** Nein, danke, ich habe eine.
> **Kassiererin:** Macht siebenundzwanzig vierzig. Haben Sie es passend?
> **Ravi:** Leider nicht. Mit Karte, bitte.
> **Kassiererin:** Girocard? Gern. Und der Pfandbon - soll der abgezogen werden?
> **Ravi:** Ja, bitte.

> **2. Im Treppenhaus**
>
> **Frau Dorn:** Guten Tag. Sie sind der Neue aus dem zweiten Stock, oder?
> **Ravi:** Ja, guten Tag. Chaudhary. Ich bin letzte Woche eingezogen.
> **Frau Dorn:** Willkommen. Haben Sie die Hausordnung gelesen?
> **Ravi:** Noch nicht ganz. Wann sind denn die Ruhezeiten?
> **Frau Dorn:** Ab zweiundzwanzig Uhr, und sonntags den ganzen Tag. Mittags von eins bis drei ist auch Ruhe, aber da ist niemand so streng.
> **Ravi:** Gut zu wissen. Wir haben am Samstag ein paar Leute da - ist das in Ordnung?
> **Frau Dorn:** Wenn Sie es vorher sagen, ist immer alles in Ordnung. Genau so macht man das.

> **3. Beim Müll**
>
> **Ravi:** Entschuldigung, wo kommt das hin? Das ist ein Joghurtbecher.
> **Frau Dorn:** In die Gelbe Tonne. Papier in die blaue, Essensreste in die braune, der Rest in die schwarze.
> **Ravi:** Und die Weinflaschen?
> **Frau Dorn:** Zum Container an der Ecke, nach Farbe sortiert. Aber nicht nach zehn - das hört man im ganzen Haus.

> **4. Am Telefon, mit dem Vermieter**
>
> **Ravi:** Guten Tag, hier spricht Ravi Chaudhary, Beispielstraße 12. Ich rufe wegen der Heizung an - sie funktioniert seit Montag nicht.
> **Vermieter:** Seit Montag? Das hätten Sie gleich sagen sollen.
> **Ravi:** Ich habe am Montag eine E-Mail geschickt. Könnten Sie einen Handwerker schicken?
> **Vermieter:** Ich rufe den Installateur an. Wann sind Sie da?
> **Ravi:** Am Donnerstag ab vierzehn Uhr. Könnten Sie mir vorher Bescheid geben?
> **Vermieter:** Mache ich. Auf Wiederhören.

`zusammen oder getrennt` is together or separately - the standard restaurant and till question - `abziehen` is to deduct, `einziehen` is to move in, `streng` is strict, `nach Farbe sortiert` is sorted by colour, `Bescheid geben` is to let someone know.

Read all four aloud twice, both parts. Then notice three things, because they are the point of the page.

**Frau Dorn's last line in scene 2 is the whole social rule:** `Wenn Sie es vorher sagen, ist immer alles in Ordnung.` Telling people beforehand is worth more in Germany than apologising afterwards, and it applies to parties, deliveries, drilling and the washing machine.

**Scene 4 has a small, important move.** The landlord says *you should have said so straight away*, and Ravi does not apologise - he says `Ich habe am Montag eine E-Mail geschickt` and then asks for what he needs. Stating the fact and moving on is the right register with a German landlord; over-apologising invites the blame to stick.

**And scene 3 is three sentences of vocabulary that will save you a year of small embarrassments.**

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Four short scenes, and one of them goes wrong. Stand up.

```text
Du spielst heute vier verschiedene Personen. Ich bin Lernender (Tag 58).
Jede Szene maximal zwei Minuten.

SZENE 1 - Kassiererin im Supermarkt. Frag nach Tüte, passendem Geld,
Kartenzahlung und Pfandbon. Sprich schnell, so wie an einer deutschen
Kasse.

SZENE 2 - Nachbarin im Treppenhaus. Du kennst mich nicht. Begrüße mich,
frag, ob ich die Hausordnung gelesen habe, und erklär mir die
Ruhezeiten. Ich muss am Ende die Party ankündigen.

SZENE 3 - Nachbar, der sich BESCHWERT. Du bist sauer: meine Waschmaschine
lief am Sonntag. Sei nicht unhöflich, aber auch nicht nachgiebig. Ich
muss mich entschuldigen UND erklären, dass ich die Regel nicht kannte.

SZENE 4 - Vermieter am Telefon. Die Heizung ist kaputt. Du bist knapp
und etwas genervt. Ich muss ruhig bleiben, die Fakten nennen, einen
Handwerkertermin ausmachen und um eine Bestätigung bitten.

Korrigiere nach jeder Szene nur EINEN Fehler - den, der am meisten
gestört hat. Fang mit Szene 1 an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="Scene 3 is the one to do twice">
Being complained at in a foreign language is a different skill from complaining, and it is much harder, because you are off balance before you start.

Three sentences handle almost all of it:

- **`Das tut mir leid, das wusste ich nicht.`** - I am sorry, I did not know that. Honest, short, and it defuses.
- **`Ich passe in Zukunft auf.`** - I will be careful in future.
- **`Danke, dass Sie es mir gesagt haben.`** - thank you for telling me. This one is unexpected and it works, because the neighbour came to the door expecting an argument.

What not to do is explain at length. A German neighbour who has complained wants the behaviour to change, not the reasoning behind it, and a long explanation reads as an excuse.
</Aside>
