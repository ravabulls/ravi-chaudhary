**Living an actual German life.**

A1 taught you to be helped. A2 is where you stop needing the other person to slow down, and where German stops being a subject and becomes the language you do things in - sit through a doctor's appointment, open a bank account, register your address, win a flat against twelve other applicants, make a phone call, send a formal email, and get through a job interview.

It is also where the grammar finishes. By Tag 53 there is no structure left that an A2 speaker needs.

<Aside type="note" title="Start here only if A1 is behind you">
A2 assumes everything in [module A1](/learnings/german/a/a1/) - the four cases in a table, the present tense, both past tenses, the Modalverben, `weil` and `dass`. The review schedule on these pages actively re-drills A1 material thirty days back, so starting cold here means the review blocks will test you on pages you never did.

The check is the self-assessment on [Tag 28](/learnings/german/a/a1/tag-28/).
</Aside>

## What A2 actually gets you

Plainly, from the Council of Europe: at A2 you can understand sentences and frequently used expressions about immediate, concrete matters; communicate in simple, routine tasks requiring a direct exchange of information on familiar topics; and describe your background, your immediate environment and matters of immediate need.

Read the middle clause again - **"routine tasks requiring a direct exchange of information"** - and then look at the four weeks above. The doctor, the pharmacy, the bank, the Amt, the flat, the phone call, the application, the interview. Every one of those is exactly that kind of task, and every one is something you will actually have to do.

What A2 still does not get you: two Germans talking to each other at speed, the radio, a newspaper read comfortably, or an argument about anything abstract. That is [B1](/learnings/german/b/b1/), and B1 is the level that institutions care about.

<Aside type="caution" title="The honest limitation of this module">
A2's weak skill here is **listening**, and it is a limitation of the pages rather than of the level. The reading, writing, drilling and speaking blocks are thorough; the listening slot has been one short audio file reused with different instructions.

Tag 56 says this at length and so does its last dialogue. The remedy is not another page: it is twenty minutes a day of real German audio, the same material more than once, and an hour a week with people who do not slow down for you.
</Aside>
