*Tag 31 von 60 - der gute Mann, die Endungen nach dem bestimmten Artikel. Zwei Stunden.*

---

For thirty days every adjective you have used has sat **after** the verb: `Die Wohnung ist **klein**`, `Der Kaffee ist **teuer**`. In that position German adjectives take no ending at all, which is why nobody mentioned it.

Today they move **in front of the noun** - `die **kleine** Wohnung`, `der **teure** Kaffee` - and there they have to agree with it. This is the last big ending system in German, and it has a reputation for being the hardest thing in the language.

It is not, if you take it in three days as this course does. Today is **only** the version after `der`, `die`, `das` - and in that version there are exactly **two possible endings**, `-e` and `-en`, with five slots taking `-e` and everything else taking `-en`. Two endings, one rule.

Two hours, top to bottom, every answer **out loud** before you type it. Nothing is saved. </Aside>

## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Zwei Stellungen, zwei Verhaltensweisen

| Stellung | Endung | Beispiel |
|---|---|---|
| **nach dem Verb** | keine | Die Wohnung ist **klein**. |
| **vor dem Nomen** | ja | die **kleine** Wohnung |

The first one you have had since Tag 22 and it never changes. Today is only about the second.

### Die Tabelle nach der, die, das

| | maskulin | feminin | neutral | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nominativ** | der gut**e** Mann | die gut**e** Frau | das gut**e** Kind | die gut**en** Kinder |
| **Akkusativ** | den gut**en** Mann | die gut**e** Frau | das gut**e** Kind | die gut**en** Kinder |
| **Dativ** | dem gut**en** Mann | der gut**en** Frau | dem gut**en** Kind | den gut**en** Kindern |

Now look at where the `-e` endings are. There are five of them, and they sit in a block in the top-left corner:

| | mask | fem | neut | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nom** | **-e** | **-e** | **-e** | -en |
| **Akk** | -en | **-e** | **-e** | -en |
| **Dat** | -en | -en | -en | -en |

<Aside type="tip" title="The whole rule in one sentence">
**After `der`, `die`, `das`: `-e` in the five slots at the top left, `-en` in the other seven.**

Or if you prefer it as a procedure: **the ending is `-en` unless the noun is singular and in the nominative, or singular feminine or neuter in the accusative.**

Or, simplest and good enough nine times out of ten: **say `-en` unless the article is `der`, `die` or `das` in its dictionary form.** `der gute`, `die gute`, `das gute` - but `den guten`, `dem guten`, `der guten`, `die guten`.
</Aside>

### Warum es nach der einfacher ist

The reason this version has only two endings is worth knowing, because it explains tomorrow. **The article has already done the work.** `dem` tells you it is dative; the adjective does not need to repeat the information, so it takes the neutral `-en`. German only loads the adjective with real information when the article fails to carry it - which is exactly what happens with `ein`, and exactly why tomorrow has more endings.

### Die zwölf Bausteine

| Deutsch | English | Wann |
|---|---|---|
| Das ist der neue Kurs | this is the new course | nominative masculine, -e |
| Ich nehme den neuen Kurs | I am taking the new course | accusative masculine, -en |
| Die kleine Wohnung gefällt mir | I like the small flat | nominative feminine, -e |
| Ich suche die kleine Wohnung | I am looking for the small flat | accusative feminine, -e |
| Das gute Buch ist teuer | the good book is expensive | nominative neuter, -e |
| Ich habe das gute Buch gekauft | I bought the good book | accusative neuter, -e |
| Ich fahre mit dem neuen Bus | I go by the new bus | dative, -en |
| Ich wohne in der kleinen Wohnung | I live in the small flat | dative feminine, -en |
| Die netten Leute helfen mir | the nice people help me | plural, -en |
| Wie heißt der neue Kollege? | what is the new colleague called | |
| Das ist die beste Lösung | that is the best solution | superlative plus ending |
| Ich hatte einen langen Tag | I had a long day | tomorrow's pattern, learn as a block |

<Aside type="caution" title="die Leute is always plural">
`die Leute` means *people* and has no singular - you cannot say *ein Leut*. It always takes plural endings, which makes it a useful default for practising the `-en` plural: `die netten Leute`, `mit den netten Leuten`.

`Ich hatte **einen langen** Tag` is in today's list even though `einen` is tomorrow's pattern, because it is a sentence you will want this week. Learn it whole; the rule arrives in twenty-four hours.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

Looking at a flat, which is where adjectives in front of nouns actually earn their keep.

> **Maklerin:** Guten Tag! Das ist die neue Wohnung. Kommen Sie rein.
> **Ravi:** Danke. Oh, das große Fenster ist schön!
> **Maklerin:** Ja, das ist das beste Zimmer. Die Küche ist dort.
> **Ravi:** Die kleine Küche? Die ist ein bisschen dunkel.
> **Maklerin:** Am Morgen ist sie hell. Und der neue Kühlschrank bleibt hier.
> **Ravi:** Gut. Und wie hoch ist die Miete?
> **Maklerin:** Siebenhundert Euro, mit den Nebenkosten.
> **Ravi:** Das ist viel. Gibt es auch die kleine Wohnung im zweiten Stock?
> **Maklerin:** Die ist leider weg. Aber diese hier hat den großen Balkon.
> **Ravi:** Der ist wirklich schön. Und die Leute im Haus?
> **Maklerin:** Die netten Leute von unten sind schon zwanzig Jahre hier.
> **Ravi:** Gut zu wissen. Ich hatte einen langen Tag - darf ich morgen anrufen?
> **Maklerin:** Natürlich. Aber die gute Wohnung geht schnell weg.
> **Ravi:** Das glaube ich. Ich rufe morgen früh an.

`die Maklerin` is the estate agent, `dunkel` and `hell` are dark and light, `der Kühlschrank` is the fridge, `die Miete` is the rent, `die Nebenkosten` are the service charges, `der Stock` is the floor, `leider` is unfortunately, `von unten` is from downstairs. All free, and all of it comes back properly on Tag 43.

Count the adjective-plus-noun pairs - there are twelve, and every ending follows today's table. Read it aloud twice, both parts, **and say each ending deliberately** rather than swallowing it.

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Describing things, which forces an ending every few words. Stand up.

```text
Du bist Maklerin und zeigst mir eine Wohnung. Ich bin Lernender (Tag 31).
Heute übe ich Adjektivendungen nach der, die, das.

Beschreibe mir fünf Zimmer, und benutze dabei jedes Mal ein Adjektiv vor
dem Nomen - das große Fenster, die kleine Küche, der neue Kühlschrank,
das helle Zimmer, der alte Balkon.

Frag mich nach jedem Zimmer: Wie finden Sie das Zimmer? Welches Zimmer
gefällt Ihnen besser? Was ist das beste Zimmer? Damit ich selbst
Adjektive vor Nomen benutzen muss.

Benutze NUR der, die, das - noch kein ein oder mein. Wenn ich die falsche
Endung sage, wiederhole meinen Satz einfach richtig, ohne Erklärung.
Fang jetzt an.
```

<Aside type="tip" title="What to listen for today">
Whether you say an ending at all. The first failure mode is not the wrong ending - it is **no ending**, because thirty days of `Die Wohnung ist klein` have trained you to leave the adjective bare.

The second is over-correcting to `-en` everywhere, which is actually a good place to be: `-en` is right in seven of the twelve slots, so a blanket `-en` is wrong less than half the time and always comprehensible. If you have to guess, guess `-en`.
</Aside>

## 9. Auf Papier - zweite Destillation

<div class="de-paper">

**Diktat.** Ten-second loop on the shadowing audio. Write six German words by hand without pausing, then correct in a second colour.

**Satz des Tages.** Write out by hand, from memory, eight sentences describing your actual flat, with an adjective in front of every noun: the big window, the small kitchen, the old building, the nice people, the new furniture. Eight lines, all using `der`, `die`, `das` only. Then **circle every ending** and check each against the table.

**Die Adjektivtabelle.** The page you will consult most over the next month. By hand, the twelve-cell grid from section 3, with the five `-e` slots in a different colour from the seven `-en` slots. The colour is the point - this is a shape to recognise, not a list to recite.

**Fehlerliste.** Week-five page. Every item the drills marked wrong today, in German only. Every missing ending gets the full phrase written twice.

**Genus-Farben.** Six new nouns: blue `der Kollege`, `der Kühlschrank`, `der Stock`; red `die Miete`, `die Lösung`, `die Maklerin`. Note `die Maklerin` and `die Kollegin` both take `-in`, which is the feminine job-title rule from Tag 16.

Plural-only, no colour: `die Leute`, `die Nebenkosten`.

**Goldlist 1 - zweite Destillation.**

The first double-distilled page of the course. Goldlist 1 went into the notebook on **Tag 03**, was distilled once on **Tag 17**, and comes back today - twenty-eight days after it was written.

<Steps>

1. **Find the Tag 17 page** - the distillation, not the original. That is the short list of what had *not* stuck after the first fourteen days.

2. **Read it once, aloud, slowly.** No testing, no covering.

3. **Cross out everything you now know.** These are the items that resisted once already, so expect a lower survival rate than the first round - which is the whole point.

4. **Copy what is left onto a fresh page**, headed *Goldlist 1 - Destillation 2* with today's date.

5. **Close the notebook and walk away.**

6. **Write in your calendar, 14 days from today:** *Goldlist 1 - dritte Destillation* - that is Tag 45.

</Steps>

Write down how many survived. You now have two numbers for the same list - Tag 17 and today. The interesting figure is **how much of the first distillation also went in without review**, because that is the method working on its own second pass, and it is the point at which most people stop doubting it.
