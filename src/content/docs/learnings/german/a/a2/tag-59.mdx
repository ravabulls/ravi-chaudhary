Second-to-last day, and the most practical page in the course.

A2 is a real level and almost nothing official asks for it. **B1 is the level institutions actually use**: it is where the Integrationskurs ends, it is the usual language requirement for naturalisation, it is the floor at which most employers treat German as a working language, and it is the entry point for the state-funded job-related courses that go on to B2 and C1.

So today is two things. The **German you need to enrol on a course and book an exam** - which is twelve sentences - and the **map of how the system actually works**, which is the part nobody explains.

<Aside type="caution" title="Rules, fees and formats change - the authority is never a web page">
Everything on this page about courses, fees and requirements describes how the German system is set up, and it is here so that you know what to ask for and what the words mean.

**It is not a source to rely on.** Fees are revised, formats are revised, and immigration law changed substantially in 2024. Before you act on any of it, check the body that decides:

- **Integrationskurs, fees, entitlement:** the BAMF - Bundesamt für Migration und Flüchtlinge.
- **Exam format and dates:** the Goethe-Institut, telc or ÖSD directly, and their free Modellsatz.
- **Naturalisation:** your city's Einbürgerungsbehörde. In Hamburg that is the Bezirksamt.
- **Course places and prices:** your local Volkshochschule, and whichever private schools are BAMF-approved near you.

Where this page gives a number, treat it as "roughly, at the time of writing" and go and check.
</Aside>

Play the whole forty seconds **three times**, without stopping and without reading anything on this page.

1. **First pass - words.** Write down every German word you catch. Three is a result. Nine is a good day.
2. **Second pass - one sentence.** Write down one complete sentence, even if half of it is a guess.
3. **Third pass - check yourself.** Listen again and correct what you wrote.

**There is no answer key, deliberately.** No transcript is printed here and you are not meant to go hunting for one. A transcript would spare you the exact thing being trained: holding unfamiliar German in your head long enough to make sense of it. ## 3. Neue Bausteine - zwölf Stück

### Wofür B1 gebraucht wird

| Wofür | Was verlangt wird |
|---|---|
| **Integrationskurs** | the course **ends** at B1 - that is its target, tested by the DTZ |
| **Einbürgerung** | "sufficient knowledge of German", in practice **B1** |
| **Berufssprachkurs** | the state-funded job-German courses **start** from B1 and run to B2 or C1 |
| **Arbeitgeber** | below B1, German is a project; from B1 it is a working language |
| **Ausbildung, Studium auf Deutsch** | B1 is the floor; most programmes want B2 or more |

### Der Integrationskurs

The backbone of the whole system, and worth understanding even if you are not obliged to attend one.

<Steps>

1. **Structure.** A **Sprachkurs** of six modules, each of 100 teaching units, so **600 units** in total - taking a complete beginner to **B1**. Then an **Orientierungskurs** of 100 units on German law, history and society.

2. **The exams at the end.** The **DTZ** - Deutsch-Test für Zuwanderer - for the language, and **Leben in Deutschland** for the orientation part.

3. **The DTZ is scored across two levels.** It reports whether you reached **A2 or B1**, which is unusual and useful: you cannot really "fail" it, you simply find out where you are.

4. **Who can attend.** Some people are **entitled** (`berechtigt`), some are **obliged** (`verpflichtet`), and others can apply to join (`Zulassung`). What you need in your hand is a **Berechtigungsschein** from the BAMF.

5. **Cost.** Heavily subsidised, with the state paying most of it; exemptions exist for people on benefits, and part of the fee can be **reimbursed** if you pass within two years of being admitted. The amounts are set by the BAMF and change - ask them.

6. **Where.** At a BAMF-approved provider: a Volkshochschule, or an approved private school. The VHS is usually the cheapest and the most patient.

</Steps>

### Welche Prüfung

| Prüfung | Wer | Wofür besonders geeignet |
|---|---|---|
| **Goethe-Zertifikat B1** | Goethe-Institut | the most widely recognised, and **modular** - the four parts can be taken and passed separately |
| **telc Deutsch B1** | telc | widely accepted; has the **Sprachbausteine** grammar section |
| **DTZ** | telc, for the BAMF | the Integrationskurs exam; reports A2 **or** B1 |
| **ÖSD Zertifikat B1** | Austrian body | accepted in Germany too |

The modularity of the Goethe B1 is worth a sentence on its own, because it changes how you prepare. **You can sit Lesen, Hören, Schreiben and Sprechen separately, pass them separately, and combine the certificates.** For a self-taught learner whose reading and writing are well ahead of their listening - which this course has probably produced - that is a genuinely large advantage.

### Die Einbürgerung

Germany's citizenship law was substantially reformed in 2024, and the detail belongs to your Einbürgerungsbehörde rather than to this page. The language part, in outline:

- The standard requirement is **sufficient German, understood as B1**, evidenced by a certificate - a Goethe, telc or ÖSD B1, or a DTZ at B1, or a German school leaving certificate.
- There is a route for **special integration achievements** that shortens the residence requirement, and that route asks for **more** German, at C1.
- A **Einbürgerungstest** on law and society is separate from the language test. Passing **Leben in Deutschland** in an Integrationskurs normally counts for it.

The practical point for today: **the certificate is the thing.** Speaking good German is not evidence; a piece of paper with B1 on it is. Sit the exam.

## 6. Text und Hören

The conversation at the Volkshochschule counter, which is where this page becomes real.

> **Beraterin:** Guten Tag. Was können wir für Sie tun?
> **Ravi:** Guten Tag. Ich möchte mich für einen B1-Kurs anmelden.
> **Beraterin:** Gern. Welches Niveau haben Sie jetzt?
> **Ravi:** A2. Ich habe aber kein Zertifikat - ich habe allein gelernt, zwei Stunden am Tag.
> **Beraterin:** Allein? Respekt. Dann brauchen wir einen Einstufungstest. Der ist kostenlos und dauert etwa eine Stunde.
> **Ravi:** Wann ist der nächste?
> **Beraterin:** Donnerstag um siebzehn Uhr. Danach wissen wir, ob B1.1 oder B1.2 passt.
> **Ravi:** Gut. Gibt es einen Abendkurs? Ich arbeite tagsüber.
> **Beraterin:** Zweimal pro Woche, montags und mittwochs, achtzehn bis einundzwanzig Uhr. Zehn Wochen.
> **Ravi:** Und was kostet das?
> **Beraterin:** Das steht im Programmheft - und wenn Sie einen Berechtigungsschein vom BAMF haben, zahlen Sie viel weniger. Haben Sie einen?
> **Ravi:** Ich glaube nicht. Ich bin über die Uni hier.
> **Beraterin:** Dann fragen Sie trotzdem beim BAMF nach. Es kostet nichts zu fragen.
> **Ravi:** Mache ich. Und die Prüfung - kann ich die hier machen?
> **Beraterin:** Ja, wir sind Prüfungszentrum. Goethe B1, viermal im Jahr.
> **Ravi:** Stimmt es, dass man die vier Teile einzeln machen kann?
> **Beraterin:** Beim Goethe-Zertifikat B1 ja. Viele machen erst Lesen und Schreiben und später Hören und Sprechen.
> **Ravi:** Das wäre für mich perfekt. Mein Hören ist viel schlechter als mein Lesen.
> **Beraterin:** Das ist bei Selbstlernern fast immer so. Dann melden Sie sich zum Einstufungstest an - hier ist das Formular.
> **Ravi:** Danke. Und wenn ich den Test gut mache, kann ich direkt in B1.2?
> **Beraterin:** Kann sein. Der Test entscheidet, nicht ich.

`das Programmheft` is the course brochure, `tagsüber` is during the day, `nachfragen` is to enquire, `das Prüfungszentrum` is an exam centre, `der Selbstlerner` is a self-taught learner, `Kann sein` is *possibly*.

Read it aloud twice, both parts. Then read Ravi's lines again on their own and notice that **every one of them is from this course** - `Ich möchte mich anmelden` from Tag 40, `Gibt es einen Abendkurs` built on Tag 28's `Gibt es`, `Das wäre für mich perfekt` from Tag 52, `Stimmt es, dass ...` from Tag 50. Nothing on this page needed new grammar, which is the point of it arriving on day 59.

<Aside type="tip" title="Sit the exam even if nobody has asked you to">
An argument worth making plainly, because self-taught learners often skip it.

**A certificate does three things that your actual German cannot.** It satisfies institutions - the Einbürgerungsbehörde does not interview you, it reads your file. It gets you into the next course at the right level without arguing. And it converts "I have been studying German for a while" on a CV into a line a German recruiter can act on.

It also does a fourth thing, which is less obvious and possibly more valuable: **it sets a date.** Sixty days of this course worked partly because each page was a fixed unit of work. An exam in four months is the same mechanism at a larger scale, and it is the single most reliable way to stop a self-study project from quietly dissolving into "I'll get back to it".

Book the Einstufungstest first. It is free, it takes an hour, and it tells you something true.
</Aside>

## 8. Sprechen - zehn Minuten

Two real errands, rehearsed before you run them. Stand up.

```text
Du spielst zwei Personen. Ich bin Lernender (Tag 59).

TEIL 1 - fünf Minuten. Du bist Beraterin an der Volkshochschule Hamburg.
Ich will mich für einen B1-Abendkurs anmelden. Frag mich nach Niveau,
Zertifikat, Beruf und Zeiten. Erklär mir den Einstufungstest. Sag mir
NICHT alles von selbst - lass mich nach Preis, Dauer, Terminen und der
Prüfung fragen. Wenn ich eine wichtige Frage vergesse, frag am Ende:
"Wollten Sie noch etwas wissen?"

TEIL 2 - fünf Minuten. Jetzt bist du am Telefon bei einem Prüfungszentrum.
Ich will das Goethe-Zertifikat B1 buchen. Frag mich, welche Teile ich
machen will und wann. Nenne zwei Termine im 24-Stunden-Format und einen
Preis. Ich muss die Termine zurücklesen und meinen Namen buchstabieren.

Sprich normal schnell. Korrigiere nur Fehler, die die Anmeldung
schiefgehen lassen würden.
```
