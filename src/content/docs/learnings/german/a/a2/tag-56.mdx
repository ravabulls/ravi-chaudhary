Eight weeks. Fifty-six days, five hundred and fifty-two chunks, eighteen Goldlist pages, and a notebook that is now the most valuable object in this whole project.

Today measures it, and the measurement has to be honest in both directions - neither the encouragement that tells you you are further along than you are, nor the perfectionism that refuses to count what you can actually do.

The official answer is straightforward: **the grammar and vocabulary on these fifty-six pages cover the A2 syllabus and parts of B1.** The real answer depends on something the pages cannot give you, and that is in section 3.

## 2. Shadowing - zehn Minuten

<div class="de-drill" data-type="shadow" data-title="Shadowing - FSI Basic Course 2, Unit 19" data-audio="/german/audio/a2/tag-56.mp3" data-note="Ten passes over a stretch picked at random, half a second behind, no reading. **Und dann Hören, fünf Minuten.** Same clip, different job, and this is the half that self-study usually skips.

Play the whole forty seconds **three times**, without stopping and without reading anything on this page.

1. **First pass - words.** Write down every German word you catch. Three is a result. Nine is a good day.
2. **Second pass - one sentence.** Write down one complete sentence, even if half of it is a guess.
3. **Third pass - check yourself.** Listen again and correct what you wrote.

**There is no answer key, deliberately.** No transcript is printed here and you are not meant to go hunting for one. A transcript would spare you the exact thing being trained: holding unfamiliar German in your head long enough to make sense of it. The clip never changes, so the only thing that can improve between today and the last day is you.

## 3. Der Test - sechs Aufgaben

Do all six before reading section 4. Time yourself honestly, write down what happens, and do not look anything up.

<Steps>

1. **Sprechen, drei Minuten.** Record yourself telling the story of your first German bureaucratic appointment - four parts, no notes, no stopping. Then listen back once.

2. **Schreiben, acht Minuten.** Write an email, by hand, to a landlord asking for a viewing: Betreff, Anrede, three sentences, closing, Grußformel. No model, no dictionary.

3. **Lesen, fünf Minuten.** Find a real German flat advert you have not seen before. Read it once and write down four things: the Warmmiete, the Kaution, the number of bedrooms, and whether there is a kitchen.

4. **Hören, fünf Minuten.** Find any short German news clip or podcast segment - not a learner recording. Listen twice. Write down, in German, the three things you understood.

5. **Grammatik, zehn Minuten.** From memory, on paper: the four-case article table, the relative pronoun table, the Konjunktiv II forms of haben, sein, können and werden, and fifteen verb-preposition pairs with their cases.

6. **Die Prozentzahl.** Ten minutes of free conversation with a model, no corrections, then ask for the comprehensibility percentage. You have one from Tag 48 and one from Tag 54.

</Steps>

Write all six results on one page before continuing.

## 4. Die Einschätzung - was die Ergebnisse bedeuten

Now read this against what you wrote.

### Aufgabe 1 - Sprechen

| Was passiert ist | Was das heißt |
|---|---|
| Three minutes, four parts, a few long pauses | **solid A2, reaching into B1** - this is the level the course was built to reach |
| Ninety seconds, then it collapsed | **A2 in production** - normal at eight weeks of self-study, and the fix is conversation, not pages |
| You could not start without notes | **A1 in production, A2 in knowledge** - the material is in there; the retrieval is not built yet |

### Aufgabe 2 - Schreiben

If you produced a correct email with the right register, a comma after the Anrede and a lower-case next line, in under eight minutes, that is **comfortably A2 and arguably B1 for this text type.** Written German is where this course has taken you furthest, because formulaic writing rewards exactly what fifty-six days of drilling produces.

### Aufgabe 3 - Lesen

Four correct answers from one reading is **A2 reading, and above A2 for this genre** - a German advert is dense and abbreviated. Two or three correct is still A2. If the Warmmiete was wrong, reread Tag 43; it is the one error with a price attached.

### Aufgabe 4 - Hören

This is the one that will be worst, and it is important to say why rather than to soften it.

**Listening is the weakest skill this course builds**, and it is worth being precise about why. You have had fifty-six distinct forty-second clips of real German - a different one on every page, each from a public-domain government course - shadowed and then listened to cold with no transcript. That is about forty minutes of audio, worked hard.

Forty minutes is a serious amount of close listening and a trivial amount of **exposure**. Three things understood from a real news clip is a reasonable result for that input: the clips train your ear on carefully recorded, slowly read, sixty-year-old studio German, and a news presenter is none of those things.

So this gap is not a flaw in the method, it is a shortage of hours - and it is the easiest gap to close, because it needs no grammar at all. It needs **German in your ears** - podcasts, radio, television with German subtitles, the same episode several times. Twenty minutes a day of that, for a month, moves listening more than any page here could.

### Aufgabe 5 - Grammatik

The tables are either there or they are not, and at this point they should be. If one of the four would not come, that is the one to put on the wall - and it is probably the verb-preposition pairs, which are not grammar at all but a list, and lists need copying rather than understanding.

### Aufgabe 6 - Die drei Prozentzahlen

Three numbers, from Tag 48, Tag 54 and today. The useful reading is not the level but the shape:

- **Rising:** the structures from week eight have become usable.
- **Flat:** you are saying more complex things at the same clarity - which is progress, because the sentences are longer now.
- **Dropped then recovered:** the most common pattern, and the healthiest one. Week eight broke your fluency open and this week it closed again around more material.

<Aside type="caution" title="What A2 actually is, and what it is not">
The Council of Europe's description of A2 is worth reading in plain terms, because it is more modest than people assume and more useful than it sounds.

**A2 means** you can understand sentences and frequently used expressions about immediate, concrete matters - family, shopping, local geography, employment. You can communicate in simple, routine tasks requiring a direct exchange of information on familiar topics. You can describe your background, your immediate environment and matters of immediate need in simple terms.

That is an accurate description of what fifty-six days produces, and look at what it includes: **the doctor, the pharmacy, the bank, the Amt, the flat, the telephone, an application and an interview.** Every one of those is "a routine task requiring a direct exchange of information", and you can now do all of them in German.

**A2 does not mean** you can follow a conversation between two Germans talking to each other, understand the radio, read a newspaper comfortably, or discuss anything abstract. Those are B1 and B2, and expecting them of yourself now is the quickest route to concluding wrongly that the eight weeks did not work.

**B1 is the level that matters institutionally.** It is the level of the German integration course, the usual language requirement for naturalisation, and the point at which most employers consider German a working language rather than a project. B1 means handling most situations that arise while travelling, describing experiences and giving reasons for opinions.

The distance from here to B1 is not more grammar - you already have some of B1's grammar. It is **volume**: hours of listening, and hours of talking to people who are not a language model. That is the honest next step, and it is in section 10.
</Aside>

## 6. Text und Hören

The last text of week eight, and it is the one you have been working towards since Tag 01: a conversation between two people that is simply a conversation.

> **Jonas:** Und, wie war die erste Woche?
> **Ravi:** Besser als ich gedacht habe. Die Leute, mit denen ich arbeite, erklären viel.
> **Jonas:** Und auf Deutsch?
> **Ravi:** Meistens. In der Montagsrunde habe ich etwa die Hälfte verstanden. Aber als Frau Kaya die Zahlen vorgelesen hat, war ich weg.
> **Jonas:** Zahlen sind das Schwerste. Das geht allen so, nicht nur Ihnen.
> **Ravi:** Wirklich?
> **Jonas:** Ich habe zwei Jahre in Frankreich gearbeitet. Nach einem Jahr habe ich Witze verstanden, aber Telefonnummern musste ich mir immer zweimal sagen lassen.
> **Ravi:** Das kenne ich. Deshalb lese ich jede Zahl zurück.
> **Jonas:** Gute Angewohnheit. Und wie machen Sie das mit dem Deutsch - Kurs?
> **Ravi:** Bis jetzt allein. Acht Wochen, jeden Tag zwei Stunden. Aber ich merke, dass es jetzt nicht mehr an der Grammatik hängt.
> **Jonas:** Woran dann?
> **Ravi:** Am Hören. Und daran, dass ich zu wenig mit Menschen rede. Wenn ich mehr reden würde, wäre ich schon weiter.
> **Jonas:** Dann reden Sie mit mir. Ich korrigiere nichts, aber ich rede nicht langsamer - abgemacht?
> **Ravi:** Abgemacht.
> **Jonas:** Und noch etwas: gehen Sie mittags mit in die Kantine. Da reden vier Leute gleichzeitig über nichts. Das ist anstrengender als jede Besprechung und es bringt mehr als ein Kurs.
> **Ravi:** Das glaube ich sofort.
> **Jonas:** Nach einem halben Jahr merken Sie es nicht mehr. Dann ärgern Sie sich nur noch darüber, dass Sie auf Englisch keine Formulare mehr verstehen.

`der Witz` is the joke, `sich etwas sagen lassen` is to have something said to you, `die Angewohnheit` is the habit, `abgemacht` is *agreed*, `die Kantine` is the canteen, `gleichzeitig` is at the same time, `anstrengend` is tiring, `die Besprechung` is the meeting.

Read it aloud twice, both parts.

Then notice two things. First: **you can read that unaided**, and eight weeks ago it would have been impossible. Second: Jonas's advice is the same as section 4's and the same as section 10's, which is not a coincidence. Everyone who has done this says the same thing, and it is about volume rather than method.

Five hundred and fifty-two chunks across eight weeks, and the grammar is now finished. There is no ninth week of structures, because there are no more structures that an A2 speaker needs.

What the eight weeks produced, stated plainly: **the complete case system, four tenses, the adjective endings, the full subordinate-clause toolkit, relative clauses, the Konjunktiv II, and the vocabulary of every institution you have to deal with in your first year in Germany.** Plus a notebook with eighteen Goldlist pages, a Problemliste, a Fehlerliste for each week and two or three hundred handwritten nouns.

<Aside type="caution" title="Was jetzt wirklich fehlt">
Three things, and only one of them can be fixed by a page like this.

**1. Hören.** Still the biggest gap, even with fifty-six clips - because forty minutes of studio German is close listening, not exposure. The fix is volume: twenty minutes a day of **modern** German audio, the same material more than once, with subtitles the second time and without them the third. Podcasts, radio, television. No new grammar required.

**2. Menschen.** The second gap, and the one that no self-study course of any quality can close. Everything on these pages was practised with a language model that waited for you, repeated on request and never changed the subject. Real speakers do none of those things.

The remedy is unglamorous and it is the thing Jonas said in section 6: **a canteen with four people talking at once.** A Sprachtreff, a Tandempartner, colleagues at lunch, a sports club, a volunteer shift. One hour a week of that is worth more now than five hours of pages.

**3. Zeit.** The third is simply that A2 to B1 takes months rather than weeks, and no amount of intensity changes that. The useful thing is that from here the work can be **passive** as well as active - audio while cooking, a German podcast on the commute, a German news app instead of an English one.

**What the last four days are for.** Tag 57 to Tag 60 are not new grammar. They are the handover:

- **Tag 57 - Prüfungstraining.** The A2 exam formats, what the Goethe-Zertifikat A2 and telc Deutsch A2 actually test, and a full practice run.
- **Tag 58 - Der Alltag.** The German of ordinary life that an institution-focused course skips: shopping, neighbours, tradespeople, complaints, small talk.
- **Tag 59 - Der Weg zu B1.** The integration course, the naturalisation requirement, what B1 contains, and a realistic six-month plan built around listening and people rather than pages.
- **Tag 60 - Rückblick und Plan.** Reading back the story you wrote on Tag 55, the final self-assessment, and what the notebook does next.

Those four will be written. Until they are, the most useful thing you can do with the next four sessions is the thing section 4 and section 6 both arrive at independently: **find someone to talk to, and put German in your ears every single day.**
