*Managing Sustainable Innovations — TUHH Institute for Technology & Innovation Management, Hamburg · part of my Technology Management MBA · my own study notes, rewritten in my words for revision.*

---

This module asks a question that's easy to say and hard to do: **how do companies innovate in a way that's good for the planet and society — and still works as a business?** It starts from what "sustainability" actually means (beyond the buzzword), builds up the strategies and tools to make innovation sustainable, and then gets into the *human* side — embedding it in an organisation, leading the change, and the kind of leadership it takes.

The through-line is the **triple bottom line**: a sustainable business has to balance **economic, social and environmental** performance at once — for people alive today *and* for future generations. That balance is genuinely a "wicked problem", full of trade-offs, and most of the course is about navigating it honestly.

I rewrote the whole thing in my own words as revision notes, with the cases (Toyota, Patagonia, Carlsberg, Interface) used to make the ideas concrete.

<Aside type="tip">
**The one idea to carry through.** Sustainability isn't a bolt-on feature or a marketing badge — it's a *constraint and a lens* on every decision. The strong version asks you to **decouple** human progress from resource use and harm: create more value while doing less damage. Almost every framework below is a different way to make that decoupling real.
</Aside>
