*Innovation & New Business Proposal - TUHH Institute of Entrepreneurship & Institute of Innovation Marketing, Hamburg · part of my Technology Management MBA · my own study notes, rewritten in my words for revision.*

---

This module is the **second stop** in the innovation track, and it changes the question. The first module was about finding an opportunity worth pursuing. This one takes an idea that already exists and asks whether it survives contact with reality: **is there a market, do customers actually want this, can anyone else do it better, and can we build it and get paid for it?** Every team carries its own venture project through the whole semester, so each framework below arrives as a tool to be used rather than as theory to be memorised.

The module opens on a deliberately uncomfortable statistic: most new businesses fail, and they fail mostly for **market and customer reasons** rather than because the technology proved impossible. Everything afterwards is an answer to that. We built and stress-tested business models, wrote personas and sized markets, ran customer interviews and journey maps, classified features with the Kano model, decomposed preferences with conjoint analysis, translated customer wishes into engineering targets with the House of Quality, mapped competitors, prototyped and pretotyped cheaply to kill bad assumptions early, and finally worked out revenue models, pricing and the engine of growth.

I rewrote everything in my own words as revision notes. Every chapter ends with a key-terms table, a short self-test with answers, and a one-minute recap. Most carry a worked example or a case from class so the ideas stay attached to something concrete.

<Aside type="tip">
**The one idea to carry through.** A business proposal is not an argument, it is a **pile of evidence**. Every chapter here is a different way of replacing an opinion with something you actually observed: a customer said it, a number showed it, a test proved it. The teams that struggle are the ones still defending the idea they walked in with; the ones that succeed are the ones who let the evidence change it.
</Aside>
