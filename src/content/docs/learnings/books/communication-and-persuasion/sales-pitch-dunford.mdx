## The big idea

Most sales pitches fail because they're a **feature-dump built around the vendor's product** instead of a story built around the customer's decision. The buyer doesn't need a catalogue - they need help making a *confident* choice among confusing options. So reframe the pitch as a guided story: here's the problem, here are the honest ways to solve it, here's why our distinct approach wins, and here's the value only we deliver. When you help the customer decide, differentiated value does the selling.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="The real competitor" icon="information">
    The biggest competitor is usually **"no decision."** Customers fear picking wrong, so the job is to *lower uncertainty* - make the path to yes feel safe, not just impressive.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Insight before pitch" icon="open-book">
    Open by teaching. Acknowledge the **different approaches** to the problem - status quo, competitors, and yours - and where each breaks down. Naming the landscape honestly earns credibility.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Two-part structure" icon="approve-check">
    Part one is **Insight**: set up the problem and the map of solution options. Part two is the **Perfect World**: your distinct approach and the value only you provide. Problem first, product second.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Differentiated value" icon="magnifier">
    Anchor everything on what alternatives *don't* do. A feature only matters when it ties to a value the buyer can't get elsewhere - that's the reason to choose you.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Equip the champion" icon="rocket">
    Deals are often closed by an internal champion selling on your behalf. A good pitch hands them a **clear, repeatable story** so they can win the room when you're not there.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Name the real enemy.** Assume the default is *no decision*. Ask what makes this choice feel risky, and design the pitch to remove that risk.

2. **Map the approaches honestly.** List the ways a buyer could solve this - doing nothing, competitors, your way - and where the old ways fall short. Say it out loud; don't hide the alternatives.

3. **Lead with insight, not product.** Open with the reframe that changes how the buyer sees the problem, *before* you introduce what you sell.

4. **Anchor on differentiated value.** For each strength, state what only you do and the outcome it creates - cut anything a competitor could claim too.

5. **Arm the champion.** Package the story so someone inside the account can retell it and build a confident case for yes.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *A pitch is a story about the buyer's problem and their options - not a tour of your product.*

> *Name the alternatives honestly; the guide who maps the terrain earns the trust to recommend a path.*

> *Features are only worth mentioning when they're tied to value no one else can deliver.*

> *Your best salesperson is often the champion inside the account - write the pitch so they can retell it.*
