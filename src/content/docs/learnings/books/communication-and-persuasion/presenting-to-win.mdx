## The big idea

The goal of any presentation is **persuasion, not information**. You are trying to move the audience from their current position (Point A) to the action you want (Point B) - and people move only when they see what's in it for *them*. So the whole craft flips: instead of parading your features, capabilities, and data, you speak from the audience's point of view, connect every message to their benefit, and carry them along a story that logically links one idea to the next. Get the thinking and the structure right first; the visuals are just a delivery system for a message the presenter owns.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Define the audience and Point B.** Write down who they are, and what you want them to think, feel, or do by the end. That endpoint governs everything.

2. **State the WIIFY for every point.** For each message, name the benefit to *them*. If you can't, cut the point or reframe it.

3. **Brainstorm ideas, then pick a flow.** Get the raw ideas out first, then choose one clear narrative structure - chronological, problem/solution, numerical - before touching slides.

4. **Build slides last, and keep them thin.** Less is more: minimal text, illustrative graphics, the presenter carrying the words.

5. **Rehearse aloud.** Verbalize the whole talk so the linkages and transitions actually work in the room.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *The goal is not to inform but to persuade - to move the audience from A to B.*

> *"What's In It For You?" - answer it from the audience's point of view, always.*

> *Ideas first, structure second, slides last.*

> *Less is more: the slide supports the speaker, never replaces them.*
