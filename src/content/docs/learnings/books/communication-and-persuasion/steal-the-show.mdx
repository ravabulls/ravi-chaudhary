## The big idea

Every high-stakes professional moment - the pitch, the negotiation, the board briefing - is a performance, and success comes from intentionally choosing the version of yourself that best serves the moment. The trap is believing that any deliberate performance is dishonest, so "being yourself" quietly becomes an excuse for being unprepared. Port reframes performance as an act of service, not ego: you play the part well so your ideas actually reach the people who need to act on them.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Escape the Authenticity Trap" icon="warning">
    "Just be yourself" is the most expensive advice in professional life. Performing your expertise well amplifies your authenticity - it doesn't betray it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Play the right role" icon="star">
    Act *as if* you already have the qualities the moment demands. Behaving like the confident, senior version of yourself produces the actual state - the room reads the signal before you speak.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Raise the stakes" icon="rocket">
    Technical framing keeps an audience at arm's length. Make the outcome feel consequential - not abstractly important - and people lean in.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Structure in threes" icon="list-format">
    The mind holds about three points. Compress everything into three before the room ever sees it; a fourth erases the other three.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use silence" icon="puzzle">
    A pause after a big statement lets it land and signals that you are in control, not rushed. Silence is an instrument, not an absence.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Name your objective first.** Before any prep, answer "What do I want this room to do when I walk out?" If you can't say it in one sentence, you're not ready.

2. **Over-prepare the first three minutes.** The opening is the one moment attention is unconditional - rehearse the entry until it's automatic, and you own everything after it.

3. **Choose an active verb.** Don't aim to *inform*; aim to *challenge*, *unsettle*, or *compel*. The verb you pick shapes the performance you deliver.

4. **Rehearse out loud and record it.** The gap between how you sound in your head and in the room is always larger than you expect. Aim for conversation, not recitation.

5. **Answer hostility with "Yes, and."** Accept the challenge fully, then build on it. It neutralises defensiveness and demonstrates control.

</Steps>
