## The big idea

Job-hunting rewards systems, not heroics. The same discipline that turns strangers into referrals in The 2-Hour Job Search also closes the offer - once you stop reinventing each application and start working from **reusable templates and frameworks**. Every stage of the close, from resume to negotiation, is a repeatable process you build once and reuse, so your effort flows into the quality of your evidence rather than into staring at a blank page.

The shift Dalton pushes is from performing to preparing. A resume is not an autobiography; it is a screening document whose single purpose is to get you into the interview. A cover letter is not a formal essay; it is a short, human note that invites a conversation. An interview is not a memory test of scripted answers; it is a chance to tell a few specific, true stories. And a negotiation is not a fight; it is an information exchange between two parties who both want the deal to work.

Underneath it all is time. Dalton's method is unapologetically efficient: build the assets once, keep them, and let each new opportunity start from most of the way there. That frees you to spend your scarce energy on the parts that actually persuade - concrete results, genuine curiosity, and a handful of stories you can tell without notes.

## The heart of it

The close moves through four stages, each building on the last.

<Steps>

1. **Resume - lead with accomplishments.** Rewrite every line so it opens with an outcome and the impact behind it, not the duty you were assigned. Keep the document concise and evidence-driven; its job is to earn the interview, nothing more. If a bullet does not help you get in the room, it does not belong.

2. **Outreach and cover letter - short and human.** Skip the long formal letter. A brief, curious, genuinely human note that invites a conversation beats a wall of credentials every time, and it respects the reader's time as much as your own.

3. **Interview stories - build a small reusable library.** Rather than scripting responses to endless hypotheticals, prepare a compact set of strong stories in situation, action, result form. Because behavioural interviews reward specific, story-based evidence, a few well-chosen stories can be adapted to answer many different questions.

4. **Negotiation - collaborate from information.** Get the offer in hand first, then assume there is room and ask for it calmly. Use collaborative, respectful tactics grounded in what you know rather than ultimatums, and keep working to be both liked and respected.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
The through-line is reuse. Resume formats, message drafts, and story outlines are all assets you build once and carry from one opportunity to the next - so each new application starts at most of the way done instead of zero.
</Aside>

## How the book unfolds

The book is organised as the companion and sequel to The 2-Hour Job Search: where the first stops at the interview invitation, this one picks up and walks through everything needed to convert that invitation into a signed offer. It moves roughly in the order a candidate experiences the close - resume, then outreach and cover letters, then interviewing, then negotiation - and treats each as a system with its own repeatable technique. Throughout, the tone is practical and time-conscious, favouring frameworks you can apply the same afternoon over theory you admire and never use.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Rewrite every bullet as a result.** Open each line with the outcome and its impact, then strip the duty-language. If a line does not help earn the interview, delete it.

2. **Keep outreach short and human.** Send a brief, curious note that invites a reply instead of a formal cover letter no one finishes. Aim for a conversation, not a credential dump.

3. **Bank a handful of stories.** Write out a few strong situation, action, result stories once, then map each to the kinds of questions it can answer. Refine them until you can tell them without notes.

4. **Keep making champions.** Carry the informational-interview habit forward and keep turning new contacts into people who will advocate for you.

5. **Get the offer before you negotiate.** Secure it in hand first, then assume there is room and ask calmly, from information rather than emotion.

6. **Negotiate to be liked and respected.** Use collaborative, respectful moves rather than ultimatums, so you protect the relationship you are about to join.

7. **Template everything reusable.** Save resume formats, message drafts, and story outlines so your next application starts at most of the way done instead of zero.

</Steps>

## Worth knowing

Read this as the second half of a pair. The 2-Hour Job Search is about getting noticed - a systematic route from a target list through networking to referrals and interview invitations; The Job Closer assumes that groundwork is done and focuses entirely on conversion. You do not strictly need the first book to use this one, but the shared philosophy - efficiency, templates, and turning contacts into champions - lands hardest when you see both halves as one continuous system. <Badge text="Companion to The 2-Hour Job Search" variant="note" />
