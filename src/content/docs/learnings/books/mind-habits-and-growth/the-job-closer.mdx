## The big idea

Job-hunting rewards systems, not heroics. The same efficiency that turns networking into referrals also closes the offer - if you stop reinventing each application and instead work from **reusable templates and frameworks**. Every stage (résumé, cover letter, interview, negotiation) is a repeatable process you build once and reuse, so effort goes into quality of evidence rather than starting from a blank page each time.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Résumés earn the interview" icon="document">
    Frame bullets around **accomplishments and impact**, not responsibilities. Keep it tight and evidence-driven - the document's whole purpose is to get you in the room, nothing more.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Short, human outreach" icon="email">
    Cover letters and messages should be **brief and curiosity-driven**, not long formal essays. A short human note that invites a conversation beats a wall of credentials.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Structured storytelling" icon="pencil">
    Behavioral interviews reward **specific, story-based evidence**. Build a compact library of situation → action → result stories and adapt them, rather than memorizing answers to endless hypothetical questions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Turn contacts into champions" icon="heart">
    Informational-interview habits carry all the way through. Keep converting the people you meet into **advocates** who speak for you when you're not in the room.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Liked and respected" icon="seti:default">
    In negotiation, be **collaborative, not combative**. Calm, information-based requests preserve the relationship; ultimatums win a point and lose goodwill.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Rewrite every bullet as a result.** Start with the outcome and impact; cut duty-language. If a line doesn't earn the interview, drop it.

2. **Keep outreach short and human.** Send a brief, curious note that invites a reply - not a formal cover letter nobody finishes reading.

3. **Bank a handful of stories.** Write out a few strong situation → action → result stories once, then map them to the questions you're likely to face.

4. **Get the offer before you negotiate.** Secure it in hand first, then ask calmly and from information, assuming there's room.

5. **Template everything reusable.** Save résumé formats, message drafts, and story outlines so the next application starts from 80%, not zero.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *A résumé exists to win the interview, not to summarize your career.*

> *Impact and results persuade; lists of duties don't.*

> *Prepare a few great stories, not answers to every possible question.*

> *Get the offer in hand first - you can't negotiate what you haven't been given.*

> *Aim to be liked and respected; negotiate from information, not ultimatums.*
