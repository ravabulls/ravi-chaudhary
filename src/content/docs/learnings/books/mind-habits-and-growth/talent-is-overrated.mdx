## The big idea

World-class performance is not a genetic lottery - it is the product of a specific, uncomfortable, high-intensity activity called **deliberate practice**. The trap most professionals fall into is mistaking experience for expertise: repetition without a structured feedback loop builds habitual autopilot, not skill. The famous 10,000 hours is a floor, not a formula - the *type* of hours matters infinitely more than the count. Excellence is available to anyone willing to trade the comfort of what they already do well for the friction of drilling what they don't.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Audit your autopilot hours.** Look honestly at your day and ask how much of it is genuinely difficult and demands full concentration. That fraction is your real practice time - everything else is repetition.

2. **Hunt for negative feedback.** We drift toward clean outcomes. Deliberately seek the case that didn't resolve, the model that misfired, the argument that lost - the gap is where the lesson lives.

3. **Deconstruct the masters.** Take work you consider excellent and rebuild it from scratch yourself, then compare. The delta shows exactly what your mental model is missing.

4. **Break skills into micro-skills and drill them alone.** You don't practise "leadership" - you practise delivering hard feedback to a resistant person. Isolate the atomic unit before you drill it.

5. **Keep a case-study library.** Log the decisions you made and the outcomes that followed, then review them as training material to sharpen your pattern recognition - not as records.

</Steps>
