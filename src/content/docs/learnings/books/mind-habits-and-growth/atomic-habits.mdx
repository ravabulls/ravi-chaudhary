<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1 · 1% compounds" icon="rocket">

  </Card>
  <Card title="2 · Systems over goals" icon="setting">

## The big idea

Remarkable results don't come from one heroic push; they come from small habits repeated until they compound. The trouble is timing: improvement stays flat for a long stretch - the **Valley of Disappointment** - while potential quietly accumulates behind the **Plateau of Latent Potential**. Most people quit in that valley, right before the breakthrough. The escape is to stop obsessing over outcomes and instead build a reliable **system**, then let it change your **identity** - because habits that stick are the ones tied to who you believe you are.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Outcomes → Processes → Identity" icon="list-format">
    Three layers of change. Most people start with outcomes (what they want). Lasting change starts with **identity** (who they wish to become) and works outward.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The habit loop" icon="seti:clock">
    Every habit runs on four beats: **cue → craving → response → reward**. Understand the loop and you can engineer - or dismantle - any behaviour.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The Four Laws" icon="open-book">
    To build a good habit: make it **Obvious, Attractive, Easy, Satisfying**. To break a bad one, invert each: **invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Environment beats willpower" icon="magnifier">
    You don't need more discipline - you need a better **environment**. Make good cues visible and bad cues invisible, and behaviour follows almost automatically.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The two-minute rule" icon="star">
    Scale any new habit down to a version that takes **two minutes** to start. "Read before bed" becomes "read one page." Master showing up first; optimise later.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The Goldilocks Rule" icon="heart">
    Motivation peaks at **just manageable** difficulty - not too easy, not too hard. Stay on the edge of your current ability to keep the habit engaging.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Cast the identity vote.** Name the person you want to become, then ask: "What would a *reader* / *runner* / *healthy person* do?" Let identity pick the habit.

2. **Write an implementation intention.** Fill in one sentence: *"I will [behaviour] at [time] in [location]."* A specific plan beats a vague intention.

3. **Stack it on an anchor.** Use habit stacking - *"After [current habit], I will [new habit]."* Bolt the new routine onto something you already do daily.

4. **Design the environment.** Put the good cue in plain sight and the bad cue out of reach. Make the right choice the obvious one.

5. **Start with two minutes, and never miss twice.** Shrink the habit so starting is effortless, track it, and if you slip once, get back on the very next day.

</Steps>
