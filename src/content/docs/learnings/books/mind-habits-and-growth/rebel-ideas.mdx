**Book:** Rebel Ideas: The Power of Diverse Thinking
**Author:** Matthew Syed
**In one line:** On complex problems, how *differently* a group thinks matters more than how *smart* each member is - cognitive diversity is a hidden engine of collective intelligence.

---

## Three things to remember

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1 · Cognitive diversity beats raw brilliance" icon="puzzle">
    For complex problems, a group's range of **perspectives, experiences and thinking styles** covers more of the problem than any set of similar-minded stars. Clever clones share the same blind spots.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2 · We cluster with our own kind" icon="approve-check">
    **Mirroring** and homophily make us hire and befriend people like us. It feels comfortable and it is dangerous - a homogeneous team is confident *and* collectively blind in the same directions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3 · Aim at diverse thinking, not box-ticking" icon="star">
    Demographic diversity helps mostly because it *often* brings cognitive diversity. The real target is a wider **problem space**, not a headcount that looks varied on paper.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## The big idea

Intelligence has been measured as an individual trait - the lone genius. But most real problems are too large for one mind to see whole. What decides the outcome is **collective intelligence**: whether the group, between them, covers the full "problem space." A team of brilliant people who all think alike leaves the same regions unexplored, so their errors *correlate* - they are wrong together, and confidently. Cognitive diversity is the counterweight, and it is a **performance tool**, not a moral nicety.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Covering the problem space" icon="magnifier">
    Differences in perspective, information and heuristics let a group reach parts of a problem no single expert can. Homogeneous teams have shared blind spots that no amount of individual IQ closes.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The CIA blind spot" icon="warning">
    A largely similar-background workforce collectively **underweighted the pre-9/11 Bin Laden threat** - not from lack of talent, but from missing the perspectives that would have made the danger legible.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dominance vs prestige" icon="seti:default">
    **Dominant** leaders suppress the sharing of dispersed knowledge. Under **prestige** (respect-based) leadership and psychological safety, people surface what they alone know - and the group actually gets smarter.
  </Card>
  <Card title="When crowds are wise" icon="list-format">
    The wisdom of crowds holds *only* when errors are **independent** and views are diverse. Echo chambers and social conformity make errors line up - and the crowd turns foolish.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ideas having sex" icon="random">
    Innovation comes from **recombination** - fusing distant fields. Outsiders and "give and take" bring the odd angle that insiders can't see, and breakthroughs sit at the seams.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Audit for cognitive clones.** Before staffing a hard problem, ask who thinks *differently* here - not just who is senior or credentialled.

2. **Recruit for the missing angle.** Add the perspective the group lacks (an outsider, a different discipline), not another version of the strongest voice.

3. **Lead by prestige, not dominance.** Speak last, invite dissent explicitly, and make it safe to surface awkward information.

4. **Protect independence.** Gather views *before* people anchor on each other - collect estimates privately so you keep real diversity, not conformity.

5. **Recombine on purpose.** Deliberately collide ideas from unrelated fields to spark the recombinations that homogeneous thinking never reaches.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *A team of clever people who think alike will share the same blind spots.*
