## The big idea

Every workplace runs on rules no one writes down - how to ask for help, when to speak up, what "done" really means. Insiders with connected families absorb these expectations at the dinner table; **first-generation and early-career professionals** are left to guess, then blamed for guessing wrong. Ng's move is to make the invisible explicit: careers aren't a pure meritocracy where good work speaks for itself, so you must **learn the tacit rules deliberately** and **manage how your competence, commitment and compatibility are perceived**.

## The key ideas

  <Card title="Tacit knowledge is learnable" icon="open-book">

  </Card>
  <Card title="The Three Cs, managed" icon="information">

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Decode the real priority.** Before starting, ask what success looks like and why it matters - surface the true ask hidden under the stated task.

2. **Audit your Three Cs.** For any project, ask: am I signalling competence, commitment and compatibility? Fix the weakest one deliberately.

3. **Observe before you act.** In a new team, watch how people communicate and take initiative for a beat, then mirror the norms you see.

4. **Ask for help the right way.** Bring your attempt and a specific question, not a blank "how do I do this" - it signals ownership, not helplessness.

5. **Build sponsors, not just contacts.** Cultivate a few relationships with people willing to advocate for you, and navigate politics with integrity.

</Steps>
