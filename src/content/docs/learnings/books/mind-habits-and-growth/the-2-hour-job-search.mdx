## The big idea

The online application is a **black hole**. Spraying resumes at job postings almost never works because applicant-tracking software screens you out before a human ever reads you, and even if you slip through, no one inside is vouching for you. Dalton's data-informed claim is blunt: the odds on blind applications are so poor that the activity mostly generates the feeling of progress rather than progress itself.

So he reframes the whole search as a *systematic, prioritised process* aimed at a single outcome - getting an internal advocate to carry your resume past the filters. The point is not to work harder but to change the mechanism: referred candidates are dramatically more likely to land interviews, so every step is engineered to manufacture referrals.

What makes the book distinctive is that it is a concrete, tech-savvy methodology rather than pep talk. You pick and rank targets (LAMP), contact the right three people at each in the right way (3B7), and run curiosity-driven conversations that earn advocacy (TIARA). The disciplined **setup** - list plus first outreach - takes roughly **two hours**; the follow-up then runs as a lightweight numbers-and-tracking game.

## The heart of it

<Steps>

1. **Build the LAMP list (about 40 employers).** Brainstorm target companies fast - quantity first, judgement later - so you have a wide field to rank rather than one or two fixations. The list is deliberately long because prioritising a big pool is the whole advantage.

2. **Prioritise with LAMP, then work it top-down.** Score each employer on Affection (how genuinely excited you are), Motivation (whether they are actively hiring or growing), and Posting (is there a relevant job open right now), and sort so your strongest fits rise to the top. You then work the ranked list in order instead of tackling everything at once - genuine excitement is what sustains the effort, so Affection carries real weight.

3. **Reach out with 3B7.** At each target, contact **three** people - a Booster, a Beneficiary, and a Boss - with a short, respectful message. If a person does not reply, **follow up once after 7 business days**; if still nothing, move on without taking it personally. Three contacts widen your odds that at least one becomes responsive.

4. **Run TIARA informational interviews.** When someone agrees to talk, do *not* ask for a job. Ask curiosity- and advice-based questions about Trends, Insights, Advice, Resources, and Assignments. These let the contact be generous and expert, which builds rapport - and a warmed-up contact is the one who later offers, unprompted, to refer you.

5. **Track, follow up, and let advocates carry you.** Treat the whole thing as a repeatable system: log who you contacted and when, honour the 7-business-day follow-up, and keep the ranked list moving. Your job is to generate advocates; their referrals do the work of getting your resume past the software.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip" title="The three acronyms, spelled out">
  **LAMP** = **L**ist (your roughly 40 target employers) · **A**ffection (how excited you are about each) · **M**otivation (are they hiring or growing) · **P**osting (is a relevant job actually open). You rank on A, M, and P.

  **3B7** = contact **3** people at each target - a **B**ooster (a helpful, well-connected peer), a **B**eneficiary (someone whose team would gain from your skills), and a **B**oss (a potential hiring manager) - then **follow up after 7** business days.

  **TIARA** = the informational-interview questions - **T**rends · **I**nsights · **A**dvice · **R**esources · **A**ssignments. Advice-based, never job-begging.
</Aside>

## How the book unfolds

Dalton opens by diagnosing why the conventional search fails - the black hole of online applications and the outsized power of referrals - to justify the whole approach. He then builds the method tool by tool: first the **LAMP list** as the prioritisation engine, then **3B7** for who to contact and how to follow up, then **TIARA** for what to actually say in an informational interview. Later chapters cover turning warm conversations into referrals and interviews, and running the process as a trackable system with realistic timelines. Throughout, the emphasis is practical and tech-savvy - LinkedIn, alumni databases, and simple spreadsheets - so the reader finishes with a repeatable playbook rather than motivation alone.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Block two focused hours.** Treat the setup as one bounded session so it gets started, not deferred.

2. **Brainstorm about 40 employers.** Go for quantity first; a wide pool is what makes ranking valuable.

3. **Score and rank with LAMP.** Rate Affection, Motivation, and Posting, then sort so your strongest fits sit at the top - and commit to working them in that order.

4. **Find three contacts per target.** Use LinkedIn and alumni tools to identify a Booster, a Beneficiary, and a Boss at each top employer.

5. **Send short outreach, then wait seven.** Keep each message brief and respectful; if no reply, follow up once after 7 business days, then move on.

6. **Hold TIARA conversations.** Ask advice-based questions about Trends, Insights, Advice, Resources, and Assignments - and let the contact be the one to offer a referral.

7. **Track everything and keep moving.** Log contacts and follow-up dates, work the list top-down, and let your growing set of advocates carry your resume forward.

</Steps>

## Worth knowing

The method is built for people who hate networking. Dalton's whole design lowers the emotional cost: short messages, advice rather than asks, a capped follow-up, and permission to move on when someone does not respond - so introverts and career-switchers can run it without pretending to be extroverts. It pairs naturally with a tidy LinkedIn profile and a spreadsheet or simple CRM, and it assumes you will get many non-replies, which is why the numbers-and-systems framing matters more than any single conversation. <Badge text="Best paired with a clean LinkedIn + a tracking sheet" variant="tip" />
