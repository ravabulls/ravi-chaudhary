## The big idea

Most self-improvement fails because it waits — for the perfect moment, enough motivation, a finished plan. Foroux's short essays keep returning to one bias: **act today**. Procrastination isn't a character flaw or a scheduling problem; it's the mind avoiding the discomfort of hard work. The cure is not more analysis but a small, immediate step. Pair that bias toward action with a clear sense of what's *meaningful* to you, and you stop confusing motion with progress.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Do one hard thing first.** Each morning, take a single small action on the task you most want to avoid — before checking any feeds.

2. **Sort by meaning, not urgency.** List today's work and mark what genuinely moves your goals. Do that first; let the rest wait.

3. **Guard your attention.** Remove one recurring distraction and set a block of focused time where creation beats consumption.

4. **Build a repeatable routine.** Pick one habit you can show up for daily, and rely on the routine instead of motivation.

5. **Ship before it's perfect.** Set a "good enough" bar, finish, and move on — done and improving beats unstarted and ideal.

</Steps>
