## The big idea

Our "always-on" culture treats overwork as a virtue and rest as weakness - but the evidence and the history point the other way. Many of the most creative and productive people who ever lived - Darwin, Dickens, leading mathematicians and scientists - did only about **four to five focused hours** of real work a day, and built the rest of the day around walks, naps, and absorbing hobbies. Rest wasn't what they did *after* the work; it was part of *how* the work got done. Treated as active and deliberate, rest recharges energy, consolidates learning, and lets the mind solve problems you've stopped consciously pushing on.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Protect four focused hours.** Do your most demanding work in the morning, undistracted, and don't judge the day by hours logged.

2. **Stop while it's still flowing.** End a session with clear momentum so tomorrow's restart is easy and the problem keeps working in the background.

3. **Walk daily and nap deliberately.** Treat a walk or a short nap as part of the work, not a guilty break - that's where insight shows up.

4. **Take deep play seriously.** Pick one absorbing, demanding hobby and give it real time; passive scrolling won't recharge you the same way.

5. **Guard sleep and take real breaks.** Protect sleep for consolidation, detach fully on time off, and use sabbaticals to sustain a long career.

</Steps>
