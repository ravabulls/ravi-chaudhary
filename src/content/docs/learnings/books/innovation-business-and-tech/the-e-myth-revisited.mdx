## Three things to remember

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1 · The E-Myth" icon="warning">

  <Card title="2 · Work ON it, not just IN it" icon="setting">

## The big idea

Most small businesses are started by a **technician** — someone who was good at the work and assumed that was enough. But doing the work and owning the company are completely different jobs. To escape, you stop being the hero who does everything and instead **design a business that runs on systems**, so it can grow, be handed off, or run without you in the room.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Three people inside every owner" icon="puzzle">
    The **Entrepreneur** (vision, the future), the **Manager** (order, planning), and the **Technician** (the doer, the present). Every owner has all three — trouble comes when the Technician dominates and crowds out the other two.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The entrepreneurial seizure" icon="seti:notebook">
    The moment a skilled worker thinks *"why work for someone else?"* and starts a business. The fatal assumption: knowing the technical work means understanding the business that does it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The franchise prototype" icon="approve-check">
    Build the business as if you will replicate it a thousand times. If every step is documented and repeatable, the prototype becomes a model that works predictably in anyone's hands.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The Turn-Key Revolution" icon="open-book">
    The franchise model's real product is not the burger — it is the **system**. Ordinary people, following a proven system, can deliver consistent results every time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Business Development Program" icon="list-format">
    A structured way to work *on* the business: your **Primary Aim** and **Strategic Objective**, then Organisational, Management, People, Marketing, and Systems strategies working together.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Name your Primary Aim first.** Decide what you want your *life* to look like, then make the business serve that — not the other way round.

2. **Separate the three roles.** Notice when you are stuck as the Technician, and deliberately book time as Entrepreneur and Manager.

3. **Pick one repeated task and systemise it.** Write it down step by step so someone else could do it to your standard.

4. **Build the prototype mindset.** Ask of every process: *would this still work if I cloned this business a hundred times?*

5. **Schedule time to work ON the business.** Protect regular hours for designing the company, not just running inside it.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *The system runs the business, and the people run the system.*

> *Work on your business, not just in it.*

> *Most people who go into business are technicians who had an entrepreneurial seizure.*

> *The business is not your life — it is meant to serve your life.*

> *Your model should run predictably in the hands of ordinary people, not only your own.*
