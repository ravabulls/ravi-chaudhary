**Book:** How Big Things Get Done: The Surprising Factors Behind Every Successful Project, from Home Renovations to Space Exploration
**Author:** Bent Flyvbjerg & Dan Gardner
**In one line:** Big projects fail by default — the cure is to *think slow, act fast*: plan long and hard on paper, then build in one quick, uninterrupted window.

## Three things to remember

<CardGrid>

## The big idea

Almost all big projects obey the **Iron Law of megaprojects: over budget, over time, under benefits — over and over.** Drawing on a database of over 16,000 projects, Flyvbjerg shows this is not bad luck but a predictable result of rushing to commit and dig. The fix is to invert the usual order: make planning slow, thorough, and cheap to fail in, so that delivery can be fast and boring. A long, careful plan is what buys you a short, safe build.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Planning is a safe place to fail" icon="pencil">
    On paper, mistakes cost almost nothing. Experiment, model, and iterate until the plan is proven — failing cheaply beats failing in concrete.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Start with what and why" icon="information">
    Lock the *purpose* before the *form*. The "what and why" is the box you must think outside of; beware the **commitment fallacy** and premature lock-in.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Optimism and lying inflate plans" icon="warning">
    Estimates are wrecked by **optimism bias** and by **strategic misrepresentation** — people low-ball costs to get a project approved. The outside view counters both.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Modularity wins" icon="seti:default">
    Solar farms, wind farms, and server racks scale from small repeated units and ride a learning curve. Nuclear plants and Olympics are one-off giants — and blow up.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Your biggest risk is you" icon="approve-check">
    A great, experienced team matters, but the deepest hazard is your own behavioural bias. Design the process to check the planner, not just the plan.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Fix the what and why first.** Write the real purpose in one line before touching design, budget, or schedule.

2. **Plan slow — fail on paper.** Model, prototype, and iterate cheaply until the plan is boringly reliable.

3. **Forecast from the outside.** Find similar past projects and anchor your cost and time estimates on how *they* actually ended.

4. **Break it into modules.** Design the work as small repeatable pieces so you can learn and speed up as you go.

5. **Then act fast.** Deliver in one tight, uninterrupted window to starve disruption of time.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *Think slow, act fast.*
