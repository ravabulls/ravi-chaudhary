## The big idea

The future is not random and it is not unknowable — it announces itself early, in **weak signals** at the fringe of the present. Most people miss those signals because they only look at the mainstream, where a trend is already obvious (and already priced in). Webb's argument is that futurism is a **teachable discipline**: you deliberately go to the edges, look for patterns, and reason forward with structure — rather than reacting to headlines or trusting a lone "expert" prediction. The payoff is not certainty but **preparedness**: seeing the plausible futures early enough to act.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Go to the fringe" icon="magnifier">
    Early signals live in **unusual experiments and edge communities** — the "unusual suspects" working at the margins. Scan there first; the mainstream is a lagging indicator.
  </Card>
  <Card title="CIPHER: spot the patterns" icon="list-format">
    Look for **C**ontradictions, **I**nflections, **P**ractices, **H**acks, **E**xtremes, and **R**arities. These are the fingerprints that a fringe signal is turning into something real.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ask if it's a real trend" icon="question">
    Interrogate the signal with the right questions: does it serve a **basic human need**, is it **timed**, does it **persist** and link to other signals? If not, it's a fad.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Calculate the timing" icon="seti:clock">
    A trend without an **ETA** is useless. Estimate when it crosses into the mainstream so you act neither too early nor too late.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Think in scenarios" icon="random">
    Don't bet on a single prediction. Build **probable, plausible, and possible** futures, then pressure-test them against evidence and consequences.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Beware your own biases" icon="warning">
    **Optimism, recency, and the craving for certainty** distort forecasts. Naming the bias is the first defence against fooling yourself.
  </Card>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Find the fringe.** Deliberately scan the edges of your field — experiments, subcultures, unusual practitioners — not just the trade press everyone reads.

2. **Run CIPHER.** Sift what you find for Contradictions, Inflections, Practices, Hacks, Extremes, and Rarities to surface the patterns that matter.

3. **Test it as a trend.** Ask whether the signal meets a basic human need, is timed, and has staying power — kill it if it's just a fad.

4. **Estimate the ETA.** Calculate roughly *when* the trend reaches the mainstream so the insight becomes actionable.

5. **Build and pressure-test scenarios.** Sketch probable/plausible/possible futures, then decide what you'll do about each.

</Steps>
