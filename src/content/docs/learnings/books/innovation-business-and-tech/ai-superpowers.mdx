## The big idea

AI has crossed from an "age of discovery" - where a few elite researchers chased breakthroughs - into an "age of implementation," where ordinary engineers apply proven deep-learning techniques to floods of data. That shift changes who wins: raw research advantage matters less than data, capital, and relentless execution. China, strong on data, entrepreneurs, and government backing, is now a genuine peer to Silicon Valley. But the deeper story is the coming shock to jobs and inequality - and Lee's own cancer diagnosis, which convinced him the answer is human, not technical.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Four drivers of AI power" icon="setting">
    AI leadership rests on four things: abundant **data**, hungry **entrepreneurs**, skilled **scientists and engineers**, and supportive **policy**. China leads on three of the four; the US still leads on elite research talent.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Gladiatorial founders" icon="seti:crystal">
    China's copy-then-innovate startup arena (WeChat, Alipay, the O2O boom) forged brutally fast, resilient entrepreneurs. Cloning was the entry fee; survival demanded real innovation on top.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The four waves of AI" icon="list-format">
    AI arrives in waves: **internet AI** (recommendations), **business AI** (data-rich decisions), **perception AI** (sensors blending online and offline), and **autonomous AI** (self-driving, robotics). Each reaches deeper into the physical world.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The great decoupling" icon="warning">
    The real risk isn't robot overlords - it's jobs. AI automates routine cognitive and physical tasks, threatening a large share of work and widening inequality between those who own the systems and those displaced by them.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What AI can't do" icon="approve-check">
    Machines optimise; they don't love. Lee proposes leaning into human strengths - care work, teaching, service - and a **social investment stipend** that pays people to do compassionate, community, and creative work.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Audit your work for routine.** List the parts of your job that are repetitive analysis or prediction - those are most exposed to automation, so start shifting your time elsewhere.

2. **Invest in the human edge.** Double down on skills AI is weak at: judgement, relationships, empathy, cross-domain creativity, and leading people.

3. **Get close to data.** Whatever you build, design it so real usage generates data that makes the next version better - that loop compounds.

4. **Pair AI with a human.** For high-stakes decisions, use AI for the analysis and a person for the care and accountability, rather than replacing one with the other.

5. **Watch all four waves.** When judging an AI opportunity, ask which wave it rides - and remember perception and autonomous AI move slower but reshape the physical economy hardest.

</Steps>
