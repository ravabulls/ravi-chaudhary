## The big idea

Traditional IT service management is reactive and labour-intensive — humans chase alerts, triage tickets, and rediscover the same fixes. Yet the same operations already emit enormous volumes of data. Applied well, **analytics, machine learning, automation, and natural language** can reshape the whole delivery lifecycle into a **data-driven, knowledge-based** system that anticipates problems, resolves the routine automatically, and hands experts the context to solve the rest. This is the **AIOps** thesis: better business outcomes come from insight and automation, not more effort.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Start from good data.** Get logs, tickets, monitoring, and events clean and accessible first — insight is only as good as the data under it.

2. **Pick one lifecycle pain.** Target a concrete task: anomaly detection, incident triage, or a common remediation worth automating.

3. **Keep humans in the loop.** Let AI recommend and augment; keep expert judgement on the critical path while trust is earned.

4. **Measure the business outcome.** Track resolution time, deflection, and reliability — not model accuracy in isolation.

5. **Iterate.** Capture what works as reusable knowledge and expand from there.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *Operational data is an asset — the value is in turning it into insight and action.*

> *Move IT service management from reactive and labour-intensive to proactive, automated, and insight-driven.*

> *Automate the routine; augment the human for everything that needs judgement.*

> *Capture knowledge so a solved problem stays solved.*
