## The big idea

School trains you to collect credentials and wait to be picked. The real economy runs on the opposite logic: you build something of value, then make it visible through deliberate communication, personal branding, and relationships. "Build, don't talk" is a priority order, not a ban on talking - first there must be something real, and then the skill of getting it seen is what multiplies it. The highest-leverage skills of a career, Shamani argues, are exactly the ones no classroom teaches - and they are learnable, not innate.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Ship something visible before you feel ready.** Pick one real piece of work and publish it where the right people can see it. Waiting for "ready" is just talking with extra steps.

2. **Define your one-line brand.** Decide what you want your name to mean, then make your public output reinforce that single theme instead of scattering across unrelated topics.

3. **Give value before you ask for anything.** For each person or community you want in your network, offer one genuinely useful thing first - an insight, an introduction, real help.

4. **Practise making the complex simple.** Rewrite one piece of your work so a smart outsider understands it in a minute. Repeat until simplifying without dumbing down is a reflex.

5. **Treat money as a skill to learn.** Deliberately study how income, assets, and investing work, the same way you would learn any professional tool. Start small, start early.

</Steps>
