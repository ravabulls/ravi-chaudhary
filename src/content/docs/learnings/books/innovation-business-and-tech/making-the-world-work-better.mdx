## The big idea

Written for IBM's 2011 centennial, the book is three linked essays on how one company lasted a hundred years and helped shape the century around it. The thread running through all three: enduring institutions combine **bold science**, **disciplined management**, **strong values**, and a **willingness to remake themselves**. Progress didn't come from a single genius product but from a repeatable posture - invest ahead of need, manage for the long term, and treat reinvention as the normal condition of survival rather than an emergency measure.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Pioneering science" icon="open-book">
    From tabulating machines to mainframes to "Watson," IBM kept pushing the technical frontier. The habit of funding hard research early is what let it lead successive eras instead of chasing them.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reinvention as survival" icon="random">
    Punch cards gave way to the System/360; the near-fatal crisis of the early 1990s gave way to Lou Gerstner's pivot into services, software and consulting. Transformation was recurring, not one-off.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Management as a discipline" icon="list-format">
    Professional management and long-term thinking - not heroics - held the company together. Structure, patience and R&D investment compounded across decades.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Values over profit alone" icon="heart">
    Standing for something beyond the next quarter - its people, its research, its role in the world - gave IBM a reason to endure that a pure profit motive could not.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A smarter planet" icon="puzzle">
    The world's systems - traffic, energy, healthcare, supply chains - can be made instrumented, interconnected and intelligent. Technology's next act is optimising the systems people already live inside.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Budget for the frontier.** Fund research and hard bets ahead of demand - the lead you build in a downturn is what you cash in during the next era.

2. **Schedule your own reinvention.** Don't wait for a crisis to remake the business; assume today's cash cow is tomorrow's legacy and start the next act early.

3. **Manage for decades, not quarters.** Treat professional management and long-term thinking as a competitive moat, not overhead.

4. **Anchor to values people can name.** Stand for something beyond profit so the organisation has a reason to hold together through its worst years.

5. **Make existing systems smarter.** Look for where instrumenting and connecting what already exists - logistics, energy, care - creates more value than a brand-new product.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *Enduring companies don't cling to what made them great - they are willing to give it up to stay great.*

> *THINK was never a slogan; it was an instruction to make research and disciplined thought the company's real product.*

> *A crisis is proof that the last reinvention has run its course, not that reinvention has failed.*

> *The next frontier is not a smarter machine but a smarter planet - the systems we already depend on, made instrumented, interconnected and intelligent.*
