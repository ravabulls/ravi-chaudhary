## The big idea

Modern wind turbines are dense sensor platforms, and a wind farm streams enormous volumes of **spatio-temporal** data - wind speed, direction, power output, and turbine condition over time and across a landscape. Raw, that data is noisy and hard to act on. The book's argument is that **statistical and data-science methods are the bridge** from measurement to decision: the same analytical toolbox, applied carefully, produces better wind forecasts, honest performance benchmarks, sound reliability estimates, and smarter maintenance plans. It is example-driven throughout - real industry datasets, case studies, and code rather than theory in isolation.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Frame the decision first.** Name the choice you're improving - forecast, benchmark, or repair - before reaching for a model.

2. **Respect the space-and-time structure.** Treat wind data as spatio-temporal; don't flatten it into independent samples.

3. **Fit the power curve to benchmark honestly.** Use kernel regression or splines to quantify performance against a fair baseline.

4. **Match the method to the question.** Pull the right tool - time series, trees, kNN, Bayesian inference - from the shared toolbox.

5. **Close the loop on maintenance.** Feed reliability estimates into when-and-what-to-repair decisions across the farm.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *A power curve is a performance verdict: fit it well and you can say, fairly, how good a turbine really is.*

> *Reliability and maintenance are two sides of one question - anticipate failure so you repair on purpose, not in panic.*

> *The same statistical toolbox serves the whole lifecycle; what changes is the decision it informs.*

> *Methods matter only where they sharpen a real choice - a forecast, a benchmark, a repair.*
