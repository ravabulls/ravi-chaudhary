## Three things to remember

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1 · Platform beats pipeline" icon="random">
    A pipeline creates value in a linear chain - design, make, sell. A platform creates value by *facilitating exchanges* between users. The platform doesn't own the value; it hosts the interaction.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2 · Network effects, not scale" icon="rocket">
    Value grows as more users join. That feedback loop - not economies of scale - is what makes a platform pull ahead and hard to catch.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3 · Nail the core interaction" icon="approve-check">
    Every platform lives or dies on one repeated exchange: a *participant*, a *value unit*, and a *filter* that matches them. Make that one interaction easy and everything else follows.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## The big idea

For a century the strongest firms were pipelines: they controlled a linear value chain and grew by getting bigger and more efficient. Platforms change the shape of value creation - they build a market where outside producers and consumers meet, and they get *stronger* the more people use them. Because the users create the value, the platform can grow faster and lighter than any firm that has to make everything itself. Compete on the health of the network, not the size of the factory.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Pipeline vs platform" icon="random">
    Pipelines push a product down a chain. Platforms orchestrate exchanges between producers and consumers, so growth comes from *enabling* others, not from owning more production.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Network effects" icon="rocket">
    Same-side and cross-side, positive and negative. More consumers attract more producers, which attracts more consumers. Manage the negative effects too - spam and clutter can repel the very users you gained.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Enable, then curate" icon="setting">
    The platform's job is to reduce friction so the core interaction happens, then curate quality so bad matches don't drive good users away. Openness and governance are dials, not switches.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Chicken-and-egg / cold start" icon="seti:crystal">
    No producers without consumers, and the reverse. Reach critical mass by seeding value, attracting *marquee* users, or building one side first before opening the other.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Threats to the moat" icon="warning">
    Disintermediation (users take the deal off-platform), multihoming (users split across rivals), and envelopment (a neighbouring platform absorbs yours) all erode network advantage.
  </Card>
  <Card title="New metrics & money" icon="list-format">
    Count interaction and engagement, not just headcount. Monetize the value you create - transaction fees, access fees, enhanced access, or charging for curation.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Define your one core interaction.** Name the participant, the value unit, and the filter that matches them - before designing anything else.

2. **Pick which side to seed first.** Solve the cold-start problem deliberately: seed value, recruit a marquee user, or build one side standalone, then open the other.

3. **Turn the network-effect flywheel.** Every design choice should make the next user more valuable to the existing ones, not just add a head to the count.

4. **Curate against the negatives.** Filter spam and bad matches early - one bad interaction can undo many good ones.

5. **Measure interactions, then monetize them.** Track engagement and match quality, and charge where you add the most value without taxing the growth loop.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *Platforms create value by facilitating exchanges - they don't make the product, they make the market.*

> *Network effects, not economies of scale, are the engine of the platform economy.*

> *Get the core interaction right and the rest of the design falls into place.*

> *The hardest problem is the first user: no one wants to join a market with no one in it.*

> *A platform can invert the firm - pushing value creation from the inside out to the crowd beyond its walls.*
