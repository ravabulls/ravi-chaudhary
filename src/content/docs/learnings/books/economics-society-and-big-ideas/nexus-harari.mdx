<CardGrid>

## The big idea

Harari retells human history as the story of **information networks**. The naive hope that "more information leads to truth and wisdom" is wrong — so is the cynical view that "information is only a weapon for power." Real networks survive by balancing **truth-seeking** against **order-keeping**, and by binding people together through shared stories. AI breaks the pattern: for the first time a non-human agent can create stories, forge intimacy, and run bureaucracies at scale. The choice of how to design and constrain it is **ours, now** — and it will decide whether these networks strengthen or destroy human freedom.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Two idealised views, both wrong" icon="warning">
    The **naive** view (information → truth → wisdom) and the **populist** view (information is only power) each miss the point. Networks must hold truth and order *together*.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Myth binds, bureaucracy organises" icon="document">
    Networks always mix two mechanisms: **mythology/stories** that bind people, and **bureaucracy/records** that organise them. Both are needed; neither is truth.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Self-correction is the safeguard" icon="approve-check">
    Courts, science, a free press, elections — **self-correcting mechanisms** are what keep a network from catastrophic error. Their absence is the real danger.
  </Card>
  <Card title="AI at the controls" icon="rocket">
    AI can **create and spread stories, forge fake intimacy, and run opaque bureaucracies** at scale — undermining the transparent, self-correcting conversation democracy depends on, and handing new tools to totalitarian control.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Separate information from truth.** When you receive information, ask what it is *doing* — connecting, organising, persuading — not just whether it's "accurate."

2. **Look for the two mechanisms.** In any institution, name the binding **stories** and the organising **records**. Seeing both reveals how it really runs.

3. **Check the self-correction.** Ask: how does this system catch its own mistakes? A network without courts, review, or dissent is one bad idea from disaster.

4. **Treat AI as an agent.** When AI acts, ask who it can persuade, what it can decide, and who audits it — evaluate it as a *member* of the network, not a neutral tool.

5. **Design the constraints now.** Push for transparency and human oversight while the choices are still open; the guardrails are hardest to add after the fact.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *Information's oldest job is to connect people, not to reveal the truth to them.*

> *A network that cannot correct itself will scale its errors as fast as its successes.*

> *Stories bind us and records organise us — but neither one is reality.*

> *AI is the first tool that can also be an author, an actor, and a decision-maker.*

> *Democracy is a conversation, and a conversation needs to stay transparent to stay honest.*
