## Three things to remember

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1 · We're systematically wrong" icon="warning">
    On basic facts - child mortality, poverty, vaccination - people (experts included) score **worse than random guessing**. We carry an outdated, overly dramatic picture of the world.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2 · Four Levels, not two worlds" icon="bars">
    Drop "developed vs developing" and "us vs them." Think **four income Levels (1-4)**. Most of humanity now lives in the **middle**, not in extreme poverty.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3 · Instincts, then rules of thumb" icon="setting">
    Ten **dramatic instincts** distort our thinking. Each has a **rule of thumb** that calms it. Factfulness is the habit of applying them.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## The big idea

Our worldview isn't just a bit out of date - it's wrong in a **consistent direction**: too dramatic, too negative, too divided. That's not stupidity or bad news reaching us; it's the product of **instincts** that once helped us survive but now mislead. **Factfulness** is the calm habit of holding opinions only when you have solid data to back them - and of staying **curious and humble** when you don't. It's not blind optimism: things can be **bad and getting better at the same time**.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Gap & Size" icon="magnifier">
    **Gap instinct:** stories split the world into extremes - find the **majority in the middle**. **Size instinct:** a lone big number always looks alarming - get it in **proportion**: compare it, and **divide** (rates, per-capita).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Negativity & Straight Line" icon="line-chart">
    **Negativity instinct:** bad news reaches you more easily, so decline feels like the default - expect it, and hold "bad" and "better" together. **Straight Line instinct:** don't assume a trend continues in a straight line - many curves bend.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Fear & Urgency" icon="warning">
    **Fear instinct:** frightening is not the same as dangerous - assess the **real risk** (risk = danger × exposure). **Urgency instinct:** "now or never" clouds judgement - resist it, ask for data, take **small steps**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Generalisation, Blame & Destiny & Single view" icon="puzzle">
    **Generalisation:** question your categories. **Blame:** look for **causes and systems**, not villains. **Destiny:** slow change is still change - cultures and nations move. **Single Perspective:** no one tool explains everything - use **several views**.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Before reacting, ask "compared to what?"** A number alone means little - find a baseline, a trend, or a rate before forming an opinion.

2. **Look for the middle.** When a story offers two extremes, ask where **most** people or cases actually sit.

3. **Separate scary from dangerous.** Feeling afraid? Estimate real exposure and probability before acting.

4. **Distrust "now or never."** Urgency is often a sales tactic. Slow down, gather data, take a reversible small step.

5. **Update on purpose.** Treat your worldview as a draft - expect it to be a bit wrong, and welcome facts that revise it.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *Forming your worldview by relying only on the media is like forming your view of a person from a photo of their foot.*

> *Things can be bad and getting better at the same time.*

> *Factfulness: the calm habit of holding only opinions for which you have strong supporting data.*

> *Beware "now or never" - it almost never is. Insist on the numbers and take the next small step.*

> *When you hear "the majority," ask: is it 51% or 99%? The word hides everything that matters.*
