**Author:** Yuval Noah Harari
**In one line:** We conquered the planet not because we're strong or clever alone, but because we can *invent shared fictions* — money, nations, religions — that let millions of strangers cooperate.

## Three things to remember

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1 · Fiction is our superpower" icon="star">
    The Cognitive Revolution (~70,000 years ago) gave us language that can describe things that **don't physically exist**. Believing shared stories together is what lets huge numbers of strangers cooperate flexibly.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2 · The glue is imagined" icon="heart">
    Money, nations, laws, human rights, corporations — none exist in nature. They're **inter-subjective fictions** that work only while we collectively believe them. That belief *is* civilisation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3 · Progress ≠ better lives" icon="warning">

## The big idea

Homo sapiens was one unremarkable ape among several human species. What set us apart was not bigger muscles or lone genius but a peculiar mental trick: the ability to **create and believe stories about things that aren't real** — gods, money, tribes, companies. Facts let a hundred people who know each other cooperate; *shared fiction* lets millions of strangers who will never meet act as one. That is the engine behind every empire, market, and religion. Harari's history is the story of these imagined orders getting larger, merging, and eventually pointing us toward one global society — and now toward redesigning life itself.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="The Cognitive Revolution" icon="open-book">
    ~70,000 years ago our language gained the power of **fiction**. Not just "there's a lion by the river," but "the lion is the guardian spirit of our tribe." Shared myths unlocked cooperation at scale.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Imagined orders" icon="information">
    Money, nations, human rights, and corporations are real **only because we agree they are**. They're not lies — they're inter-subjective realities, the strongest and most flexible glue humans have ever had.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Agriculture's trap" icon="seti:crystal">
    ~12,000 years ago farming let more people survive on less land — but on worse diets, longer labour, and new diseases. "We didn't domesticate wheat; **wheat domesticated us**."
  </Card>
  <Card title="The unification of humankind" icon="random">
    Money, empire, and universal religions each dissolved local boundaries, slowly merging fragmented cultures toward a **single interconnected world**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The Scientific Revolution" icon="magnifier">
    ~500 years ago progress began with **admitting ignorance** — "we don't know." Married to empire and capitalism, that admission drove an unprecedented explosion of power.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Power without happiness" icon="warning">
    We are far more powerful than our ancestors but not obviously happier. Along the way we drove mass extinctions — and we are now starting to **redesign life itself**.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Ask "who agreed to believe this?"** When something feels permanent — a currency, a border, a job title — remember it holds only because enough people share the story. Fictions can be rewritten.

2. **Separate objective, subjective, and inter-subjective.** A river is objective; my mood is subjective; money is inter-subjective. Knowing which one you're dealing with clarifies what it takes to change it.

3. **Distrust "progress" as automatic good.** More output or growth for the whole often hides a worse deal for the individual. Ask *who actually benefits* before calling a change progress.

4. **Start from admitted ignorance.** The scientific mindset that reshaped the world began with "we don't know." Treat gaps in your knowledge as the starting point, not an embarrassment.

5. **Judge power by well-being, not scale.** Bigger, faster, and more capable is not the same as better off. Keep asking whether the humans (and animals) inside the system are actually flourishing.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *"We didn't domesticate wheat. Wheat domesticated us."*

> *Fiction has enabled us not merely to imagine things, but to do so collectively — and to cooperate in ways no other animal can.*

> *Money is the most universal and efficient system of mutual trust ever devised — trust in a shared fiction.*

> *The great discovery that launched the Scientific Revolution was the discovery that humans do not know the answers to their most important questions.*

> *We are more powerful than ever before, but we have very little idea what to do with all that power — and we are not noticeably happier for it.*
