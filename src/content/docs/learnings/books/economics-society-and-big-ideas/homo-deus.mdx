## The big idea

This is the forward-looking sequel to *Sapiens*. Harari argues that the projects that defined human history - surviving famine, plague and war - are increasingly *solvable* problems, so ambition turns upward: toward engineering longer life, engineered happiness, and god-like powers over life itself. But the same technologies that promise the upgrade also **undercut the belief system we built the modern world on** - that the individual human, with their feelings and free choices, is the measure of all things. Read it as **provocative forecasting, not prophecy**: a set of questions about where our current trajectory points, built on the story *Sapiens* told about how we got here.

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Separate the two axes.** When judging any AI system, ask *how intelligent* and *how conscious* - they are different questions, and conflating them causes bad predictions.

2. **Watch what a system optimises for.** Notice when a process values **smoother data flow** over an individual's interests - that is Dataism in miniature.

3. **Audit your algorithmic self-knowledge.** Track where recommendation engines already predict your choices; treat that as data on how much authority you've outsourced.

4. **Read forecasts as questions.** Turn each claim into "what would have to be true?" - Harari offers scenarios to steer away from, not fates to accept.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping

> *In this century, humanity's next projects are likely immortality, bliss, and divinity.*

> *Intelligence is decoupling from consciousness - a system can be brilliant without being aware.*

> *Dataism judges everything by whether it improves the flow and processing of information.*

> *The real danger is not machines that hate us, but machines that know us better than we know ourselves.*

> *These are not predictions - they are possibilities, offered so we can choose differently.*
