## The big idea

Orwell's 1945 novella - subtitled *A Fairy Story* - is an allegory of the Russian Revolution and its betrayal under Stalin. Inspired by old Major's dream, the animals expel Mr Jones and found **"Animalism,"** with Seven Commandments promising that all animals are equal. But **Napoleon** (a Stalin figure) drives out his rival **Snowball** (a Trotsky figure) using dogs raised as secret police, and the pigs steadily convert shared victory into private privilege. The horror is not a sudden coup but a slow, deniable slide - each betrayal explained away until the ideal is unrecognisable.

## The key ideas

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Animalism and the Seven Commandments" icon="document">
    The revolution codifies equality in rules painted on the barn wall. Written ideals feel permanent - which is exactly why altering them unnoticed is so powerful.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Napoleon vs Snowball" icon="seti:notebook">
    A power struggle decided not by argument but by force. Napoleon's dogs make dissent dangerous, and Snowball becomes the scapegoat blamed for every failure.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Squealer and the rewriting of history" icon="pencil">
    The propagandist reinterprets each broken promise as loyalty, and quietly edits the commandments until only one remains - twisted to justify the pigs' rule.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Boxer, the loyal worker" icon="heart">
    The carthorse answers every crisis with "I will work harder." When he collapses, worn out and useless, he is sold to the knacker - loyalty repaid with betrayal.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How to use it

<Steps>

1. **Watch what changes on the wall.** When the rules of an institution get quietly reworded, ask who benefits - the edit, not the announcement, is where power moves.

2. **Distrust the single scapegoat.** When every failure is blamed on one absent enemy, someone is using that enemy to avoid accountability.

3. **Notice private force behind public unity.** Loyal "guards" who answer only to the leader are how a movement's ideals get captured.

4. **Protect the record.** Tyranny needs a malleable past. Keep independent memory of what was actually promised and done.

5. **Question "work harder" as the only answer.** Boundless loyalty without questions is exactly the trait the powerful most love to exploit.

</Steps>

## Lines worth keeping
