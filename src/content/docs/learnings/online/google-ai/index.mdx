*Google AI Professional Certificate (Google on Coursera) - study notes for revision.*

---

Eight short courses, about thirteen hours of material, and no coding prerequisite. The certificate's claim is narrow and honest: it does not teach you to train models or to write software, it teaches you to put AI into the work you already do, and then - in the last two courses - to build small tools for yourself without writing code.

The framing that runs through all eight courses comes from the first video: AI is a **collaborator**, not a vending machine. You bring the context, the judgment and the accountability; it brings speed, breadth and a tireless willingness to produce a first draft. Every course is built as demo first, practice second, with more than twenty hands-on labs where you repeat the demo on your own material.

<Aside type="note">
**Where this sits in my notes.** This is separate from the [Google Project Management Certificate](/learnings/online/project-management/) and from the shorter [Job Search with AI](/learnings/online/job-search-with-ai/) course, both of which live elsewhere in these online-learning pages. There is some deliberate overlap: the project management capstone has a chapter on AI for project managers, and the job search course uses the same Gemini tools for a different purpose.
</Aside>

## Every tool the certificate touches

| Tool | What it is | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gemini app** | The all-purpose assistant and the course's home base, at gemini.google.com or on mobile | All eight courses |
| **Gemini Live** | Real-time spoken conversation, in the mobile app. Used for roleplay and for talking through notes | Writing and communicating |
| **Canvas** | A document and code workspace inside Gemini for drafting and iterating on long output | Writing, content, data analysis |
| **Deep Research** | An agentic mode that opens and reads hundreds of sources in a reasoning loop, then writes a report | Research and insights |
| **Gemini Notebook** | Source-grounded research and synthesis, with audio, video and interactive overviews. Formerly NotebookLM | Brainstorming, research |
| **Nano Banana Pro** | The image generation and editing model inside the Gemini app | Fundamentals, content creation |
| **Veo** | Video generation, including image-to-video and native audio | Content creation |
| **Gemini in Sheets** | Analysis, cleaning and formula help in plain language inside a spreadsheet | Data analysis |
| **Google AI Studio** | The developer-facing surface: a playground with model settings, and Build mode for vibe coding | Fundamentals, app building, deployment |
| **SynthID** | Invisible watermarking that marks content as AI-generated | Responsible AI |

<Aside type="caution">
**Model names in these notes date fast.** The course demos run on **Gemini 3.5 Flash** and **Gemini 3.1 Pro**. By the time I wrote these notes the current Flash model was **Gemini 3.8 Flash** and Pro had left the free tier. Where a version number matters I have given both: what the recording shows, and what is current. Treat every number as a reading from one day, not a constant - the [Gemini API chapter](/learnings/online/google-ai/11-gemini-api/) says where to look up today's.
</Aside>

## How these notes handle the labs

More than twenty of the certificate's items are hands-on labs and activities: you watch a short tutorial, then redo it on your own material in a split-screen workspace. Those are the most useful part of the certificate and the least recoverable part afterwards - there is no transcript of a lab you did yourself, and a concept explained half in a video and half in an activity arrives in notes with a hole in the middle.

So these notes close the holes, and say where they did.

<Aside type="tip">
**Why bother separating them.** Because the two have different shelf lives and different authority. The course's own framing - collaborate rather than command, check before you use, tell people when you used it - will outlast every model number in these pages. My additions are accurate as of writing and will need rechecking. Keeping them visibly apart means a reread in a year can trust one half and verify the other.
</Aside>
