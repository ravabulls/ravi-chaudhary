This is the whole vocabulary of Course 6, the capstone, collected as study notes for revision and grouped alphabetically so a term is quick to find. It is meant for the few minutes before a quiz, or for the moment a term turns up in one of the Sauce and Spoon activities and I want the short version without going back through the videos.

<Aside type="tip">
Read the letter blocks straight through once, then come back for the parentheticals. They mark the clusters the quizzes like to mix up, such as the three-point estimates and their two formulas, quality assurance against quality control, and the closeout report against the impact report.
</Aside>

## A

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Accountability** | Owning the decisions attached to a project or a task, and answering for how they turn out. |
| **Alignment** | The point at which two or more parties actually agree on something, rather than merely having heard each other out. |
| **Artifact** | Any document a project produces and keeps, such as a charter, a project plan or a closeout report. |

## B

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Benchmarking** | Judging how well something did by measuring it against an agreed standard or a comparable case. |
| **Benefits** | What the organisation expects to gain from the project. (benefits are the expected upside; costs are what is spent to get there, and a business case weighs the two) |
| **Beta distribution (PERT)** | A three-point estimating formula that gives the most likely estimate extra weight, on the reasoning that it is the outcome most likely to happen. (weights the middle value heavily; triangular treats all three equally) |
| **Budget** | The amount of money set aside to get the project finished. |

## C

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Coalition** | A short-lived alliance between people or groups formed to pursue a shared aim or run a joint activity together. |
| **Confidence level rating** | A stated measure of how much trust you place in an estimate being accurate, useful for flagging the shaky numbers in a plan. |
| **Conger's four steps** | Jay A. Conger's sequence for persuading someone to take a new idea seriously: earn credibility, frame the idea around shared ground, back it with evidence, and connect with the person emotionally. |
| **Constructive criticism** | Feedback given respectfully and with the aim of making the work better, not of scoring a point. |
| **Costs** | Everything the project consumes, from money spent on tasks to the value of the time, labour and resources it uses up. (costs are the outlay; benefits are the expected return) |

## D

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Deliverables** | The concrete outputs and tangible results that, once produced, get the team to its project goals. |
| **Dependency** | A link between two tasks where one cannot start or finish until the other starts or finishes. |
| **Domain knowledge** | Familiarity with a particular industry, subject or line of work, as opposed to general project management skill. |

## E

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Effort estimate** | How much working time a task itself will actually take. (effort is the hands-on time; a total duration estimate adds everything else around it) |
| **Empathy** | Being able to grasp and share what another person is feeling. |
| **Evaluation** | Watching a project, measuring what it produced, and comparing that against criteria everyone agreed on beforehand. |
| **Evaluation indicator** | The precise piece of data you will collect in order to answer an evaluation question. (the question is what you want to know; the indicator is the measurement that answers it) |
| **Evaluation presentation** | A presentation that reports how the project is performing when measured against a set of standards. |
| **Evaluation question** | A central question about a project's outcomes, impact or effectiveness that the evaluation is designed to answer. (broad and about the project; indicators are the specific data points chosen to answer it) |
| **Executive summary** | A short opening block, a few sentences up to a paragraph, that gives a busy reader the purpose and the outcome of a longer report. |

## G

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Good product hygiene** | The habit of pulling every link and document into a single place so there is a clean record for anyone who needs the project later. |

## I

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Impact report** | A short deck or slideshow that shows senior stakeholders the value the project delivered. (the impact report sells the outcome upward to executives; the closeout report is written by a project manager for project managers) |
| **Influence** | How much power a stakeholder holds, and how far their actions can move the project's outcome. (power is what they can do to the project; interest is how much the project affects them) |
| **Interest** | How much a stakeholder's own needs are touched by how the project runs and how it ends. (interest is the stake they hold; influence or power is the leverage they have) |

## K

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Key accomplishments** | The part of an executive summary that names the activities, tasks and milestones that made the project work. |

## L

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Lessons learned** | The part of an executive summary that sets out what could have gone better and how the next project will be run differently because of it. |

## M

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Micromanaging** | Watching, controlling or reminding your team about their assigned work so closely that it gets in the way of them doing it. |
| **Milestones** | Significant checkpoints in the schedule that show how far the project has got. (a milestone marks a point in time and takes no effort itself; tasks are the work that gets you to it) |
| **Most likely estimate** | The time a task normally takes, assuming the usual minor problems crop up but nothing extraordinary. (the middle of the three-point estimates) |
| **Multiple choice question** | A closed question that offers a fixed set of answers to pick from. (a short fixed list; a scaled question asks for a rating along a range, and an open-ended one asks for the respondent's own words) |
| **Mutual benefit** | A situation in which every party involved comes away with some gain or advantage. |
| **Mutually beneficial agreement** | A deal struck so that everyone signing it gains something, which is the aim of a good negotiation. |

## O

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective criteria** | Neutral reference points for settling a disagreement, such as market prices, research results, documented precedent, or law and regulation. |
| **Objectives and key results (OKRs)** | A goal paired with the metrics that will show whether it was reached, so ambition and measurement sit in the same statement. |
| **One-pager** | A single page giving stakeholders a quick overview of the project. |
| **Open-ended question** | A question that cannot be answered in a word, inviting the respondent to reply in their own language. (free text; multiple choice and scaled questions are both closed) |
| **Optimistic estimate** | The best-case time for a task, assuming nothing goes wrong. (the shortest of the three-point estimates) |
| **Out-of-scope** | Work deliberately left out of the project plan because it does not serve the project goal. (out-of-scope work is agreed as excluded; in-scope work is what the plan commits to deliver, and blurring the line is how scope creep starts) |
| **Overhead** | Running costs of the business that cannot be tied directly to making the product or delivering the service. |

## P

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Pessimistic estimate** | The worst-case time for a task, assuming the problems really do happen. (the longest of the three-point estimates) |
| **Power-interest grid** | A chart that sorts stakeholders by how much power they hold and how much interest they have, so you can see who to prioritise and how to approach each one in a negotiation. |
| **Project charter** | The formal document that defines the project and records the detail needed to reach its goals, signed off before the work starts. |
| **Project closeout report** | The wrap-up document a project manager writes for other project managers, future project managers, and anyone who wants to understand the project and find its artifacts. (peer to peer and detailed; the impact report is a short pitch of value to senior stakeholders) |
| **Project goals** | The results the project is set up to achieve. |
| **Project plan** | The documentation that pins down scope, tasks, milestones, budget and the rest of the activity, so the project can be kept on track. |
| **Project summary** | A brief overview of the project that states what it was trying to achieve. |
| **Project tasks** | The individual pieces of work that have to be completed inside a set timeframe. (tasks are the effort; milestones are the markers they add up to) |
| **Project vision** | The part of an executive summary that names the need the project exists to meet. |

## Q

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Quality assurance** | The review process that checks whether the project is on course to deliver a high-quality product or service, shortened to QA. (QA is preventive and looks at the process; QC inspects the output once a problem shows up) |
| **Quality control** | The techniques used to hold quality standards once an issue has been spotted, shortened to QC. (QC reacts to defects in the output; QA works on the process that produces them) |
| **Quality management plan** | The document that captures everything needed to manage quality across the project life cycle: the policies, the processes, the criteria, and who is responsible for each. |
| **Quality planning** | The process a project manager or team follows to work out which standards of quality actually matter for the project and how they will be met. (planning is the activity of deciding; standards are the specific bar that comes out of it) |
| **Quality standards** | The requirements and specifications a product or service has to hit before the organisation and the customer will call it a success. (the bar itself; quality planning is the process of setting and satisfying it) |

## R

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Retrospective** | A meeting or workshop where the team stops to reflect on how a project went and what to carry forward. |

## S

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Scaled question** | A closed question with more than two options that asks the respondent to rate their answer along a scale. (a rating along a range; multiple choice offers discrete options, open-ended asks for free text) |
| **Scope** | Where the project starts and stops. (what falls inside is in-scope; anything else is out-of-scope) |
| **Slide-based presentation** | A run of digital slides used to put information across visually. |
| **SMART action item** | A single task made specific, measurable, attainable, relevant and time-bound. (a task-level commitment; SMART goals apply the same test to the project's goals) |
| **SMART goals** | Project goals written to be specific, measurable, attainable, relevant and time-bound. (goal-level; a SMART action item is one task written the same way) |
| **Stakeholder analysis** | A visual mapping of everyone with a stake in the project, used to see the whole field at once. |
| **Stakeholder management** | The ongoing work of keeping good relationships with the people whose influence most affects your work. |
| **Storytelling** | Turning raw facts into a narrative so an audience takes the point and remembers it. |
| **Summary sheet** | A one or two page write-up carrying only the most relevant information, like a snapshot of the findings. |
| **Survey** | An instrument for evaluating and measuring the quality of a project's process, goal or deliverable by asking people. |
| **Survey question** | A question written to gather the data that will answer an evaluation question. |
| **Synthesizing** | Pulling information from several sources together and using the combined picture to build an analysis. |

## T

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Three-point estimating** | A technique for arriving at a realistic time estimate by combining an optimistic, a most likely and a pessimistic figure. (the three inputs; triangular and beta or PERT are the two formulas for combining them) |
| **Time estimation** | A forecast of how much time a task will need in total. |
| **Total duration estimate** | The effort estimate plus everything wrapped around it, such as approvals, preparation and testing. (the calendar reality; the effort estimate counts only hands-on working time) |
| **Triangular distribution** | A three-point estimating formula that weights all three estimates equally, so the most likely case pulls no harder on the result than the optimistic or pessimistic one. (equal weights; beta or PERT leans on the most likely value) |
