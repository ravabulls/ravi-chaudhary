## The problem: time-critical information, scattered everywhere

Litigation is where small slips become expensive. A counter affidavit not filed before a hearing, or an appeal period allowed to lapse, can decide a case before its merits are ever argued. Yet the information needed to prevent that was spread across commissionerates, forums and files.

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Where do we stand?" icon="magnifier">
    How many cases are pending, in which forum, on which subject and in which commissionerate? How long have they been pending?
  </Card>
  <Card title="What is coming up?" icon="list-format">
    Which hearings are this week, and has the counter or reply been filed for each of them?
  </Card>
  <Card title="How are we doing?" icon="approve-check">
    How many cases were disposed, and how many in the department's favour? Was every adverse order appealed in time, or a decision recorded not to appeal?
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

| Why it was hard | |
|---|---|
| **Scattered records** | Every commissionerate kept its own register, in its own layout |
| **Many forums** | Each forum has its own case types, stages and timelines |
| **Time-critical** | Hearing dates and limitation periods for appeals leave no room for late information |
| **Repeated compilation** | Every review meeting or request from senior authorities started a fresh round of calls and spreadsheets |
| **No common view** | There was no way to see which unit was behind on counters, appeals or updates |

<Steps>

1. **One template for every commissionerate.** I designed a single Excel template with fixed columns and dropdowns for forum, stage, outcome and counter status, so every unit recorded its cases the same way and the sheets could be combined without manual rework.

2. **Ownership stays with the unit.** Each commissionerate kept and updated its own sheet, because the unit handling a case knows it best. The dashboard only read the sheets; it never changed them.

3. **One central connection.** Power BI connected to all the sheets through Power Query, appended them into one case table, cleaned dates and names, and flagged records with missing fields or sheets that had not been updated recently.

4. **A model built around the case.** Cases were linked to forums, commissionerates and a date table, with measures for pending and disposed cases, outcomes, appeal decisions, counters due and overdue, and the days left to each hearing and each appeal deadline.

5. **Report pages for each question.** An overview for senior authorities, a hearings-and-deadlines page for what needed action, a side-by-side view of the commissionerates, and a case register that any total could be filtered down to.

6. **Reporting upwards.** As the nodal officer, I used the dashboard to report to senior authorities: figures for review meetings, answers to questions as they came, and periodic reports, all from the same source.

</Steps>

### What each case record captured

| Field group | What it held | What it fed |
|---|---|---|
| **Case identity** | Forum, case type, case reference and commissionerate | The key that ties every update to one case |
| **Subject and stakes** | The issue in dispute and the amount involved | Priorities and the revenue at stake |
| **Stage** | Filed, pending, reserved for orders or disposed | Pending and disposal counts |
| **Counter or reply** | Due date and filing date | Counters due and overdue |
| **Hearings** | Next date of hearing | The hearing calendar |
| **Outcome** | In favour, against, remanded or withdrawn, with the order date | Outcome shares and success rates |
| **Appeal decision** | Appeal filed, accepted or under consideration, with the limitation date | The appeal-deadline watch |

## Design decisions

| Decision | Why | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Excel as the entry point** | Every unit already worked in Excel; new software would have slowed adoption | One shared template, with dropdowns to keep entries consistent |
| **Ownership stays local** | The unit handling a case knows its status best | Each commissionerate maintained its own sheet; the dashboard only read it |
| **One central source** | Different counts from different files caused confusion in reviews | The nodal dashboard became the single source for all litigation reporting |
| **Deadlines first** | A missed hearing or a lapsed appeal period is the costliest failure | Hearings and appeal deadlines on their own page, sorted by urgency |
| **From total to case** | Senior authorities ask about totals first, then about one case | Every number filters down to the underlying case records |
| **Freshness on show** | A dashboard is only as good as its latest update | Each unit's last update displayed beside its figures |

## Value delivered

| | Before | With Sankalan |
|---|---|---|
| **Status of a case** | Calls and letters to the commissionerate handling it | One click from the zone total to the case |
| **Reports to senior authorities** | Compiled afresh from every unit for each request | Ready on the dashboard whenever they were needed |
| **Hearings** | Tracked separately by each unit | One calendar across every forum and unit |
| **Counters and replies** | Chased when a hearing came close | Due and overdue counters visible per unit, in advance |
| **Appeal decisions** | Limitation dates buried in individual files | Orders against the department listed with the days left to appeal |
| **Follow-up** | No view of which unit was behind | Each unit's position, and how fresh its data is, side by side |
