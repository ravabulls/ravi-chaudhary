## Phase by phase, with evidence

| Phase | What I do | Where it happened |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiate** | Define the problem in the users' terms, show what it costs, and get the sponsor's approval before anything is built | Each government project went ahead only with the approval of senior authorities; [Pravah](/projects/pravah/) started with a timed walk through the process |
| **Plan** | Gather requirements by talking to the people who will use it, fix the scope and what is out of scope, plan milestones and a pilot | [GST Nirikshak](/projects/gst-nirikshak/): a written specification and fifteen milestones; [Sankalan](/projects/sankalan/): template fields agreed with the users |
| **Build or set up, and test** | Build or configure in stages, matching the real workflow, and test each stage before the next | [MPR Drishti](/projects/mpr-drishti/): a staged pipeline tested on real reports; GST Nirikshak: 507 automated tests |
| **Pilot** | Start small, watch where people get stuck, fix it before scaling | [eOffice](/projects/eoffice-rollout/): one section first; Pravah: real shifts; Sankalan: the first units; MPR Drishti: a first version rebuilt to run without installation |
| **Roll out and train** | Bring every user group on board, train them hands-on and give them guidance they can keep | eOffice: training at the desk; [Santulan](/projects/santulan/): templates with a fill-in guide; GST Nirikshak: a built-in manual and a demo zone |
| **Monitor and control** | Track adoption and results, follow up where things slip, report progress to the sponsor | Sankalan: flags for missing or stale updates; MPR Drishti: completeness checks; regular reports to senior authorities |
| **Close and hand over** | Hand over a running system, its documentation and its routine, so it survives without me | Every project was handed over to my successor or the team; Santulan was used for actual transfers two years in a row |

## The formal toolkit

In government, my projects ran inside a hierarchy and without formal budgets, so the paperwork was proportionate: an approval from senior authorities, a plan, progress reports and a handover to my successor. The formal project management toolkit I learned and practised separately, and I now use it to put structure on the way I have always worked.

| Where | What I practised |
|---|---|
| [Google Project Management Certificate](/learnings/online/project-management/) | The full life cycle from initiation to closing: project charters, stakeholder analysis, scope, schedules, budgets, risk management, RACI, quality, communication plans, agile and Scrum, and a capstone that pilots a new ordering system |
| [Project RE:CHARGE](/projects/cermedes-recharge/) | A complete team project plan: charter, work breakdown structure, RACI, communication plan, Gantt chart, stakeholder matrix and risk register |
| [Contract & SLA Copilot](/projects/contract-sla-copilot/) | A gated 30/60/90-day rollout with go and no-go criteria, eleven KPIs, stop-and-fix triggers and a kill switch |
| [Agile project management](/learnings/mba/applied-market-and-business-strategy/09-agile-project-management/) | Agile and hybrid delivery, from my MBA course in applied market and business strategy |
| [SAP Certified Implementation Consultant](/resume/certifications/) | End-to-end business processes in SAP S/4HANA, the processes an enterprise system implementation has to support |

## What I bring to a system implementation

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Before go-live" icon="magnifier">
    A stakeholder map, the current process mapped with the people who run it, requirements in their words, a configuration that fits the real workflow, and a pilot with clear criteria for going wider.
  </Card>
  <Card title="At go-live" icon="rocket">
    A training plan for every user group, short user guidance they can keep at hand, and a known first point of contact for questions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="After go-live" icon="setting">
    Adoption and performance tracked against simple KPIs, a log of risks, issues and actions, regular reporting to the sponsor, and improvements fed back into the next release.
  </Card>
  <Card title="At the end" icon="approve-check-circle">
    A clean handover: documentation, a routine that works without me, and the lessons learned written down for the next project.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>
