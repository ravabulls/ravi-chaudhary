## The problem: important decisions, no shared numbers

A tax zone is a hierarchy, with separate Audit (Circles, Groups) and Appeals formations beside it. Each level does different work, including registrations, return scrutiny, refunds, adjudication, audits and appeals. Each year, officers have to be distributed across all of them.

The questions decision-makers could not answer with data:

  <Card title="Is it fair?" icon="list-format">
    Is support (ministerial) staff spread fairly across formations? Which Commissionerates have an unusual balance of work across their own levels?

| Why it was hard | |
|---|---|
| **Silos** | Workload returns sat with each office, staffing in registers, sanctioned strength in a separate cadre file, and geography nowhere at all |
| **Different units** | Each level measures different work, so 100 points at a Range office and 100 points at headquarters are not the same thing |
| **Messy staffing** | Many officers hold additional charge of two or three offices, so headcount alone misstates capacity |
| **Inconsistent keys** | The same office was spelled differently in different files (a city's full name in one, its short form in another), and column order varied from file to file |
| **Low-tech setting** | Offices work in Excel. There was no server, no budget and no IT project, and the data was sensitive and had to stay on the user's machine |
| **Trust** | A black-box score would never be accepted for postings. Every number had to explain itself |

<Aside type="tip" title="The design goal I set">
Turn the debate from *"I feel this office is overstaffed"* into *"this office's officers carry 72% of the zonal average load, and here is the calculation."* The tool should measure workload and never judge the officer. The decision stays with the competent authority.
</Aside>

## The analytical approach: from raw counts to a fair, explainable plan

<Steps>

1. **Weighted workload index.** `Score(office) = Σ (value × weight)` across 108 parameters in 7 levels, grouped into areas such as taxpayers, registration, refunds, scrutiny, adjudication, audit and enforcement. Each weight reflects how much work one unit means.

2. **Capacity in officer-equivalents.** `Strength(office) = Σ (officers × grade weight)`. Grade weights run from 25 for a Commissioner to 4 for an Inspector. An officer holding additional charge of N offices counts as 1/N in each, so capacity reflects reality, not headcount.

3. **Load per officer against the zone.** `Load = Score ÷ Strength`, shown as a percentage of the zonal average (total score ÷ total strength). Every office is shown against 100%: below means lighter than average, above means heavier.

4. **Making levels comparable.** `R(level) = (share of level × department workload) ÷ level score`. Each level scores in its own points. One visible policy input, the **Level Shares** (for example Ranges 50%, Divisions 20%, HQ 30%), converts them into one unit, with the Ranges anchoring the scale at R = 1.

5. **Requirement and gap.** `Required = workload ÷ zonal average load`. The gap between required and actual strength is converted into whole Superintendents and Inspectors, using each office's own grade mix.

6. **Constraint-based allocation.** Four guardrails apply to every suggestion: an office giving officers is never pushed above the zonal average; a receiving office is never filled beyond sanctioned strength, checked grade by grade; no office gives officers it does not actually have; and moves are in whole officers, by grade, paired giver → receiver.

7. **Outlier detection: a peer mix check.** Each Commissionerate's split of work across its own levels is compared with the peer average, and deviations of ±30% or more are flagged for a closer look. Because it compares like with like, the check doesn't depend on the policy shares.

8. **Support-staff fairness.** Ministerial staff (executive assistants, tax assistants, clerks and multi-tasking staff) are compared with each formation's fair share, in proportion to the officers they serve. The tool shows staff per 100 officers, surplus and deficit, and a grade-wise movement plan.

</Steps>

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Templates generated from the model" icon="add-document">
    Each level's Excel template is generated from its current parameter list: one column per parameter, officer columns, office names pre-filled and a "How to fill" sheet. Add a parameter and the next template carries it automatically.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Google Forms, automated" icon="rocket">
    A Google Apps Script embedded in the tool turns any template into a **cascading form**: an office picks its Commissionerate, then sees only that Commissionerate's Divisions, then only that Division's Ranges. Every figure is required and validated, and the help text explains the 1/N rule with examples. A second function removes duplicates (the latest reply per office wins), produces an upload-ready file in the exact format, and lists the offices that have not replied.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Validation before anything loads" icon="approve-check-circle">
    Columns are matched by normalised heading, so column order, curly quotes, dashes and extra spaces don't matter. Missing columns, duplicate rows, negative values and text in number cells are listed in plain words, and nothing is applied until the file is clean.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Governed parameters" icon="setting">
    All levels' parameters and weights can be exported to one sheet, edited and re-imported. The import is validated and only applied after a confirmation summary. A renamed parameter is treated as new, so old figures are never silently relabelled.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Filling gaps responsibly" icon="information">
    Levels without real returns were seeded by **proportional extrapolation** from the Range study, with headquarters staff split between Division offices and HQ without double counting. Every such dataset is labelled until real data replaces it. Embedded data is versioned: upgrades refresh demo levels and never touch uploaded ones.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Skills, results and principles

| Skill | Where it shows |
|---|---|
| **Problem framing** | Turned a postings debate into measurable questions, with separate views for zone-level and Commissionerate-level decision-makers |
| **Data modelling** | A hierarchical model (zone › Commissionerate › Division › Range) across 7 levels, plus cadre and geography |
| **Data integration** | Nine inputs joined through hierarchy keys and normalised office names |
| **Data cleaning & validation** | Heading normalisation and checks for duplicates, negatives and text, with plain-language error reports |
| **KPI & index design** | Weighted workload score, capacity in officer-equivalents, load per officer, % of zonal average |
| **Normalisation across units** | Level Shares conversion ratios with an anchor level |
| **Optimisation under constraints** | Guardrails, sanction caps, whole-officer rounding, giver → receiver pairing |
| **Outlier detection** | A peer mix check that flags ±30% deviations from the peer average |
| **Synthetic data** | Proportional extrapolation to fill missing levels, clearly labelled |
| **Visualisation & dashboards** | Six chart types, drill-downs, a choropleth map and KPI tiles |
| **Data collection & automation** | Generated Excel templates and a Google Forms + Apps Script pipeline |
| **Data storytelling** | Live briefing deck, handbook, present and print modes |
| **Responsible analytics** | Transparent assumptions, the human keeps the decision, privacy by design |

### Value delivered

- **Used for real decisions:** actual transfers were carried out on its analysis two years in a row, leading to a better distribution of officers across offices.
- **Imbalance made visible:** load per officer for every office, side by side.
- **Defensible decisions:** every suggestion shows its reason and full calculation.
- **Faster AGT preparation:** a grade-wise draft plan in minutes, straight from the data.
- **Built to last:** survives reorganisations and works for other zones.
- **Zero cost, fully private:** no vendor, no server, no data leaving the machine.
